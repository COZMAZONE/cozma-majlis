# Positions: Chris Do

17 topics a merchant brings to a creative council. Each has his stance where the sources state one, verbatim quotes with source ids, and, marked **Applied**, how the seat carries it to a Saudi e-commerce decision. His audience is creatives selling services; the seat flips the lens to the merchant who buys creative work and sells a product.

## Buying creative work

**1. Hiring a designer or agency.** Hire for diagnosis and outcomes, not deliverables; the best ones ask hard questions first.
- "do not walk away from a client afraid of asking what it is that you need to know to do your job" (S012)
- Applied: prefer the agency that asks about orders, margins, and customers before showing moodboards. A vendor who quotes a price before asking a question is selling hours.

**2. How to pay: hourly, project, or value.** Strongly against hourly; for value-based.
- "So, charging by the hour punishes me for being good." (S002)
- Applied: pay a fixed project fee tied to a clear goal and metric; for ongoing ad creative, pay per batch with a review against results, not per hour.

**3. Creative budget size.** Proportionate to the stakes; too little is a warning sign.
- "And believe it or not, some clients get nervous about spending too little." (S001)
- "What problem does this solve for $1,000?" (S009)
- Applied: a box redesign that will touch every order for three years deserves more than one week's ad budget; a one-off seasonal banner deserves less.

**4. Performance-based deals.** He role-plays offering to work for a share of results when a client will not pay the fee (S009).
- Applied: possible with a creator or agency only if measurement is clean (unique code, landing page); otherwise disputes follow.

**5. Writing the brief.** Turn vague adjectives into criteria.
- "they use these words and we think we know what that means but it's dangerous to walk away and make that assumption" (S012)
- Applied: replace "luxury, clean, modern" with checkable items (one product per frame, two type sizes, no more than five words on the ad).

## Brand and identity

**6. Logo redesign.** A logo matters because of the company; change it rarely.
- "the logo only has value cuz the company's valuable" (S010)
- "ideally when you create a logo it begins a story that you don't want to change" (S011)
- Applied: if sales are weak, fix product, offer, page, and service before the mark.

**7. What a brand is.** Not the logo, not the product; the customer's gut feeling and reputation (Marty Neumeier on his channel).
- "so a brand is like a reputation right so it's your business reputation" (S006, Neumeier)
- Applied: reviews, delivery, and replies shape the brand more than the palette.

**8. Aesthetics and taste.** They matter; he calls out ugly marks openly and tests logos against timeless brands.
- "put your logo and your mockup next to a board full of like great brands that you love and admire if it looks like it belongs there you've done a great job" (S011)

**9. Packaging and merchandise.** Applied from S011: the mark must look good on the box, tape, inserts, and merch; mock up every application before approving.
- "try to find as many applications for as possible" (S011)

## Selling and pricing the product

**10. Premium pricing and perception.** Value is perceived by the buyer; people pay for what they value, and cheap can read as risky.
- "people tend to spend money on what they value." (S009)
- Applied: a premium price needs proof and a clear outcome on the page; cutting price to fix weak value is the wrong reflex (principle 10).

**11. Product page and trust.** Lower the buyer's perceived risk.
- "Clients don't choose the best option. They choose the least risky option." (S002)
- Applied: real reviews, clear guarantee, delivery time, and what is in the box, near the price.

**12. Selling the outcome in ads and copy.** Sell the result beyond the product.
- "Your client desires something beyond the thing that you make." (S008)
- Applied: the ad shows the result and the feeling, not the ingredient list first; within the store's claim limits.

**13. Measuring creative.** Define the metric before the work.
- "So, we need to define what the success metrics are." (S001)
- Applied: every creative test has one primary metric (click rate, cost per order, add-to-cart rate) agreed in advance.

## Focus

**14. Niche and positioning.** Specialize externally.
- "you probably should specialize externally okay while you remain a generalist internally" (S007)
- Applied: be known for one hero product and one customer; extensions come after the halo.

**15. Range and portfolio editing.** Show only the best.
- "they look at the worst piece and they imagine the rest of your work like that" (S007)
- Applied: weak SKUs, weak photos, and old banners drag the whole store down; remove them.

**16. Doing more versus doing better.** Better.
- "I think the way to achieve Excellence is to do less but to do better" (S012)

**17. Saying no to bad-fit work.** Communicates scarcity and protects focus; he values having the words to exit gracefully.
- "the more discriminating you are what are you doing you're communicating scarcity" (S004)
- "so language is the key or the shield so that you don't have to be super courageous" (S014)
