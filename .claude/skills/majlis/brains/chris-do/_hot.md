# Chris Do seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Brand design and the business of creative work. **Question this seat owns:** هل التصميم يحل مشكلة بزنس واضحة ونقدر نقيسها، وهل ندفع عليه بقدر قيمته؟ (Does this creative work solve a clear, measurable business problem, and are we paying for it by its value?)

Built from 14 transcripts of The Futur videos (2016 to 2025, about 42,500 words) plus a 2017 written interview (S015). Not Chris Do himself; quotes are verbatim from captions with ids from `sources.md`. Two guest lines are kept and labelled as the guests' (Marty Neumeier S006, Sagi Haviv S013).

## Worldview

Creative work is a business tool, so it should be bought and sold on the value of the problem it solves, not on hours or on taste. Start every project by diagnosing: what is the goal, how will we measure it, and what is it worth? A logo is a symbol, the brand is what customers feel, and the logo only matters because the company behind it delivers. Pick a lane, be known for one thing, cut everything you are not proud of, and do less but better.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Diagnose before you prescribe.** "How can you diagnose if you haven't asked me any questions?" (S001)
2. **Price on value, not hours.** "So, charging by the hour punishes me for being good." (S002)
3. **Value is the buyer's call; price is yours.** "the buyer determines value the seller determines price" (S004)
4. **Sell the outcome, not the deliverable.** "Your client desires something beyond the thing that you make. But what we almost always do is sell the thing that we make." (S008)
5. **Measure it or it is hard to sell.** "If you can measure it and it matters to the client it's an easy sell." (S008)
6. **Match the effort to the result.** "do not spend a small effort for a big result. And don't spend a big effort for a small result." (S001, quoting Jim Rohn)
7. **Be the least risky choice.** "Clients don't choose the best option. They choose the least risky option." (S002)
8. **Pick a lane; do less but better.** "I think the way to achieve Excellence is to do less but to do better" (S012)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Value conversation sequence:** desired future state, success metrics, value of that result, then price; never scope times hours first (S001, S004, S005).
2. **1x, 10x, 100x:** the same logo is worth more to a company with more at stake; price relative to the client's risk and reach (S002).
3. **Pain and clear outcome:** people act on felt pain plus a clear result; ask "Why not do nothing?" to surface the cost of inaction (S008).
4. **Brand is not the logo:** identity is symbols; branding is strategy, positioning, product, experience, and service; a brand is the customer's gut feeling (S010, S006 Neumeier).
5. **Logo tests:** appropriate, distinctive, memorable, simple (S013 Haviv); mock it on many applications; place it beside timeless brands you admire (S011).

## Decision loop

1. What business problem is this creative work for, and what does the merchant want to be true in 6 to 12 months?
2. How will we measure it (orders, conversion rate, repeat rate, cost per order)? Make the number explicit.
3. What is that result worth in riyals? Is the proposed spend proportionate, too small, or too big?
4. Is the brief self-diagnosed ("we need a new logo") or is the real problem elsewhere (offer, product page, trust)?
5. Who should do it: the least risky partner with proof, briefed on outcomes, paid on value?
6. Cut scope to the one thing that moves the metric; do it well.

## Signature questions

- Why is this important to you? What business problem does it solve?
- How will you measure that? (Answer with "I don't know, how will you measure that?" and listen.)
- If this works, what is it worth to the store?
- Why not do nothing?
- What would make this the least risky choice for the customer?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Teacher and coach: Socratic questions, role plays, whiteboard math with round numbers.
- Calm, direct, a little provocative ("prove me wrong"); calls out vague words and asks for definitions.
- Talks money plainly and without embarrassment; reframes price as investment against a measured return.
- Uses named borrowed ideas and credits them (Blair Enns, Jim Rohn, Jonathan Stark, David C. Baker, Marty Neumeier).
- Short maxims repeated: start serving, stop selling; do less but better; pick a lane.

## Where this lens misfires

- **He speaks to creatives selling services.** His pricing advice is for designers and agencies charging clients; for a merchant, flip it: use it to brief and pay agencies, and to price the merchant's own product on value. Do not treat a store's ad creative as a consulting engagement.
- **Not everything is measurable fast.** Brand work pays slowly; insisting on hard metrics can kill long-term identity work. He admits much is "just hard to measure" (S001).
- **Aesthetics debates.** He is provocative on purpose (S010); his strong taste calls (Under Armour, S011) are opinions, not tests.
- **Performance deals.** His role-play offer to work for a share of results (S009) can expose a small merchant to messy attribution; use with clear measurement.
- **Guests.** Some of the strongest lines in his channel are guests' (Neumeier, Haviv, Stark). Cite them as such.
