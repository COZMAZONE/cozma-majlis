# Frameworks: Chris Do

12 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (diagnose, price, brand, logo, focus). Most are taught by Chris Do on The Futur; some he credits to others (Blair Enns, Jim Rohn, David C. Baker, Jonathan Stark) and two come from guests on his channel (marked). Applications to a Saudi e-commerce merchant are the seat's reasoning: Chris Do teaches creatives how to sell; this seat uses the same logic to help the merchant brief, buy, and judge creative work, and to price on value.

---

## Diagnose

### 1. Diagnose before you prescribe (stop selling, start serving)
- **What it is:** Treat a client's request ("I need a webcam", "I need a logo") as a self-diagnosis to be checked. Ask questions to find the real problem before offering any solution, like a doctor ruling out causes.
- **When to use:** The merchant asks for a specific deliverable (new logo, new box, new site, more videos) as the fix for a sales problem.
- **How to score:** Has the real problem been named, with evidence? An option that answers the request without checking the diagnosis scores low.
- **Quote:** "people who are self-diagnosed, I need a webcam, usually don't know what they're doing." (S001)
- **Second quote:** "You want to close more jobs? Start serving." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S005, S008, S012

### 2. The value conversation sequence
- **What it is:** Replace "scope times hours equals price" with: (1) desired future state, (2) success metrics, (3) value of reaching it, (4) price as a fraction of that value. This lifts the talk "from the tactical to the strategic" (S001).
- **When to use:** Briefing any creative project; deciding a creative budget; judging an agency proposal.
- **How to score:** Does the option state the goal, the metric, and the riyal value? Missing any one means the budget is a guess.
- **Quote:** "So, we need to define what the success metrics are." (S001)
- **Second quote:** "This is how you have the money conversation, okay? How valuable is that to you?" (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S008

### 3. "I don't know, how will you measure that?" (mirroring)
- **What it is:** When the client asks how success will be measured, say you don't know and ask them back in their exact words, so they define the metric themselves.
- **When to use:** Setting KPIs for a campaign, a redesign, or a creative test.
- **How to score:** Was the success measure set by the merchant in plain words before work began?
- **Quote:** "I don't know is a very powerful statement okay you should use it more often" (S004)
- **Sources:** S004

### 4. Pain plus clear outcome ("Why not do nothing?")
- **What it is:** People act when they feel the pain and see a clear outcome. Ask "Why not do nothing?" to surface the cost of inaction; the size of the problem in the client's mind sets the budget.
- **When to use:** Deciding whether a creative project is worth doing now, or whether a product page and ad speak to a pain the customer actually feels.
- **How to score:** Can the merchant state the cost of doing nothing in riyals or lost orders? If not, the project is probably not urgent.
- **Quote:** "We're motivated by um degree of pain felt and how clear the outcome is." (S008)
- **Second quote:** "Anytime you can't sell something and it's not worth a lot, it's because they don't feel the pain." (S008)
- **Sources:** S008, S009

## Price

### 5. Value-based pricing: buyer sets value, seller sets price
- **What it is:** Price follows the value of the result to the buyer, not the seller's hours or costs. Hourly pricing punishes speed and skill.
- **When to use:** Paying a designer or agency; pricing the merchant's own bundles and premium tiers.
- **How to score:** Is the price anchored to a result the buyer values? An option priced by time or by competitors' price only scores low.
- **Quote:** "the buyer determines value the seller determines price" (S004)
- **Second quote:** "So, charging by the hour punishes me for being good." (S002)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S008

### 6. 1x, 10x, 100x (value relative to risk and reach)
- **What it is:** The same mark or asset is worth very different amounts to a small shop and a large company, because of what it touches and what it costs if it goes wrong. Price, and invest, relative to the stakes.
- **When to use:** Deciding how much to spend on identity, packaging, or hero photography that will be printed and seen at scale.
- **How to score:** Estimate how many units, ads, and pages the asset will touch, and the cost of getting it wrong. Spend in proportion.
- **Quote:** "So, why is it that some people can charge 1x for that, 10x for it, and 100x?" (S002)
- **Second quote:** "And it has to be relative to the risk of the company." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002

### 7. Proportionate investment and anchoring
- **What it is:** Match the effort to the size of the result (Jim Rohn's rule, which he quotes), and set the price conversation against the value, so the fee looks small next to the return. Discount for uncertainty when results are not in your control (S001; the phrase is usually traced to Blair Enns, though S001 does not name him).
- **When to use:** A merchant wants a big result (new launch, Ramadan peak) on a tiny creative budget, or the reverse.
- **How to score:** Fee as a percentage of the expected gain. Very small percentages for big stakes are a warning, not a bargain.
- **Quote:** "do not spend a small effort for a big result. And don't spend a big effort for a small result." (S001)
- **Second quote:** "And believe it or not, some clients get nervous about spending too little." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S009

### 8. Be the least risky choice
- **What it is:** Buyers rarely pick the best option; they pick the one that feels least risky. Social proof, referrals, and clear expertise lower perceived risk.
- **When to use:** Product page trust, packaging, choosing which proof to show, and choosing an agency.
- **How to score:** List what makes the option feel safe to the buyer (proof, guarantee, reviews, clarity). More real risk reducers, higher score.
- **Quote:** "Clients don't choose the best option. They choose the least risky option." (S002)
- **Second quote:** "Social proof is really important. They want to know that you're an expert." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002

## Brand and logo

### 9. Brand is not the logo
- **What it is:** Identity design makes symbols; branding includes strategy, positioning, product, user experience, and customer service. The brand itself is the customer's gut feeling and reputation (Marty Neumeier, S006). A logo is valuable only because the company is.
- **When to use:** The merchant believes a new logo or box will fix weak sales or reputation.
- **How to score:** Does the option change what customers experience (product, service, delivery, messaging), or only the symbol?
- **Quote:** "the logo only has value cuz the company's valuable" (S010)
- **Second quote (Neumeier, S006):** "a brand is a result it's it's a customer's gut feeling about a product a service or a company" (S006)
- **Sources:** S006, S010, S008

### 10. Logo and identity tests
- **What it is:** Haviv's three criteria on his channel: appropriate, distinctive and memorable, simple (S013). Chris Do adds: pass a basic aesthetic test, mock it on many applications (box, tape, uniform, truck, pattern), check it small and large, and put it beside long-lived brands you admire; it should look like it belongs (S011).
- **When to use:** Approving a logo, monogram, or packaging mark.
- **How to score:** One point per test passed: appropriate, distinctive, memorable, simple, works small, works on 6+ applications, belongs on the timeless board.
- **Quote:** "if anything the simpler your logo is the easier it is to pass this test" (S011)
- **Second quote (Haviv, S013):** "It needs to be appropriate, distinctive, and memorable. And then, it needs to be simple." (S013)
- **Sources:** S011, S013

## Focus

### 11. Specialize externally, generalist internally
- **What it is:** Be known for one thing to the market (halo bias then credits you with more), while staying curious inside. Show only your best work; buyers judge you by your worst piece.
- **When to use:** Positioning the store (one hero category, one customer), editing the product range and the content grid.
- **How to score:** Can a customer say in one line what the store is for? Is anything shown that is not the best?
- **Quote:** "you probably should specialize externally okay while you remain a generalist internally" (S007)
- **Second quote:** "they look at the worst piece and they imagine the rest of your work like that" (S007)
- **Sources:** S007, S012

### 12. Do less, but better (master the fundamentals, define the words)
- **What it is:** Excellence comes from mastering a few things, not doing more. Turn vague words in a brief ("make it epic, clean, modern") into explicit criteria on a scale, and stay in the discomfort of asking until they are clear.
- **When to use:** Writing or reviewing a creative brief; giving feedback on designs.
- **How to score:** Does the brief define its adjectives as testable criteria? Is the option the one thing done well, or many things done half-way?
- **Quote:** "I think the way to achieve Excellence is to do less but to do better" (S012)
- **Second quote:** "they use these words and we think we know what that means but it's dangerous to walk away and make that assumption" (S012)
- **Sources:** S012
