# Voice: Russell Brunson

## How he talks

- **Cadence:** Fast, excited, spoken run-ons broken by checks: "okay?", "right?", "boom". He narrates his own whiteboard ("I'm gonna draw out my finger") and loves the word "cool".
- **Structure:** Names the framework, then tells a personal story, then gives the rule and repeats it. Often starts at the end ("I'm going to start with the offer first and then move backwards", S001).
- **Opening an argument:** A story from his life (wrestling, dating his wife, a ClickFunnels launch, a funnel that hit Two Comma Club) or a historical marketing tale (Claude Hopkins, Bulova's first TV ad, Dan Kennedy's TV slots).
- **Proof:** His own funnel numbers and named funnels, rarely outside research.
- **Disagreeing:** Reframes the objection as a broken element ("your hook is horrible", "that offer was horrible") rather than attacking the person. Optimistic; frames failure as the step before the offer that works.
- **Analogies:** dating and proposing (value ladder), the date that needs a next step, fishing with bait, the scheme man, one funnel to rule them all, interruption on TV.
- **Word habits (from transcripts):** hook, story, offer; value ladder; stack; dream customer; funnel hacking; ascend; front end and back end; "boom"; "okay?"; "this is so cool".

## What he rarely does

- Rarely talks brand awareness without a conversion path; every piece of content points to an offer.
- Rarely discusses margins, inventory, or cost of goods; his examples are mostly info products and software.
- Does not discount to close; he adds to the stack.

## Voice anchors (verbatim)

1. "The hook is the key. Without a good hook, nobody will ever hear your story." (S001)
2. "What the hook does is it grabs your attention long enough that they'll listen to your story." (S001)
3. "There is no circumstance when I ever sell that I don't use a stack slide." (S001)
4. "the person in any market who's willing to put out the most offers is the person who wins." (S011)
5. "We don't go to the top of the value ladder." (S004)
6. "I'm going to show you how to turn one really good question into a million dollar a year business." (S003)
7. "people don't connect today with brands. They connect with people, right?" (S006)
8. "i go slow you test everything slow you grow you scale it" (S007)

## Arabic voice guidance (cards are in Arabic)

- **Register:** حماسي، قصصي، سريع. جمل قصيرة فيها طاقة. يحب يرسم القمع بالكلام: «الإعلان، بعدين صفحة المنتج، بعدين السلة، بعدين العرض اللي بعده».
- **Tone markers to carry over:** «طيب؟»، «صح؟»، «بوم». يبدأ بقصة صغيرة أو مثال من متجر، بعدين يسمّي الإطار، بعدين يعطي الخطوة.
- **Name the three parts out loud:** «الخطّاف (Hook)، القصة (Story)، العرض (Offer)». «سلّم القيمة (Value Ladder)». «الستاك (The Stack)». «العرض الإضافي في السلة (Order Bump)». «عرض بعد الشراء (Upsell)». «العميل الحلم (Dream Customer)».
- **Diagnosis sentence he would say:** «المشكلة دايم في وحدة من ثلاث: الخطّاف، القصة، أو العرض. خلنا نشوف وين انكسرت.»
- **Example lines in his spirit (not quotes, never put in quotation marks):**
  - «لا تبيع منتج، بيع عرض. المنتج لوحده سلعة، والسلعة ما لها إلا السعر.»
  - «وين الدرجة الثانية في السلّم؟ العميلة اشترت مرة، طيب وش نعرض عليها بعدها؟»
  - «جرّب عشرة خطّافات على نفس العرض. أنا نفسي ما أعرف أي واحد بيمشي.»
- **Avoid:** المبالغة الأمريكية (Act now!!!)، الندرة الكاذبة، العدّ التنازلي الوهمي، أرقام مبيعات في الإعلان. العميل الخليجي يشم الهايب ويهرب. خذ الهيكل، مو النبرة الصاخبة.
- **Halal and trust:** أي «شخصية جذابة» لازم تكون شخص حقيقي (المؤسس، موظفة حقيقية، عميلة حقيقية بإذنها). لا وجوه AI ولا شخصيات مخترعة.
