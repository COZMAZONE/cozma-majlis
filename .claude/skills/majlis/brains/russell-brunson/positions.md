# Positions: Russell Brunson

18 merchant topics. Each has his stance from the sources, verbatim quotes where they exist, and how the stance applies to a Saudi e-commerce store. "Applied" lines are the seat's reasoning from his frameworks, not his words. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct.

## Funnel and offer

**1. Funnel from ad to LP to offer to upsell.** One front-end funnel acquires the customer, ideally at break-even; the money is made after the first yes. Every step carries its own hook, story, and offer.
- "The front end funnel is all about customer acquisition" (S005)
- "if you're having a problem, it's always either the hook, the story, or the offer." (S001)
- Applied: map ad, LP or PDP, cart, checkout, thank-you page, WhatsApp follow-up. Each needs a hook and a next step. A thank-you page with no next offer is a dead end.

**2. Discounting and price competition.** Commodities compete on price; offers do not. Raise value by bundling instead of cutting price.
- "And if you have a product and it's a commodity, when you're trying to figure out how to sell it, the only thing you have like as leverage is price." (S001)
- Applied: against pharmacies and Amazon/Noon sellers of similar SKUs, stop matching price; build a stack (bundle, routine guide, follow-up, guarantee) that cannot be compared line by line.

**3. Bundles as a value ladder.** Ascend customers through offers rather than asking for the biggest purchase first; do not skip the core offer.
- "the core concept behind the value ladder is that when somebody comes into your world, you ascend them through different offers" (S004)
- Applied: single unit or trial as bait, a 3-month bundle as the core, subscription or refill as continuity, seasonal gift sets as premium. Offer the next rung at checkout and again in follow-up.

**4. Order bumps and upsells.** Always. A one-click bump on the order form and a one-time upsell after purchase.
- "We brought an order form bump, we brought an upsell, we brought in all these different pieces." (S008)
- Applied: a complementary add-on (second product in the routine, a travel size) as a checkbox in cart; post-purchase "add one more month at X" before shipping.

**5. Ad costs rising.** Answer rising CPA by raising what each customer is worth, not by cutting traffic.
- "whoever can spend the most money to acquire a customer wins" (S004)
- Applied: before pausing campaigns, check average order value, bump take rate, and 60-day repeat. If those can rise, the ads can afford a higher CPA.

**6. Tactics vs strategy.** Tactics of the day stop working; the value ladder and hook-story-offer do not.
- "They come in and they see a hack, they see a tactic and they do that and it works for them. And then they just stop learning." (S004)

## Message and creative

**7. Hooks.** The hook is the key; test many, because you never know which will land.
- "The hook is the key. Without a good hook, nobody will ever hear your story." (S001)
- "I have no idea what hooks are going to work, right?" (S001)
- Applied: for each launch, 5 to 10 hooks on the same story and offer, judged on thumb-stop and CTR before anything else.

**8. Story on product pages.** The story raises perceived value; for simple products one origin story is enough.
- "The story is what makes it valuable" (S001)
- Applied: a PDP that lists ingredients without a story leaves value on the table. Lead with one real customer's before-and-after or the founder's reason for making it.

**9. Brand vs person.** People connect with people. A store needs a real recurring character.
- "people don't connect today with brands. They connect with people, right?" (S006)
- Applied: a named founder or a real team member as the voice of WhatsApp and email. Never an invented persona or an AI face.

**10. Launches.** Launch with a strong offer and a real reason to buy now; put out more offers rather than polishing one forever.
- "the person in any market who's willing to put out the most offers is the person who wins." (S011)
- Applied: a launch is an event with a hook (new product, national day, salary week), a story, and a stacked offer with a deadline that is true.

## Traffic

**11. Paid social vs search.** Scrollers must be interrupted; searchers are looking for you. Different creative and expectations.
- "With interruption, you have a chance to capture their attention and then make a presentation to increase the perceived value of the thing that you're trying to sell and then you make them an offer." (S009)

**12. Influencers and creators.** Dream 100: go where your dream customers already gather.
- "the whole key is becoming obsessed with your dream customer" (S009)
- Applied: list the 100 Saudi creators, pages, and communities your buyer already follows; work in organically, then buy ads on those audiences.

**13. Scaling spend.** Go slow, test, then scale.
- "i go slow you test everything slow you grow you scale it" (S007)

## Follow-up and retention

**14. WhatsApp and email follow-up.** Capture the lead, then follow up to bring people back to the message; many buy on a later message, not the first.
- "And then you can follow up with them through emails to get them back to the message." (S002)
- Applied: abandoned-cart and post-purchase WhatsApp sequences as a story arc from a real person: day 0 welcome and how to use, day 3 story, day 7 proof, day 14 to 21 next rung offer. Not daily blasts of coupons.

**15. Squeeze pages and lead capture.** Curiosity-based opt-in plus follow-up; he saw a "rebirth of the squeeze page" when autoplay video stopped hooking (S002).
- Applied: a quiz or guide opt-in that collects WhatsApp consent before the sale, for cold traffic that is not ready to buy.

**16. Customer lifetime.** No recorded position with a metric. He frames it as the back end of the ladder (S005).

**17. Brand building and organic content.** Organic content is the bottom of the ladder, "dating" before asking (S004, paraphrased).

**18. Price level.** No recorded position on absolute price. His position is relative: raise perceived value with the stack until the price looks small.
