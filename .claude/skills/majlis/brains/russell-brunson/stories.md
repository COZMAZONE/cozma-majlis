# Stories: Russell Brunson

7 stories and cases he reuses in the sources. Figures are as he tells them.

1. **Offers that saved the company, five times.** At each wall in his career (an early Christmas, a payroll crisis, losing every merchant account overnight, a ClickFunnels launch that failed until a live room of about 300 people, a library project no bank would fund), the turnaround was a new offer, not a new product. Lesson: when stuck, make an offer; whoever makes the most offers wins. (S011, S012)

2. **The ClickFunnels launch and the stage presentation.** The software launch underperformed; a presentation delivered on stage closed a large share of the room and became the webinar that grew the company, without venture capital. Lesson: one-to-many selling with hook, story, offer beats a product launch on its own. (S011, S013, S014 company account)

3. **Claude Hopkins and the "scheme man."** In the early 1900s companies paid a scheme man, whose only job was the offer, the equivalent of over a million dollars a year. Lesson: the offer is the most valuable part of marketing. (S001)

4. **The 30days.com question funnel.** One good question ("if you lost everything, what would you do in 30 days?") answered by 30 experts became a book, a summit, a challenge, and a funnel with an order bump and upsells that reached his Two Comma Club level quickly. Lesson: one strong question can be the spine of a whole value ladder. (S003, S008)

5. **Dating and the value ladder.** You do not propose on a first date; you ascend the relationship. Organic content is the dating; the free-plus-shipping book is the first yes; coaching is the top. Lesson: ask for the next rung, not the top rung. (S004)

6. **Dan Kennedy and the late-night TV slots.** Kennedy's team bought off-hours TV time, then had to add upsells, downsells, and higher-ticket follow-ups to afford rising media costs. Lesson: the business that monetizes best can buy the most traffic. (S004)

7. **Changing the attractive character at Magnetic Marketing.** After acquiring Dan Kennedy's company he deliberately introduced a new attractive character the community could connect with, rather than making it about himself. Lesson: brands need a real person, and that person can be someone other than the founder. (S006)
