# Frameworks: Russell Brunson

12 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (message unit, offer, ladder and funnel, traffic, character). Book tags show where each sits in his series, as he teaches it in public. Quotes come from auto transcripts. Each framework has an e-commerce note for a Saudi store selling physical products on Salla or Zid.

---

## The message unit

### 1. Hook, Story, Offer *(all three books)*
- **What it is:** Every ad, landing page, email, sales page, and upsell does three jobs in order. The hook stops attention, the story raises the perceived value, the offer converts. He calls it the only framework in all three of his books (S001).
- **Parts:** Hook: the thing that makes you stop (headline, first frame, subject line, thumbnail). Story: usually the origin or Epiphany Bridge story that breaks a false belief. Offer: the stack and the close.
- **When to use:** Diagnosing any underperforming funnel step, writing any new creative, reviewing an LP.
- **How to score:** Read the metrics as three gates. Low CTR or thumb-stop: hook. Clicks but low time on page or low add-to-cart: story. Add-to-cart but low purchase: offer (price, risk, stack, checkout). Fix the earliest broken gate first.
- **Quote:** "What the hook does is it grabs your attention long enough that they'll listen to your story. The story increases the value and then the offer increases the value." (S001)
- **E-commerce note:** For a PDP or ad, the hook is the first second of video or the first line; the story is one customer's before-and-after or the founder's reason; the offer is the bundle, the guarantee, and the price shown against the stack.
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S009, S010

### 2. Hook testing *(Traffic Secrets)*
- **What it is:** You cannot know in advance which hook lands, so you launch many and let the market pick.
- **Parts:** Write many hooks for the same story and offer; watch which ones people grab onto; scale those, retire the rest.
- **When to use:** Before arguing about which creative is "right." Before scaling budget.
- **How to score:** An option that tests 1 creative scores low; one that tests 5 to 10 hooks against the same offer scores high.
- **Quote:** "I have no idea what hooks are going to work, right?" (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002

### 3. Epiphany Bridge story *(Expert Secrets)*
- **What it is:** Tell the story of how you (or a customer) had the realization that changed everything, so the listener has the same epiphany.
- **Parts:** Backstory (normal life before), the wall, the epiphany, the new belief, the result. For simple products one origin story is enough; complex offers need stories that break each false belief (the vehicle, internal, and external beliefs).
- **When to use:** Product page copy, founder videos, launch content, the first emails after opt-in.
- **How to score:** Does the copy tell one specific story that breaks the buyer's main objection, or does it list features?
- **Quote:** "So for simple products, one story, the origin story is all you need and people are in." (S001)
- **Quote:** "you have to tell a better story that trumps theirs." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

## The offer

### 4. Offer vs commodity and the Stack *(DotCom Secrets, Expert Secrets)*
- **What it is:** A product alone is a commodity and competes on price. An offer bundles the product with other things that raise value and make it impossible to compare.
- **Parts:** List everything the buyer gets, put a value on each, show the total against the price. He uses a stack slide in every sale, even in an email.
- **When to use:** Any time the merchant is tempted to discount, or the product looks like the pharmacy's product.
- **How to score:** If a shopper can compare the offer one-to-one with a competitor's SKU, it is a commodity. Score higher the more unique pieces are bundled (guide, routine, bonus size, follow-up, guarantee).
- **Quote:** "And if you have a product and it's a commodity, when you're trying to figure out how to sell it, the only thing you have like as leverage is price." (S001)
- **Quote:** "There is no circumstance when I ever sell that I don't use a stack slide." (S001)
- **E-commerce note:** A 3-month bundle plus a printed routine plus a WhatsApp check-in at day 14 is an offer; "collagen 30 sachets" is a commodity.
- **Sources:** S001

### 5. More offers, one offer away
- **What it is:** Whoever puts out the most offers wins; a business is always one good offer away from a breakthrough.
- **When to use:** When a store is stuck; when the merchant wants to perfect one page for months.
- **Quote:** "the person in any market who's willing to put out the most offers is the person who wins." (S011)
- **Quote:** "the thing that turned everything around was an offer." (S012)
- **Sources:** S011, S012

## Ladder and funnel

### 6. Value Ladder *(DotCom Secrets)*
- **What it is:** A sequence of offers rising in price and value. The customer enters at a low rung and ascends.
- **Parts:** Free content (dating, building the relationship), bait (free or low-cost), the core offer, then higher-ticket continuity or premium. He warns that many businesses skip the core middle offer and jump from free to expensive, a shortcut that worked for a season while ad costs were low (S004). His own ladder runs from free content to free-plus-shipping books, low-priced courses and events, then coaching and inner circle (S004).
- **When to use:** Designing bundles (1, 2, 3 months), subscriptions, sampler kits, and what comes after the first order.
- **How to score:** Draw the ladder. Is there a clear first rung that a stranger buys easily, a core rung most people should land on, and a next rung offered right after? Missing rungs score low.
- **Quote:** "We don't go to the top of the value ladder." (S004, about not proposing on a first date)
- **Quote:** "And so the value ladder is the key." (S004)
- **E-commerce note:** Trial size or single unit (bait), 3-month bundle (core), subscription or refill program (continuity), gift sets for seasons (premium).
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S007, S009

### 7. Front end and back end ("one funnel to rule them all")
- **What it is:** Every business has one front-end funnel whose only job is customer acquisition, often at break-even or a small loss. A second system (back end) monetizes the customer.
- **Parts:** Front end: ad, landing page, entry offer. Back end: order bump, upsells, follow-up email or WhatsApp, higher rungs.
- **When to use:** Deciding where the merchant's time and budget go; judging an ad on first-order ROAS alone.
- **How to score:** Is there one named front end the team obsesses over? Does the back end exist so first-order break-even is acceptable?
- **Quote:** "The front end funnel is all about customer acquisition" (S005)
- **Quote:** "But then after you have them in, you've already spent the money to acquire that customer." (S005)
- **Sources:** S005, S008

### 8. Order bump and one-time upsell
- **What it is:** A one-click add-on on the order form, then an upsell right after the first yes.
- **Parts:** Bump: small, complementary, one click (the audiobook with the book; he reports about 36% taking it on the Traffic Secrets funnel, S009). Upsell: the next logical thing, offered once, right after purchase.
- **When to use:** Every checkout. In Salla terms: cart upsell, "add a second unit" toggle, post-purchase offer page or thank-you message.
- **How to score:** Count the monetization points after "add to cart." Zero scores low.
- **Quote:** "We brought an order form bump, we brought an upsell, we brought in all these different pieces." (S008)
- **Sources:** S008, S009

### 9. Whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer wins
- **What it is:** A Dan Kennedy principle he builds his model on: the business that makes the most money from a customer can pay the most for traffic and starves competitors of it. Rising ad costs forced direct-response marketers to add upsells, downsells, and higher-ticket follow-ups (S004).
- **When to use:** Whenever CPA rises and the merchant wants to cut ads instead of raising value per customer.
- **Quote:** "So now instead of just selling the product off of our TV time, now we had to start having upsells and downsells, right?" (S004)
- **Sources:** S004

## Traffic

### 10. Dream customer, searcher vs scroller *(Traffic Secrets)*
- **What it is:** Know your dream customer obsessively, then decide if they are searching for you (search marketing) or must be interrupted (social feed).
- **Parts:** Searchers: Google and YouTube search, capture demand. Scrollers: interruption ads, where hook, story, and offer must work in seconds.
- **Quote:** "the whole key is becoming obsessed with your dream customer" (S009)
- **Quote:** "With interruption, you have a chance to capture their attention and then make a presentation to increase the perceived value of the thing that you're trying to sell and then you make them an offer." (S009)
- **E-commerce note:** Snap and TikTok are scroller channels; Google brand and category search is searcher traffic. Different creatives, different expectations.
- **Sources:** S009

### 11. Dream 100
- **What it is:** List the 100 people, pages, and creators who already gather your dream customers, then work your way into them (organic first, then buy ads on their audiences).
- **When to use:** Influencer and creator partnerships; when cold paid ads fail but the offer is sound.
- **Quote:** "In fact, Dana, this is the reason why your stuff works really, really good on Dream 100 and sucks on Facebook ads. It's not because the offer doesn't work." (S010)
- **Sources:** S010

## Character and follow-up

### 12. Attractive Character and follow-up sequence *(DotCom Secrets)*
- **What it is:** People connect with a person, not a logo. A recurring character (founder, expert, real customer) tells stories in follow-up messages that move people up the ladder.
- **Parts:** Backstory, flaws, story arcs; a daily or sequence follow-up (Soap Opera Sequence and daily "Seinfeld" emails in his books, paraphrased here, not quoted). Squeeze page plus email follow-up brings people back to the message (S002).
- **When to use:** WhatsApp and email sequences after opt-in or purchase; brand voice decisions.
- **Quote:** "people don't connect today with brands. They connect with people, right?" (S006)
- **Quote:** "And then you can follow up with them through emails to get them back to the message." (S002)
- **E-commerce note:** A WhatsApp sequence from a named person ("Nora from the store") who shares her own routine converts better in his model than a broadcast from the logo. Keep it real: a real founder or real customers, never invented personas.
- **Sources:** S002, S006
