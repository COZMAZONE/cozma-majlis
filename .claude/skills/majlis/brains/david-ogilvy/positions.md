# Positions: David Ogilvy

34 topics a Saudi or Gulf Salla/Zid merchant (beauty and supplement DTC) brings to the creative council. Each has his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and year, and where it applies today. Years are the work's year (Confessions 1963, Ogilvy on Advertising 1983) or the recording's year. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct; the stance after it is marked "(inferred, no direct source)". He worked in print and TV, so anything about phones, feeds, and Salla pages is applied, not his words.

## Attention and the first seconds

**1. Hooks and the first seconds of a video.** Open with the product's most dramatic visual, not a logo or a slow build. The opening must work with no sound.
- "If you are advertising a fire extinguisher, open with the fire." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- "If you grab attention in the first frame with a visual surprise, you stand a better chance of holding the viewer." (S014, OoA 1983)
- Applied: first frame = the result or the problem on a real face, product in hand by second 2, captions burned in.

**2. Headlines (and the first line of a caption, product title, push).** The headline is the ad. Promise a benefit, add news, name the brand, be clear at a glance; no puns or blind headlines.
- "On the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy." (S013, Confessions 1963)
- "Some copywriters write tricky headlines" (S015, OoA 1983); "This is a sin." (S035, citing Confessions)
- "include the brand name and the promise in their headline" (S007, c. 1980)

**3. Story appeal in images.** Choose pictures that make people ask a question; the copy answers it.
- "it's another example story appeal in the picture" (S008, c. 1973, on the empty-room Puerto Rico ad)
- Applied: a thumbnail of a real woman mid-routine with an unexpected detail beats a clean pack shot on white.

## Copy

**4. Long versus short copy.** Long copy sells more when the buyer must think or spend; short copy is for impulse. Length must be earned by facts and interest.
- "in print advertising you know that long copy sells more than short copy" (S006)
- "Long copy sells more than short copy, particularly when you are asking the reader to spend a lot of money." (S027, OoA 1983)
- Applied: a 3-month collagen bundle deserves a long product page and an advertorial; a retargeting story frame can be one line.

**5. Specific facts over adjectives.** Facts persuade; "best", "luxury", "amazing" make readers discount everything.
- "All facts, no adjectives or specific." (S011)
- "Concrete figures must be substituted for atmospheric claims" (S018, 1936 credo)

**6. Tone and language.** Write the way people talk, to one person, in their words; never jargon, never talk down.
- "Write the way you talk. Naturally." (S003, 1982)
- "It is a mistake to use highfalutin language when you advertise to uneducated people." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- Applied: write in the buyer's Gulf Arabic or plain MSA, not translated marketing English.

**7. Emotion versus reason.** Mostly reason-why, with facts, because most brand choice is conscious. He later admitted emotion sells products that are no different from rivals.
- "I give her rational reasons why she should buy" (S008, c. 1973)
- "I think I've always underestimated the importance of emotion" (S007, c. 1980)
- Shift: 1955 he already said similar products are bought on image (S002); by c. 1980 he conceded emotion outright (S007).

**8. Humour.** Early: never; clowns do not sell. Late: research showed funny commercials can work. Keep humour on the product, never at the buyer.
- "people do not buy from clowns" (S018, 1936 credo)
- "we discovered for the first time that funny commercials can be very effective at the cash register" (S007, c. 1980)

## People and proof

**9. UGC, testimonials, and reviews.** Testimonials work; real users with specifics are among the strongest techniques. Fake ones fail his family test.
- "testimonials also work well" (S007, c. 1980)
- "Never write an advertisement which you wouldn't want your own family to read." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- Applied: real customer clips with names (with permission) and specific results; never scripted actors posing as buyers.

**10. Real people versus models and celebrities.** Celebrities are remembered but do not persuade; viewers know they were paid. Real people and long-running real characters (Commander Whitehead was the actual Schweppes executive) work.
- "people remember the celebrity and forget the product" (S009, c. 1983)
- "Viewers guess the celebrity has been bought, and they are right." (S026, quoting OoA 1983)
- His own regret: the $35,000 Eleanor Roosevelt margarine ad (S017, S009).

**11. Influencers.** No recorded position. (inferred, no direct source) He would treat a paid influencer as a celebrity (remembered, discounted as bought) unless the creator genuinely uses the product and demonstrates it; demand disclosure and a product-first script.

**12. AI-generated actors or synthetic faces.** No recorded position. (inferred, no direct source) Fails his honesty test if presented as a real customer; at best a stock model, which he rated weak next to real testimony and story appeal.

## Brand, offers, price

**13. Brand building versus direct response.** He held both: direct response is the measurable discipline, and every ad also builds or erodes the brand image. The 1955 talk recants his early pure mail-order view.
- "direct response was my first love and later it became my secret weapon" (S006)
- "I used to accuse them of hiding behind long-term effect." (S002, 1955)
- Applied: every performance ad must still look like the brand; every brand ad must still have a measurable action.

**14. Discounts, deals, and price promotions.** Deals buy short-term volume and cheapen the image; spend on advertising that builds the brand. He chose a testimonial campaign over an "Amazing Offer" that would have sold more shoes in the short term (S002).
- "Sure, deals will get you display space on the stores, and features. But they can cost too much." (S002, 1955)
- "Any damn fool can put on a deal, but it takes genius, faith and perseverance to create a brand." (S036)
- Applied: run the national-day offer as news with a real end date, not as the permanent identity.

**15. Premium positioning and price perception.** Superior products at premium prices are a real trend; taste in execution signals quality.
- "there are now unmistakeable signs of a trend in favor of superior products at premium prices" (S014, OoA 1983)
- "There are very few products which do not benefit from being given a first-class ticket through life." (S001, Confessions 1963)

**16. Brand image and consistency.** Write a one-line image definition and stick to it for years; similar products are chosen on image.
- "Every advertisement is part of the long-term investment in the personality of the brand." (S002, 1955)
- "Blurred images don't inspire much loyalty among consumers." (S002, 1955)

**17. Packaging and naming.** Personality comes from name, pack, price, and ad style together; make the brand name impossible to miss.
- "The personality of a product is an amalgam of many things" (S001, OoA 1983)
- "most advertisements are deficient in brand identification" (S001, Confessions 1963)
- No recorded position on naming a new product; (inferred, no direct source) a name that states the benefit beats a clever one.

**18. Guarantees and risk reversal.** No recorded position in this corpus. (inferred, no direct source) He would favour a stated, honest guarantee as a fact in the copy, never a fine-print trick.

## Design and formats

**19. Design and layout.** Picture, headline, caption, copy, in that order; editorial look; readable type; no reverse body text; check it at real size.
- "It is physically impossible to read it" (S005, 1982, on white-on-black type)
- "the less an advertisement looks like an advertisement, and the more it looks like an editorial, the more readers stop, look, and read" (S001)
- "If you pin up the layouts on a bulletin board and appraise them from fifteen feet, you will produce posters." (S001)
- Applied: review every creative on a phone, in the feed, not on a desktop monitor.

**20. Video ads.** Demonstration, problem-solution, slice of life, news, and testimonials sell; jingles, celebrities, and visual cliches do not. Never hide the brand.
- "problem solution works like Gang Busters" (S007)
- "don't use visual cliches like happy families at the dinner table sunsets and sunrises" (S007)
- "Try running your commercial with the sound turned off; if it doesn't tell without sound, it is useless." (S001, Confessions 1963)

**21. Music and jingles.** Do not sing the pitch; people cannot hear the words. Music may set mood.
- "The advertisers who believe in the selling power of jingles have never had to sell anything." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- "if you've nothing to say sing it" (S007), said with contempt.

**22. Sex, modesty, and relevance.** Use only if relevant to the product; his first ad (a nude painting for a cooker) was his own example of what not to do. For a Saudi audience, the relevance test and his respect for religious sensibilities both point to modest casting.
- "what the hell did a nude girl have to do with a cooking stove I know better now" (S009)
- "So play it straight." (S026, quoting OoA 1983)

**23. Landing pages and product pages.** No recorded position on web pages. (inferred, no direct source) Treat the page as a long-copy direct response ad: benefit headline, facts, captions under every photo, testimonials, subheads, a clear order button; the editorial advertorial format he championed (S001, S008) fits.

## Research, testing, measurement

**24. Research before creative.** Mandatory; he built the agency on it.
- "Advertising people who ignore research are as dangerous as generals who ignore decodes of enemy signals." (S014, OoA 1983)
- "I am helpless without research material" (S004, 1955)

**25. Testing and A/B.** Test the promise, headline, picture, length, spend; split-run headlines.
- "The most important word in the vocabulary of advertising is TEST." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- "keep testing new Headlines by split run" (S007)

**26. Data and measurement.** Prefer what you can measure to the dollar; most advertising is guesswork.
- "But most advertising all over today is guess work, even here in these United States." (S005, 1982)
- "you know to a dollar the general advertisers and their agencies know almost nothing for sure" (S006)
- Caution: he also said research cannot predict long-term image effects (S008).

**27. Creative fatigue and changing campaigns.** Change because results fall, not because the team is bored; new buyers arrive daily.
- "You aren't advertising to a standing army; you are advertising to a moving parade." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- "Scores of great advertisements have been discarded before they have begun to pay off." (S001)
- Applied: on Snap and TikTok, rotate executions of one idea; check frequency and CTR decay before declaring the idea dead.

**28. Repetition and frequency.** Repeat a good ad; only the advertiser is tired of it. Test frequency like everything else.
- "Apparent monotony of treatment must be tolerated, because only the manufacturer reads all his own advertisements." (S018)
- "Test your frequency." (S001; four words, part of the TEST list)

## Industry and process

**29. Awards and "creativity" versus sales.** Distrust both; originality for its own sake is the most dangerous word in advertising.
- "Resist the temptation to write the kind of copy which wins awards." (S001, Confessions 1963)
- "they worship at the altar of creativity which really means originality the most dangerous word in the Lexicon of advertising" (S006)

**30. Agencies versus in-house.** Pick a strong agency, then do not do its job; the client decides because it is his money. He would hire people bigger than himself.
- "Do not compete with your agency in the creative area. Why keep a dog and bark yourself?" (S001, OoA 1983)
- "it's his product it's his company and it's his money" (S007)

**31. Committees and approvals.** One writer with research and a closed door beats a committee; the final call belongs to the owner.
- "A lot of advertisements and television commercials look like minutes of a committee meeting, and that is what they are." (S001, Confessions 1963)

**32. Competitors.** Study their ads for years back; do the opposite; never sling mud.
- "In general, study the methods of your competitors and do the exact opposite." (S018, 1935 to 1936)
- "Try and avoid being drawn into discussing competitive makes of cooker, as it introduces a negative and defensive atmosphere." (S018)

**33. Claims and honesty.** Lies are found out; an inferior product cannot be sold for long by ads. For health-adjacent products, every claim needs proof you could show your family.
- "If you tell lies about your product you will be found out" (S001, Confessions 1963)
- "If you don't think the product is good, you have no business to be advertising it." (S013)

**34. Production budget.** Expensive production does not mean more sales.
- "I suspect that there is a negative correlation between the money spent on producing commercials and their power to sell products." (S026, quoting OoA 1983)
- Applied: a well-lit phone testimonial with a strong headline can beat a costly studio film.
