# Frameworks: David Ogilvy

23 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (research and strategy, the idea, headline and copy, picture and layout, video, proof and honesty, brand, testing, process). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that teach the framework or a clear synonym of it. Book tags in italics show where it sits in his work: *Confessions* (Confessions of an Advertising Man, 1963), *OoA* (Ogilvy on Advertising, 1983), *HCATS* (How to Create Advertising That Sells, 1971 house ad), *Unpublished* (The Unpublished David Ogilvy). Video quotes are auto captions, so they keep caption spelling.

He insisted these are not laws: "They are not rules. They are reports." (S001) Score with them, then let a big idea overrule a technique if the test results say so.

## Research and strategy

### 1. Do your homework first: product, precedents, research *(Confessions, OoA)*
- **What it is:** Before any idea, learn more about the product, the buyer, and the category's past ads than anyone in the room.
- **Parts:** (1) Study the product until you find the fact nobody has used. (2) Study the precedents: what competitors ran for years and what worked. (3) Study the research on how buyers think and what promise moves them. (4) Write a one-paragraph problem statement and get it agreed before writing (S004).
- **When to use:** Every brief, and every time a creative is presented without a reason it should work.
- **How to score:** 1 to 10 on evidence behind the ad: 1 = "we liked it", 5 = one customer insight, 8 = product fact plus competitor review plus buyer language, 10 = all three plus a past test result. Below 5, send it back.
- **Quote:** "some flashy agencies scorn homework they skid about on the surface of irrelevant Brilliance" (S007); "I look at every advertisement which has appeared for competing products during the past 20 years." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S007, S008, S010, S015, S021, S025)

### 2. Positioning: decide what it does and for whom *(OoA, HCATS)*
- **What it is:** The single most important decision, made before the ads: what the product is for and who it is for.
- **Parts:** Name the job (Dove: a toilet bar for dry skin, not a detergent bar; SAAB in Norway: a car for winter). Name the buyer. Then check execution, because the right position can still produce a dead ad.
- **When to use:** New products, flat sales, or when the team argues about the message.
- **How to score:** Can the merchant say the position in one sentence a stranger repeats back? Yes and it is narrow = 8 to 10; vague or "for everyone" = 1 to 4.
- **Quote:** "The most important decision is how to position your product." (S001); "I could have positioned Dove as a detergent bar for men with dirty hands, but chose instead to position it as a toilet bar for women with dry skin." (S019)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S008, S019, S029)

### 3. One promise, important and unique, tested *(HCATS, OoA)*
- **What it is:** Boil the strategy down to one benefit the buyer wants, that you can own, and prove it is the most persuasive one before you scale.
- **Parts:** List candidate promises; cut to one; check it is important (the buyer cares) and unique (rivals cannot say it); test it.
- **When to use:** A brief with five messages, or an ad that tries to say everything.
- **How to score:** Count the promises in the ad. One clear promise = up to 10; two = max 6; three or more = max 3. Add points only if research or a test picked the promise.
- **Quote:** "It pays to boil down your strategy to one simple promise and go the whole hog in delivering that promise." (S001); "the promise should be important and unique and use research to find out what promise is most persuasive" (S007)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S015, S018, S029)

## The idea

### 4. The Big Idea test (five questions) *(OoA)*
- **What it is:** Without a big idea the ad "will pass like a ship in the night". His five-question check for recognizing one.
- **Parts:** Did it make me gasp? Do I wish I had thought of it? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy to perfection? Could it run for 30 years? He adds that big ideas are usually simple and come from a well-fed unconscious.
- **When to use:** Before production money is spent; when choosing between concepts.
- **How to score:** 2 points per yes (max 10). Under 6, do not shoot it. Fit-to-strategy "no" is a veto.
- **Quote:** "Unless your advertising is built on a BIG IDEA it will pass like a ship in the night." (S001); "Could it be used for 30 years?" (S015)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S007, S008, S010, S011, S015, S016, S029)

### 5. Story appeal in the picture *(Confessions, OoA)*
- **What it is:** A picture that makes the viewer ask "what is happening here?" gets looked at, and the curious read the copy. The Hathaway eye patch and the empty room with Casals's cello came from this research finding.
- **Parts:** An unexpected detail tied to the product (patch on an elegant man, an empty chair before a concert); the copy then answers with product facts.
- **When to use:** Hero images, thumbnails, the first frame of a video.
- **How to score:** Show the image for one second to three people. If they ask a question about it = 7 to 10; if they only say "nice" = 3 to 5; if they cannot say what was sold = 1 to 2.
- **Quote:** "the more story appeal there was in a picture in a photograph the more people would look at your ad" (S008); "research led me to the idea which put our agency on the map and made Hathaway famous" (S007)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S008, S009, S027, S028, S035)

### 6. Make the product the hero *(OoA)*
- **What it is:** The product, its ingredients, and how it works carry the ad. No borrowed interest that steals attention from the pack.
- **Parts:** Product on screen, product fact in the headline, product demonstrated. "There are no dull products, only dull writers."
- **When to use:** When a creative leans on a mood, a model, or a joke that could sell any brand.
- **How to score:** Cover the logo. If a rival's name could be swapped in without changing a word = 1 to 3; if only this product fits = 8 to 10.
- **Quote:** "Whenever you can, make the product itself the hero of your advertising." (S015); "you notice the product was the hero I'm very keen on that" (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S008, S015, S021)

## Headline and copy

### 7. Headline rules *(Confessions, OoA)*
- **What it is:** The headline does most of the selling because most people read only it.
- **Parts:** Promise a benefit; flag the prospect ("the ticket on the meat"); put news in it (NEW, how to); include the brand name; 6 to 12 words pull best in direct response; no blind, tricky, or pun headlines; avoid negatives that readers misread; write many and test.
- **When to use:** Ad headlines, the first line of a caption, the first spoken line of a video, product page titles, email subject lines.
- **How to score:** One point each: benefit, brand name, news or specific fact, understood at a glance, no pun, no negative, under about 12 words, flags the buyer, chosen from 10 or more options, tested. Max 10.
- **Quote:** "On the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy." (S013); "The headlines which work best are those which promise the reader a benefit" (S015); "Your headline should telegraph what you want to say." (S015)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S007, S011, S013, S015, S030, S035, S006)

### 8. Facts, not adjectives *(Confessions, OoA, Aga manual)*
- **What it is:** Specific, checkable facts persuade; superlatives and atmosphere make readers discount everything.
- **Parts:** Replace each adjective with a number, an ingredient, a test, a source, or a named person. Quote third parties (Rolls-Royce quoted motoring magazines).
- **When to use:** Every line of copy; especially premium or health-adjacent products where trust is the sale.
- **How to score:** Count facts versus adjectives. Facts over 70% of claims = 8 to 10; mostly adjectives ("best", "amazing", "luxury") = 1 to 3.
- **Quote:** "When I advertised Rolls-Royce, I gave the facts - no hot air, no adjectives, no gracious living" (S021); "Concrete figures must be substituted for atmospheric claims" (S018)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S008, S011, S018, S021, S027, S035)

### 9. Long copy when the buyer must think *(OoA, We Sell or Else)*
- **What it is:** Direct response tests showed long copy outsells short copy, most of all for complicated or expensive purchases. Length is earned by interest, not padding.
- **Parts:** Subheads every few paragraphs, numbered facts, captions, a clear offer at the end. If it bores, cut it.
- **When to use:** Product pages, landing pages, advertorials, long Snap/TikTok explainers for considered buys.
- **How to score:** Does the page answer every question a careful buyer asks (what, how, proof, price, guarantee, how to use)? Each missing answer costs 2 points.
- **Quote:** "in print advertising you know that long copy sells more than short copy" (S006); "when you have a complicated message to deliver long copy works better than short" (S007); "Long copy sells more than short copy, particularly when you are asking the reader to spend a lot of money." (S027)
- **Sources:** 7 (S006, S007, S008, S021, S027, S028, S034)

### 10. The dinner-party test *(filmed interviews)*
- **What it is:** Write what you would say to one intelligent friend who asked "which one should I buy?"
- **Parts:** Personal, factual, truthful, interesting; reasons, not slogans; one person, not "the market".
- **When to use:** Any copy that sounds like an ad, a brochure, or a committee.
- **How to score:** Read it aloud as if to a friend. Natural and specific = 8 to 10; slogans or jargon = 1 to 4.
- **Quote:** "your answer should be personal factual truthful and interesting" (S007); "when you advertise you're talking to one consumer one woman or one man" (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S007, S008, S005)

### 11. Helpful information and the editorial look *(OoA, Creative Council)*
- **What it is:** Ads that look and read like editorial matter, and that teach something useful, get read more than ads that look like ads.
- **Parts:** Service content (how to use, how to choose, how to remove a stain), editorial typography, a real headline, no "addy" look.
- **When to use:** Content ads, advertorial landing pages, carousel posts, product page sections.
- **How to score:** Would a magazine editor or a respected creator publish this as content? Yes = 8 to 10; it screams "ad" = 1 to 4.
- **Quote:** "On average, helpful information is read by 75 per cent more people than copy which deals only with the product." (S015); "Study the graphics used by editors and imitate them." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S008, S015, S028)

## Picture and layout

### 12. Layout and typography rules *(OoA, Creative Council)*
- **What it is:** Readers scan picture, then headline, then caption, then copy; arrange the page in that order and remove anything that slows reading.
- **Parts:** Picture at the top, headline under it; a caption under every picture; serif body type, upper and lower case; never reverse (white on black) body copy; subheads in long copy; drop initial; space between paragraphs; no clutter at the bottom; judge the layout at real size in the real medium.
- **When to use:** Product page design, static ads, landing pages, carousels.
- **How to score:** One point each for: reading order correct, captions present, readable type, no reverse body text, subheads, nothing decorative that blocks reading, checked on a phone at real size, logo visible, one focal image, clean bottom. Max 10.
- **Quote:** "never set body copy in reverse white type on black background" (S007); "Never approve a layout until you have seen it pasted into the magazine or newspaper for which it is destined." (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S005, S007, S026, S029, S013)

### 13. A first-class ticket *(Confessions)*
- **What it is:** Good taste, applied quietly, raises perceived value; an ugly layout suggests an ugly product.
- **Parts:** Quality photography, restraint, consistent look, no cheap gimmicks.
- **When to use:** Premium positioning, gifting, any product that will be worn, eaten, or put on the skin.
- **How to score:** Put the ad next to the category's most respected brand. Holds its own = 8 to 10; looks like a discount flyer = 1 to 3.
- **Quote:** "An ugly layout suggests an ugly product." (S001); "it pays to give most products an image of quality a first class ticket" (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S007, S013, S029)

## Video

### 14. Commercial technique scorecard *(Confessions, OoA, Touffou film)*
- **What it is:** His research list of what tends to sell on screen.
- **Parts:** Open with a dramatic visual ("open with the fire"); pass the sound-off test; name the brand early and often; demonstration; problem-solution; slice of life with a doubter converted; news; testimonials; long-running characters; close-ups for food; eccentric casting. Weak: jingles, celebrities, visual cliches, concealing the brand.
- **When to use:** Every video ad, UGC edit, and Snap or TikTok cut.
- **How to score:** +2 visual hook in the first frame, +2 works with sound off, +2 brand named and shown, +2 demonstration or problem-solution, +2 real testimony or news. Minus 2 for a sung pitch, a celebrity, or a cliche shot.
- **Quote:** "If you are advertising a fire extinguisher, open with the fire." (S001); "Try running your commercial with the sound turned off; if it doesn't tell without sound, it is useless." (S001); "next to dullness the most common crime in advertising is to conceal the name of your product" (S007)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S007, S009, S014, S026, S005)

## Proof and honesty

### 15. The family test *(Confessions)*
- **What it is:** Never run an ad you would not want your own family to read or see. Lies get found out, by the regulator or by the buyer who never buys again.
- **Parts:** Is every claim true and provable? Would it embarrass you in front of your family? Does it offend anyone's beliefs?
- **When to use:** Every claim, review, before-and-after, urgency message, and image.
- **How to score:** Pass or fail first. A fail (a false claim, a fake review, invented urgency) is 0 no matter how well it sells. Then score 1 to 10 on how much proof backs each claim.
- **Quote:** "Never write an advertisement which you wouldn't want your own family to read." (S001); "If you tell lies about your product you will be found out" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S005, S007, S012, S013, S017)

### 16. Testimonials from real people, not celebrities *(OoA)*
- **What it is:** Testimonials work; celebrity endorsements are remembered but do not persuade, because viewers know the star was paid.
- **Parts:** Real customers or experts with a specific story; a recognizable recurring character can work over years; avoid paid fame.
- **When to use:** Choosing faces for UGC, influencer, and review ads.
- **How to score:** Real buyer with specific result = 8 to 10; anonymous stock model = 3 to 4; paid celebrity with no product link = 1 to 2.
- **Quote:** "testimonials also work well" (S007); "The celebrity commercials sell us celebrities, but not the product." (S005)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S005, S007, S009, S014, S017, S026)

## Brand

### 17. Brand image as a long-term investment *(1955 talk, Confessions)*
- **What it is:** Every ad adds to or subtracts from one complex symbol, the brand's personality. When products are similar, the image decides.
- **Parts:** Write a one-line definition of the image; check every ad, deal, price, and package against it; refuse short-term tactics that cheapen it.
- **When to use:** Deciding between a hard-sell discount ad and a brand-building ad; launching sub-lines; agency handovers.
- **How to score:** Does this creative fit the written image definition? Fits and strengthens = 8 to 10; neutral = 5; borrows a cheap look that contradicts it = 1 to 3.
- **Quote:** "Every advertisement is part of the long-term investment in the personality of the brand." (S002); "The greater the similarity between products, the less part reason really plays in brand selection." (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S008, S016, S031)

### 18. Stick with it: the moving parade *(Confessions, 1936 credo)*
- **What it is:** New buyers enter the market every day, so a winning campaign keeps working long after the team is bored of it.
- **Parts:** Change because results fall, never because the client is tired; refresh executions inside the same idea.
- **When to use:** "Creative fatigue" requests, new marketing managers, annual rebrands.
- **How to score:** Is there data that the idea stopped working (falling response at stable spend)? If not, the change scores 1 to 3.
- **Quote:** "You aren't advertising to a standing army; you are advertising to a moving parade." (S001); "Apparent monotony of treatment must be tolerated, because only the manufacturer reads all his own advertisements." (S018)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S008, S013, S018)

## Testing and measurement

### 19. Test everything, keep testing *(Confessions, Touffou film)*
- **What it is:** Treat every element as a hypothesis: premise, promise, headline, picture, size, media, frequency, spend.
- **Parts:** Split-run headlines; test the promise first; back winners and drop losers.
- **When to use:** Always; especially before scaling spend on a new creative.
- **How to score:** Was this creative chosen by a test or by taste? Tested against at least one rival version = 7 to 10; untested = max 5.
- **Quote:** "The most important word in the vocabulary of advertising is TEST." (S001); "Back your winners, and abandon your losers." (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S004, S007, S013, S015, S016)

### 20. Direct response as the standard *(We Sell or Else)*
- **What it is:** Direct marketers know to the dollar what sells; judge all advertising by that discipline.
- **Parts:** Measurable response, benefit headlines, long copy, a clear call to action; distrust "creativity" that cannot be measured.
- **When to use:** Any argument between "brand people" and "performance people"; hiring copywriters.
- **How to score:** Can we attribute sales to this creative (code, link, holdout)? Yes = 7 to 10; no measurement at all = max 4.
- **Quote:** "direct response was my first love and later it became my secret weapon" (S006); "Nobody should be allowed to create advertising for press or broadcast until he has served his apprenticeship in direct response." (S022)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S007, S022, S026)

### 21. Research for illumination, judgment for image *(1955 talk, filmed conversation)*
- **What it is:** Use research to find facts and the strongest promise; do not use it to prove you were right, and do not expect it to predict long-term brand effects.
- **Parts:** Read the research before the brief; challenge it when it supports what you already wanted; for brand image decisions, use experienced judgment.
- **When to use:** When a deck cites "data" to justify a creative choice made earlier.
- **How to score:** Did the data change any decision? Yes = 7 to 10; it only decorated a decision = 1 to 4.
- **Quote:** "Advertising people who ignore research are as dangerous as generals who ignore decodes of enemy signals." (S014); "they use research as a drunk man uses a lamppost - for support, rather than illumination" (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S005, S007, S008, S014, S024)

## Process

### 22. The copywriter's routine: 20 headlines, sleep, edit *(1955 letter, 1982 memo)*
- **What it is:** His own method: research pile, problem statement, many headlines, a draft, then ruthless editing the next morning.
- **Parts:** Write 20 headlines; throw away the first attempts; read aloud the next day; get a colleague to improve it; short words, short sentences, no jargon.
- **When to use:** Writing any ad, product page, or memo to the team.
- **How to score:** How many headline options were written? Under 5 = 1 to 3; 10 = 6; 20 or more and edited the next day = 9 to 10.
- **Quote:** "I try to write 20 alternative headlines for every advertisement." (S004); "I am a lousy copywriter, but I am a good editor." (S004); "Use short words, short sentences and short paragraphs." (S003)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S009, S018)

### 23. Sell, do not entertain: the Demosthenes test *(Confessions, OoA)*
- **What it is:** The aim is "let us march", not "how well he speaks". An ad that draws praise for itself has failed if it does not sell.
- **Parts:** Ask buyers what they will do, not whether they liked it; distrust award-bait and originality for its own sake.
- **When to use:** Reviewing clever, funny, or award-oriented creative.
- **How to score:** After viewing, does the viewer name the product and a reason to buy? Both = 8 to 10; only the joke = 1 to 3.
- **Quote:** "It is the professional duty of the advertising agent to conceal his artifice." (S001); "The purpose of a commercial is not to entertain the viewer but to sell him." (S001); "they worship at the altar of creativity which really means originality the most dangerous word in the Lexicon of advertising" (S006)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S006, S014, S016, S017, S027, S011)
