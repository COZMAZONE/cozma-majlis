# Books: David Ogilvy

No book text was available in full; each entry is built from his agency's tagged quotation collection (S001), his own talks and films, and compilations that name the book. Framework numbers point to `frameworks.md`.

## Confessions of an Advertising Man (1963)

- **Year:** Written on summer vacation in 1962, published 1963; he expected 4,000 copies and it sold over a million (S012). Reissued with a 1988 introduction.
- **Core thesis (our words):** Advertising is salesmanship in print. Good products, honestly advertised with facts, tested, and built on a consistent brand image, sell; vanity, committees, jingles, and award-chasing do not. Half the book is about running an agency and keeping clients.
- **Key frameworks:** headline rules (#7), facts not adjectives (#8), first-class ticket (#13), commercial scorecard: open with the fire, sound-off test (#14), the family test (#15), stick with it / moving parade (#18), test everything (#19), the Demosthenes test (#23).
- **Taught in:** S001, S012, S013, S017, S035; he says he wrote it to win clients, prepare a share offering, and make himself known (S012).

## Blood, Brains and Beer (1978)

- **Year:** 1978, his autobiography (S023, S031); later revised as An Autobiography (1997).
- **Core thesis (our words):** The life behind the method: chef in Paris, Aga salesman, Gallup researcher, wartime intelligence, Amish farmer, and late-starting agency founder at 38.
- **Key frameworks:** the origin stories behind research first (#1, #21) and direct response (#20).
- **Taught in:** No direct text in the corpus. The same life story is told in his own film The View From Touffou (S007) and the filmed conversation (S008). Cite those, not the book.

## Ogilvy on Advertising (1983)

- **Year:** 1983; he promoted it on Letterman (S009).
- **Core thesis (our words):** What the agency's research says works, chapter by chapter: big ideas, positioning, brand image, headlines, long copy, layout, TV techniques, direct response, research. Advertising is "a medium of information", and "creative" that does not sell is not creative.
- **Key frameworks:** positioning (#2), Big Idea test (#4), product as hero (#6), helpful information (#11), layout rules (#12), real testimony over celebrities (#16), research for illumination (#21).
- **Taught in:** S001, S014, S015, S017, S019, S020, S021, S024, S026, S027, S028; the same material on film in S007 and S010.

## The Unpublished David Ogilvy (1986; reissued 2012)

- **Year:** Compiled by his partners for his 75th birthday (1986), later published.
- **Core thesis (our words):** His memos, letters, and early writings, including the 1935 Aga cooker sales manual, the 1955 letter on his writing habits, and the 1982 "How to Write" memo.
- **Key frameworks:** the copywriter's routine (#22), do your homework (#1), do the opposite of competitors (positions #32).
- **Taught in:** S003 (memo), S004 (letter), S018 (Aga manual and 1936 credo lines).

## Other key texts

- **The Image of the Brand (1955 talk):** the brand image speech (#17, #18). Taught in S002; he credits Gardner and Levy's Harvard Business Review article (S002, S008).
- **How to Create Advertising That Sells (1971 house ad):** 2,651 words of his rules as an ad for the agency (S007); lines tagged in S001 (#3, #4).
- **Principles of Management and Creative Council reports, "Magic Lanterns":** internal teaching material on layouts, TV, and management (S001, S008).

## Books and people he credits

- Claude Hopkins (Scientific Advertising): first his model, then partly rejected for short-term mail-order thinking (S002, S008).
- Harold Rudolph, Attention and Interest Factors in Advertising: the story appeal research behind Hathaway (S007, S008).
- George Gallup, Raymond Rubicam, John Caples: whose brains he picked (S007, S008).
- Roman and Raphaelson's book on writing: required reading in the 1982 memo (S003).
