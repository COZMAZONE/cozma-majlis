# Frameworks: Donald Miller

10 frameworks, grouped by job (the story, the words, the funnel, the business). Book tags show where each comes from. Quotes are verbatim from the sources; book structures that are not in a fetched source are paraphrased. Each has an e-commerce note for a Saudi store on Salla or Zid.

---

## The story

### 1. SB7 (the StoryBrand Framework) *(Building a StoryBrand)*
- **What it is:** Nearly every story follows the same seven beats. A brand's message should invite the customer into that story as the hero.
- **Parts:** (1) A character (the customer) who wants something, (2) has a problem (with a villain behind it), (3) meets a guide (the brand), (4) who gives them a plan, (5) and calls them to action, (6) that helps them avoid failure, (7) and ends in success.
- **When to use:** Writing a homepage, PDP, landing page, ad script, WhatsApp sequence, or launch message.
- **How to score:** Check the page for each of the seven. Missing want, missing stakes, or a missing call to action are the most common gaps. A page where the brand is the hero (our story, our factory, our awards first) scores low.
- **Quote:** "Here is nearly every story you see or hear in a nutshell: A CHARACTER who wants something encounters a PROBLEM before they can get it. At the peak of their despair, a GUIDE steps into their lives, gives them a PLAN, and CALLS THEM TO ACTION." (S001)
- **E-commerce note:** Hero: the woman who wants firmer, glowing skin before an occasion. Problem: tired skin and wasted money on products that did not work. Guide: the store that understands and shows proof. Plan: 3 steps (pick the bundle, use daily, see results by week X). CTA: one "اطلبي الآن" button. Failure: another wasted month. Success: confident in photos.
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S004

### 2. Hero and guide *(Building a StoryBrand; interviews)*
- **What it is:** Every person sees themselves as the hero. Brands that position themselves as the hero compete with the customer for attention; brands that play the guide get trusted.
- **Parts:** The guide shows empathy (we understand how this feels) and authority (testimonials, numbers, credentials, logos). He describes four characters in every story: victim, villain, hero, guide (S003).
- **When to use:** Reviewing "about us" heavy pages, founder-centric ads, or copy that leads with the company.
- **How to score:** Count sentences about "we" versus "you". Check that empathy and authority both appear above the fold.
- **Quote:** "every hero needs a guide" (S003)
- **Quote:** "People trust those who understand them, and they trust brands that understand them too." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S004

### 3. Three levels of problem *(Building a StoryBrand)*
- **What it is:** Companies sell solutions to external problems; people buy solutions to internal problems. A philosophical problem (why this is wrong) adds meaning.
- **Parts:** External (the physical problem), internal (how it makes the customer feel), philosophical (why it should not be this way). Behind them a villain the brand fights. (S007 show notes and the book; paraphrased.)
- **When to use:** When copy talks only about ingredients and specs.
- **How to score:** Does the copy name the feeling (frustration, self-doubt, embarrassment) and not only the symptom?
- **Quote:** "By talking about the problems our customers face, we deepen their interest in everything we offer." (S001)
- **E-commerce note:** External: joint pain or dull skin. Internal: feeling older than she is, avoiding photos. Philosophical: she should not have to spend a fortune at the clinic to feel like herself.
- **Sources:** S001, S007

### 4. Stakes: failure and success *(Building a StoryBrand)*
- **What it is:** A story without stakes is boring. Show what is lost by not acting and paint the life after.
- **Parts:** Negative stakes (cost of inaction), positive vision (the transformation). He uses it in sales follow-up: step four of his follow-up email is to paint the negative stakes (S002).
- **Quote:** "Simply put, we must show people the cost of not doing business with us." (S001)
- **Quote:** "Then step four is actually to paint the negative stakes." (S002)
- **E-commerce note:** Keep stakes honest and kind. No shame, no medical scare claims; name a real cost (another month of trying random products, money spent at the pharmacy).
- **Sources:** S001, S002

## The words

### 5. The grunt test *(Building a StoryBrand)*
- **What it is:** Within five seconds of seeing your website or ad, a customer must be able to answer three questions.
- **Parts:** What do you offer? How will it make my life better? What do I need to do to buy it?
- **When to use:** Every page, ad, and first WhatsApp message.
- **How to score:** Show the asset for five seconds to someone outside the business. Each unanswered question costs a third of the score.
- **Quote:** "1. What do you offer? 2. How will it make my life better? 3. What do I need to do to buy it?" (S001)
- **Quote:** "That has to be crystal clear within seconds of interacting with your brand." (S003)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

### 6. Soundbites and the one-liner *(Building a StoryBrand, Marketing Made Simple, StoryBrand 2.0)*
- **What it is:** Filter the message into a few short, repeatable soundbites tied to the customer's survival, and use them in the same words everywhere. The one-liner states problem, solution, result in a sentence or two (structure paraphrased from Marketing Made Simple).
- **Parts:** One-liner; a few soundbites (StoryBrand now teaches a "Soundbite Strategy", S006); repetition across site, emails, scripts, and packaging.
- **When to use:** When the store has many clever taglines and none stick; when every surface says something different.
- **How to score:** Can the merchant and three staff say the same one-liner from memory? Does it name the problem first?
- **Quote:** "You need short, simple, memorable, and repeatable sound bites that you can use over and over in the same wording to get people to understand how you can help them survive." (S005)
- **Quote:** "But the idea is short, simple soundbites that associate with your, with your customer's survival. And if you don't do that, you will not grow a business." (S004)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006

### 7. Own a problem *(interviews)*
- **What it is:** Be known for solving one problem and talk about it endlessly. The bigger the problem, the more valuable you are.
- **Quote:** "once you identify what problem you solve, you need to talk about it endlessly because the bigger the problem is, the more valuable you are because you're somebody who solves problems." (S004)
- **Quote:** "Well, if your tagline is trust is the commodity we exchange, I have no idea what problem you solve." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S004

## The funnel

### 8. Marketing Made Simple sales funnel *(Marketing Made Simple, paraphrased)*
- **What it is:** A simple checklist funnel: a one-liner, a wireframed website (header with clear offer and a "buy now" button, stakes, value, guide, plan, explanatory paragraph, a video, price choices, junk drawer), a lead generator (PDF, quiz, guide) that collects contact details, a nurture email sequence, and a sales email sequence.
- **When to use:** Building or auditing the full path from cold traffic to purchase to follow-up.
- **How to score:** Count which of the five pieces exist and whether each repeats the same soundbites.
- **E-commerce note:** Lead generator: "دليل الكولاجين: كيف تختارين وتشوفين النتيجة في 8 أسابيع" in exchange for WhatsApp consent. Nurture: weekly helpful messages. Sales sequence: a short series that names the problem, the plan, the stakes, a testimonial, and a direct call to action.
- **Sources:** books (paraphrased); S002 (his follow-up email formula)

### 9. Direct call to action *(Building a StoryBrand)*
- **What it is:** Customers do not act unless challenged to. Use one obvious direct CTA ("Buy now") and, for not-ready buyers, a transitional CTA (a free guide or sample).
- **Quote:** "CUSTOMERS DO NOT TAKE ACTION UNLESS THEY ARE CHALLENGED TO TAKE ACTION." (S001)
- **Quote:** "Never assume people understand how your brand can change their lives. Tell them." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

## The business

### 10. The small-business airplane *(How to Grow Your Small Business)*
- **What it is:** A business is a plane: leadership is the cockpit, marketing and sales are the two engines, products are the wings, overhead is the body, cash flow is the fuel. Keep the six in proportion.
- **When to use:** When a messaging fix is being asked to solve a product, margin, or cash problem.
- **Quote:** "Your products are your wings of the airplane." (S002)
- **Quote:** "the fuel tanks of your airplane are your cash flow." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002
