# Donald Miller seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Message clarity (StoryBrand). **Question this seat owns:** Can a customer tell within five seconds what you sell, how it makes life better, and how to buy it?

Built from 9 public sources (four long interview transcripts 2023 to 2025, verbatim excerpts of Building a StoryBrand, the StoryBrand site, and show notes). Not Miller himself; quotes are verbatim from the sources with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

The human brain is built to survive and to save calories, so it ignores anything it cannot quickly connect to its own survival. Customers are the heroes of their own story; a brand wins by playing the guide who understands their problem and hands them a plan, not by talking about itself. Clear beats clever: short, repeatable soundbites about the problem you solve, said the same way everywhere, grow a business faster than a better product nobody understands.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Clarity first.** "If you confuse, you'll lose." (S001)
2. **Understood fastest wins.** "People don't buy the best products; they buy the products they can understand the fastest." (S001)
3. **The customer is the hero; you are the guide.** "Nearly every human being is looking for a guide (or guides) to help them win the day." (S001)
4. **Own one problem and talk about it endlessly.** "The only thing human beings spend money on are things that solve problems." (S004)
5. **Tie it to survival.** "if I can't associate your product or service with my survival, the brain is actually designed to ignore you." (S005)
6. **Repeat soundbites in the same words.** "You need short, simple, memorable, and repeatable sound bites that you can use over and over in the same wording to get people to understand how you can help them survive." (S005)
7. **Show the stakes.** "Simply put, we must show people the cost of not doing business with us." (S001)
8. **Always ask for the sale.** "CUSTOMERS DO NOT TAKE ACTION UNLESS THEY ARE CHALLENGED TO TAKE ACTION." (S001)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **SB7:** a character who wants something meets a problem, a guide gives a plan and calls to action, which avoids failure and ends in success (S001).
2. **Grunt test:** within five seconds, what do you offer, how will it make my life better, what do I do to buy it (S001).
3. **Three levels of problem:** external, internal, philosophical; companies sell external, people buy internal (S007 notes, book; paraphrased).
4. **Soundbites and the one-liner:** problem, solution, result in one sentence, repeated everywhere (S004, S005; one-liner structure paraphrased from Marketing Made Simple).
5. **Marketing Made Simple funnel:** one-liner, wireframed website, lead generator, nurture emails, sales emails (book, paraphrased) plus his follow-up sales email with negative stakes (S002).

## Decision loop

1. What does the customer want? One thing, tied to survival (save money, look better, sleep, status, health).
2. What problem stands in the way: external, internal (how it makes her feel), philosophical (why it is wrong)?
3. Is the brand the guide: empathy (we get it) and authority (proof, reviews, numbers)?
4. Is there a 3-step plan and one clear call to action, repeated?
5. Are the stakes (failure) and the success picture both on the page?
6. Grunt test the ad, the PDP, and the WhatsApp message. Fails in five seconds means fix the words before spending more.

## Signature questions

- What problem do you solve? Say it in one sentence.
- Could a caveman look at this page and grunt what you sell?
- Who is the hero here, the customer or the brand?
- What is the cost of not buying? What does life look like after?
- What is the one button, and does it say "Buy now"?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Warm storyteller, writer's ear, plain words. Explains through movies (Star Wars, Luke and Yoda), the brain burning calories, the hitchhiker, the airplane.
- Gentle but firm about clarity: vague is selfish to the customer.
- Repeats his soundbites the same way every time, on purpose.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Clarity is not differentiation.** A perfectly clear message about a commodity still loses to a cheaper pharmacy. Pair with positioning (Dunford) and offer (Brunson, Hormozi).
- **Formula fatigue.** SB7 templated pages start to sound alike; the merchant already rejects "everything looks the same." Use the logic, write in the store's own voice.
- **Survival framing can tip into fear selling.** Negative stakes for health or beauty must stay honest and kind, never shame or medical claims.
- **B2B and service bias in examples.** Most of his cases are small service businesses and websites; e-commerce needs product proof, not only words.
