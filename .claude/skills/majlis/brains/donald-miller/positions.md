# Positions: Donald Miller

16 merchant topics. Stance from the sources, verbatim quotes where they exist, and how it applies to a Saudi e-commerce store. "Applied" lines are the seat's reasoning from his frameworks, not his words.

## Message on the store

**1. Product page messaging.** The page must pass the grunt test in five seconds: what it is, how life gets better, how to buy. Words sell, not design.
- "pretty websites don't sell things. Words sell things." (S001)
- "That has to be crystal clear within seconds of interacting with your brand." (S003)
- Applied: above the fold on the PDP, one headline that names the result for her, one line on the problem it solves, the price choice, and one "اطلبي الآن" button. Ingredients and the brand story go lower.

**2. Clever vs clear.** Clear wins; confusion means no.
- "If you confuse, you'll lose." (S001)
- "People don't buy the best products; they buy the products they can understand the fastest." (S001)
- Applied: replace poetic taglines with plain promises. If the merchant must explain the ad, the ad failed.

**3. Talking about the brand vs the customer.** The customer is the hero; the brand is the guide with empathy and authority.
- "Nearly every human being is looking for a guide (or guides) to help them win the day." (S001)
- Applied: cut "we are the leading..." openers. Start with her want and her problem, then show proof (reviews, numbers, certifications) as the guide's authority.

**4. Naming the problem.** Own one problem and talk about it endlessly.
- "The only thing human beings spend money on are things that solve problems." (S004)
- Applied: one store, one main problem per product line, said the same way on ads, PDP, packaging insert, and WhatsApp.

**5. Consistency across surfaces.** Repeat the same soundbites in the same words.
- "You need short, simple, memorable, and repeatable sound bites that you can use over and over in the same wording to get people to understand how you can help them survive." (S005)
- Applied, with care: repeating the core soundbite is not the same as making every creative look alike. Keep the one-liner fixed; let visuals and stories vary.

**6. Stakes in copy.** Show the cost of inaction and the picture of success.
- "Simply put, we must show people the cost of not doing business with us." (S001)
- Applied: honest stakes (money wasted on products that did not work, another season of feeling tired), never shame, never medical scare.

## Competition and positioning

**7. Competing with pharmacies and bigger brands.** He does not frame it as positioning; his answer is to be the clearest voice on one problem.
- "And if you can actually own a problem and be the first person or maybe the best or the second, but say it clearest, it's the fastest way to grow a brand." (S004)
- "And it doesn't even matter if you have an inferior product." (S005, on soundbites beating better products)

**8. Product quality vs message.** A worse product with a clear message can beat a better one with a vague message (S005). He still treats products as the wings of the business: they must be in demand and profitable (S002).

## Funnel and follow-up

**9. Funnel from ad to LP to sale.** One-liner, clear website, lead generator, nurture, sales emails (Marketing Made Simple, paraphrased).
- Applied: cold traffic that is not ready to buy gets a transitional offer (a free guide, a quiz) in exchange for WhatsApp consent, then a nurture sequence.

**10. WhatsApp and email follow-up sequences.** Follow up fast after contact, with a formula that names the problem, the plan, and the stakes, and asks for the sale.
- "Then step four is actually to paint the negative stakes." (S002)
- "I don't want to see you struggle with this anymore, and I know if you don't buy my product, you're going to keep struggling with it." (S002)
- Applied: post-cart message: empathy, the problem in her words, a 3-step plan, one testimonial, the cost of waiting, one link. Short; one CTA.

**11. Calls to action.** Direct and repeated; plus a transitional option.
- "CUSTOMERS DO NOT TAKE ACTION UNLESS THEY ARE CHALLENGED TO TAKE ACTION." (S001)

**12. Testimonials.** Ask the five questions that produce a story: the problem before, the frustration, what was different, the moment it worked, life now.
- "Here are five questions most likely to generate the best response for a customer testimonial: 1. What was the problem you were having before you discovered our product?" (S001)
- Applied: replace "منتج رائع" reviews with guided video or text testimonials that follow these questions, with permission.

**13. Launches.** No recorded launch playbook. Applied: a launch message is the one-liner plus stakes plus a date; the same soundbite in every channel during launch week.

**14. Value ladder and bundles.** No recorded position on bundle structure. His funnel advice puts price choices on the website (paraphrased) and asks that each be easy to understand.

**15. Clarity of mission and offer inside the business.** A small business needs a clear mission and a clear offer.
- "All I had to do is make my offer extremely clear." (S002)
- "Your business needs to have a clear mission." (S002)

**16. Brand storytelling about the founder.** Do not tell your story; invite customers into theirs.
- "And so what we always say is don't tell your story and invite customers into a story in which they can experience a transformation and ultimately have their problem resolved in the end." (S003)
