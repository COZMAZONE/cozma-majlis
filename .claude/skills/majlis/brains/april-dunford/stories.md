# Stories: April Dunford

6 stories she reuses in the sources. Figures are as she tells them.

1. **The "Access killer" that became an embeddable mobile database.** Her first product-marketing job: a SQL database positioned as a "Microsoft Access killer" sold about 200 copies. Calling 100 buyers, 94 barely knew they had it and 6 had transformed their business using it on laptops and mobile devices in the field. Repositioned as an embeddable database for mobile devices, it took off and was acquired. Lesson: the few customers who love you show the real positioning. (S001)

2. **Janna vs Siebel.** Positioned as enterprise CRM, a tiny company kept getting "how are you better than Siebel?", a giant 2,000 times bigger. Reframed around the segment where its differentiators mattered (investment banks, with Goldman Sachs as a customer), it stopped losing head-to-head and won its niche. Lesson: choose the frame and segment where you are the obvious leader. (S001, S006)

3. **"How are you better than Oracle?"** A fast-analysis database kept being compared to Oracle on everything Oracle did. Repositioned as a business intelligence tool, its differentiator moved to the center and the trap disappeared. Lesson: the category sets the comparison. (S002, S005)

4. **Joshua Bell in the subway.** A world-famous violinist played in a Washington subway station and almost nobody stopped. Without the context of a concert hall, exceptional value went unnoticed. Lesson: context tells people how to value what they see. (S006)

5. **Cake as dessert or breakfast.** The same cake framed as a dinner dessert competes with pie and ice cream for fancy diners; framed as breakfast it competes with donuts and danishes. Lesson: the frame changes competitors, buyers, and roadmap. (S005)

6. **The dashboard gizmo.** A pitch-contest product could be described as a safety device or as a way to get fewer tickets; each answer changes the market and the company's future. Lesson: decide what the product is, deliberately. (S015)
