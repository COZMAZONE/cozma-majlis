# Positions: April Dunford

15 merchant topics. Her stance from the sources, verbatim quotes, and how it applies to a Saudi e-commerce store. "Applied" lines are the seat's reasoning from her method, not her words. Her own examples are B2B software; the transfer to consumer e-commerce is the seat's.

## Competition

**1. Positioning against pharmacies and big competitors.** Do not fight the giant head-on in its own frame unless you can beat it at its own game. Find the frame where your strength is obvious.
- "You are unlikely to out-Coke Coke but you might still be able to be the best virtual reality headset on the market if you hurry." (S005)
- "Startups will often place their products within a frame that fails to highlight their strengths and often puts their competitors at an advantage." (S005)
- Applied: if the pharmacy brand is trusted and cheaper, a "collagen supplement" frame loses. Pick the segment and frame where the store's real differences (format, taste, program, follow-up, proof) are decisive.

**2. Who the competition really is.** Competitive alternatives, starting with the status quo, and only shortlist rivals.
- "To win business, the first competitive alternative we need to beat is the status quo." (S004)
- Applied: for many buyers the alternative is doing nothing or keeping the half-used jar. Copy must beat "I'll manage without it" first.

**3. Matching competitor price or features.** Not recorded as a price stance; her view is that the frame sets price expectations, so change the frame rather than chase the leader's price.
- "Good positioning sets off a set of assumptions about my product that are true." (S001)

**4. Customers comparing you to the wrong product.** That is a positioning failure, not a sales failure.
- "Customers will decide what you are, using the clues they can most easily assemble." (S003)

## Message and pages

**5. Product page messaging.** Lead with differentiated value against the real alternatives, for the best-fit customer. Do not list every feature equally.
- "If we're showing everything like all these features are equal, we're not really homing in on what makes us different and special." (S013)
- Applied: the first screen of the PDP should answer "why this instead of the pharmacy option" for her, with proof, before the ingredient list.

**6. Taglines and positioning statements.** Neither is positioning.
- "Positioning is not equivalent to messaging." (S001)
- "I believe this exercise is not only pointless but potentially dangerous." (S001)

**7. Storytelling and StoryBrand-style copy.** Fine and useful, but only after positioning is clear.
- "But starting with storytelling without working on positioning first is dangerous." (S009)

**8. Brand claims and skepticism.** Buyers research and distrust what brands say about themselves; proof and third-party evidence carry the claim.
- "We've been lied to and we're skeptical of what brands have to say about themselves." (S011)

## Targeting and launches

**9. Target audience.** Narrow to the customers who love your unique value most; go broad later.
- "In my experiences the smaller and tighter you can get on your segmentation, the easier it is to get early traction. You can always go broader later." (S007)

**10. Launch plans.** Decide the frame and the best-fit segment before the launch creative. In a new category, the launch must educate the problem first; in an established one, it must show why you beat the alternatives.
- "For new markets the first job of marketing is to educate your target segment that there is a problem to be solved in the first place." (S008)
- Applied: a new product line in a known category (collagen, serum) launches as "better for this specific buyer than X", not as "a new revolution".

**11. Repositioning a struggling product.** Talk to customers. The few who love it reveal the real value and the real frame.
- "In the end, I talked to 100 customers. 94 didn't even know they had it." (S001)
- Applied: before killing a slow SKU, interview the buyers who reordered it; their reason may be the positioning.

## Selling and follow-up

**12. Helping buyers decide.** Buyers fear a bad choice; reduce risk by teaching the options honestly.
- "They're worried about making a bad choice" (S013)
- "They want help choosing between alternatives." (S013)
- Applied: a comparison section on the LP (pharmacy option vs clinic vs ours, honest), and WhatsApp replies that help her choose the right bundle, even if that is the smaller one.

**13. Opening a sales conversation.** Start with your insight about the market, not a generic problem every competitor also claims.
- "Starting with my particular insight in the market is a much better way of starting that conversation because it's all about my point of view and it's particular to me." (S013)

**14. WhatsApp follow-up sequences.** No recorded position on messaging cadence. Applied: each message should reinforce one differentiated value against the alternative she was considering, not repeat discounts.

**15. Value ladders and bundles.** No recorded position. Applied: bundle and price design follow the frame; a premium frame cannot carry constant discounting.
