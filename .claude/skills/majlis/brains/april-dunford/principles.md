# Principles: April Dunford

12 decision rules, clustered across sources. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## What positioning is

**1. Positioning is context that triggers assumptions; make the true ones fire.**
- "Good positioning sets off a set of assumptions about my product that are true." (S001)
- "Every offering is presented to potential customers within a context or a frame of reference, whether we set one deliberately or not." (S006)
- Sources: S001, S003, S006, S015. Confidence: high.

**2. Positioning is not messaging, a tagline, or a fill-in-the-blank statement.**
- "Positioning is not equivalent to messaging." (S001)
- Sources: S001, S002, S011. Confidence: high.

**3. If you don't position, customers will do it for you, badly.**
- "Customers will decide what you are, using the clues they can most easily assemble." (S003)
- Sources: S003, S002. Confidence: medium.

## Method

**4. Start with competitive alternatives, then attributes, value, customers, category.**
- "We start with competitive alternatives, or what would customers do if our solution didn't exist." (S001)
- Sources: S001, S004. Confidence: medium.

**5. The status quo is the first competitor.**
- "To win business, the first competitive alternative we need to beat is the status quo." (S004)
- "You need to position yourself as clearly superior to the status quo if you want to convince customers to act." (S004)
- Sources: S004, S013. Confidence: medium.

**6. Differentiation only counts relative to the alternatives, and only as value.**
- "Great positioning is a process of deeply understanding what sets you apart from the other solutions in the market, the value that differentiator brings to customers, and which customers care the most about that value." (S006)
- Sources: S001, S006. Confidence: medium.

**7. Pick the frame where your strengths are obvious; a winner in one category loses in another.**
- "A winner in one category is a loser in another" (S003)
- Sources: S003, S005, S006, S015. Confidence: high.

**8. Target who loves your unique stuff most; narrow first.**
- "What are the characteristics of prospects that love my unique stuff the most?" (S007)
- Sources: S007, S001. Confidence: medium.

## Selling

**9. Positioning before story.**
- "But starting with storytelling without working on positioning first is dangerous." (S009)
- Sources: S009, S010. Confidence: medium.

**10. Pitch customers on today's alternatives, not on your future vision.**
- "For customers on the other hand, you need to position what you have today as uniquely valuable and better than the current set of alternatives customers might choose." (S010)
- Sources: S010. Confidence: low.

**11. Guide the buyer through the market; fear of a bad choice causes no decision.**
- "And so we as vendors, I think need to adopt the attitude of we're really here to guide the customer." (S013)
- Sources: S013. Confidence: low.

**12. Customers, not the office, reveal the real positioning.**
- "6 had transformed their business with it." (S001)
- Sources: S001, S002. Confidence: medium.
