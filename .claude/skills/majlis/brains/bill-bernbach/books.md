# Books: Bill Bernbach

Bernbach wrote no book. His thinking survives in a few primary texts and two posthumous compilations. No book text is reproduced here; entries describe the works and point to the frameworks they carry.

## The 1947 letter to Grey Advertising (May 15, 1947)

- **What it is:** A one-page memo to the owners of Grey, written when he was 35 and creative director. Reprinted in "Bill Bernbach's Book" and in Shaun Usher's Letters of Note: Volume 2 (2016) (S001, S002, S016).
- **Core thesis (our words):** Growth tempts an agency to hire technicians with formulas; persuasion is an art, and the agency must be a distinctive personality that proves good taste and good writing can sell.
- **Key frameworks:** #1 persuasion is an art, #3 rules are what the artist breaks, #19 distinctive personality. See `frameworks.md`.
- **Taught in:** S001, S002, S010, S016, S017, S019, S036, S037, S039, S045.

## "Facts Are Not Enough" (speech to the 4A's annual meeting, 1980)

- **What it is:** His fullest surviving argument, available as a complete text (S003; listing S049; excerpt S043).
- **Core thesis (our words):** Facts and knowledge do not persuade on their own; insight into unchanging human instincts, expressed with fresh artistry, does. Execution becomes content, and the sharpest tool left is how you say it.
- **Key frameworks:** #2 fresh idea test, #5 execution becomes content, #6 feel it in the gut, #17 research as springboard, #21 mass media shapes society.
- **Taught in:** S003, S043, S049.

## Bill Bernbach Said... (DDB, 1989)

- **What it is:** A small compilation of his aphorisms published by the agency after his death; most widely circulated Bernbach lines trace back to it (S005 traces the principle line there before finding the 1971 original).
- **Core thesis (our words):** Short, quotable rules on art over formula, truth, the product, attention and courage.
- **Key frameworks:** #3, #4, #8, #10, #13, #14, #16, #18, #20.
- **Taught in:** via quote compilations S007, S011, S012, S013, S014, S015 (attributed, mostly undated).

## Bill Bernbach's Book: A History of the Advertising That Changed the History of Advertising (Bob Levenson, Villard, 1987)

- **What it is:** A large illustrated history by his longtime DDB colleague, published with Evelyn Bernbach, with a chapter per campaign (Levy's, Volkswagen, Ohrbach's and more) and his letters (S033, S019).
- **Core thesis (our words):** DDB's style (clean, simple layouts, straight copy, honest and funny) changed the history of advertising; it carries his credo that we must sell what we believe in (S033).
- **Key frameworks:** #7 writer and art director team, #12 candour play, #14 product as hero, #19 distinctive personality.
- **Taught in:** S002 (letter excerpted from it), S033 (1987 review).

## Related, not his

- **Doris Willens, Nobody's Perfect: Bill Bernbach and the Golden Age of Advertising (2009):** a critical biography by DDB's former PR head; useful as a check on the myth (S027, S028, S029).
- **Frank Rowsome, Think Small: The Story of Those Volkswagen Ads (1970)** and other campaign histories (S020).
