# Frameworks: Bill Bernbach

21 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (idea, execution, truth, product, team and process, judging). Bernbach never wrote a method book, so these are reconstructed from his own words (the 1947 letter, the 1980 "Facts Are Not Enough" speech, interviews) and from the campaigns his agency made. "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that carry the idea. Tags in italics show the work it is most tied to: *(1947 letter)*, *(Facts Are Not Enough, 1980)*, *(Bill Bernbach Said..., 1989)*, *(Bill Bernbach's Book, 1987)*. Quote-compilation lines are attributed and undated; prefer the letter and the speech where they overlap.

---

## Idea

### 1. Persuasion is an art, not a science *(1947 letter)*
- **What it is:** The founding stance. Rules about readership, sentence length and layout are technique; persuasion comes from inspired, original work, and technique only polishes it.
- **Parts:** (1) Technicians who "know all the rules" versus creative people who do inspiring things. (2) Technique is useful but secondary: "Superior technical skill will make a good ad better." (S001) (3) The danger is mistaking technical skill for creative ability, and hiring people with a formula.
- **When to use:** When a team defends a creative because it "follows best practice" (hook in 1.5 seconds, captions, UGC format) but nobody can say what the idea is.
- **How to score:** 1 to 10 on "inspired versus routinised". 1 to 3: a template filled in; could be any brand. 4 to 6: competent technique around a familiar claim. 7 to 10: a recognisable idea that technique serves. Technique cannot lift a score above 6 on its own.
- **Quote:** "Advertising is fundamentally persuasion and persuasion happens to be not a science, but an art." (S001)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S002, S003, S008, S010, S011, S016, S017, S019, S036, S037, S045)

### 2. The fresh idea test (escape the category sameness) *(Facts Are Not Enough, 1980)*
- **What it is:** If every competitor has the same research fact, saying that fact plainly makes you invisible. The job is to say the true thing in a way the category has never heard.
- **Parts:** (1) List what every competitor says. (2) Keep the true benefit, change the expression. His proof: Clairol Nice 'n Easy kept the "look natural" benefit but said it as a girl saying "It Lets Me Be Me", and sales doubled over seven years with no change in product, price or distribution (S003). (3) Re-check over time: what was fresh becomes cliche.
- **When to use:** Every new ad, hook, headline or PDP hero. Especially in crowded categories (collagen, skincare, supplements) where every brand says "glowing skin" and "premium ingredients".
- **How to score:** Put the line next to five competitor lines with logos removed. 1 to 3: interchangeable. 4 to 6: different words, same thought. 7 to 10: you can tell whose ad it is without the logo, and the claim is still true.
- **Quote:** "In communications familiarity breeds apathy." (S003)
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S003, S007, S010, S011, S012, S014, S035, S039, S045)

### 3. Rules are what the artist breaks *(Bill Bernbach Said..., 1989)*
- **What it is:** Conventions exist to be broken when breaking them makes the product stand out; following them guarantees you look like everyone.
- **Parts:** (1) Name the category convention (colour, model shot, glossy illustration, long feature list). (2) Ask whether breaking it serves the product truth. Think Small used black and white, a tiny car, lots of white space, a sans-serif face and a full stop after the headline when every car ad was colour, big and glamorous (S020, S027). (3) Break one thing on purpose, not everything at random.
- **When to use:** When the creative looks like the category leader's, or when a reviewer says "that's not how ads in this space look".
- **How to score:** Count deliberate, product-serving rule breaks (0 = 2/10, one strong break = 7/10). Random weirdness with no product reason scores low; he called that graphic acrobatics (#9).
- **Quote:** "Rules are what the artist breaks; the memorable never emerged from a formula." (S007)
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S008, S010, S012, S013, S014, S024, S025)

### 4. Principles endure, formulas don't
- **What it is:** Separate the permanent principle (you must get attention, you must be believed) from the changing technique (how you get attention this year).
- **Parts:** (1) Permanent: attention, truth, human insight, the product. (2) Temporary: styles, formats, trends. (3) Any formula, once copied, loses the shock that made it work.
- **When to use:** When a team wants to copy a competitor's winning ad format, or reuse last quarter's winner unchanged.
- **How to score:** Ask "what principle does this format serve?" If the answer is only "it worked for them", score 3 or below.
- **Quote:** "Working from a method or a formula is guaranteed to do the same thing to the effectiveness of an idea that time does to a loaf of bread." (S010)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S010, S012, S013, S038, S014)

## Execution

### 5. Execution becomes content *(Facts Are Not Enough, 1980)*
- **What it is:** Refuse the split between what you say and how you say it. The how is part of the what: it decides whether anyone looks, listens and believes.
- **Parts:** (1) Find what to say (research and product truth). (2) Then the harder job: say it so people look and listen. (3) Test by substitution: would the Lemon ad work as "The greatest inspection system in the world"? Would Churchill's line work as "We owe a lot to the R.A.F."? (S003)
- **When to use:** When a reviewer approves "the message" and waves the craft through, or when a team ships a correct but flat ad.
- **How to score:** Rewrite the line in the plainest possible form. If the plain version loses nothing, the execution scores 3 or below. If the plain version is clearly weaker and less believable, 7 or above.
- **Quote:** "Great execution becomes content. And it brings what you have to say to the eyes and ears of your audience believably, persuasively." (S003)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S012, S013, S019, S039, S043)

### 6. Feel it in the gut: facts are not enough *(Facts Are Not Enough, 1980)*
- **What it is:** People act on feeling. Facts persuade only when wrapped in an expression that makes people feel something; on their own they can even block persuasion.
- **Parts:** (1) The receiver's attitude matters more than the facts in the message. (2) Find the unchanging instinct (to survive, be admired, succeed, love, care for one's own; S029). (3) Wrap the fact in artistry: Rachel Carson's facts were known; her lyricism moved a country (S003).
- **When to use:** Supplement and beauty ads that list ingredients and milligrams but never make the viewer feel the before and after.
- **How to score:** After viewing, can a tester name a feeling (relief, pride, being seen) and the product? Feeling plus product = 8 to 10. Fact recall only = 4 to 5. Neither = 1 to 3.
- **Quote:** "You can say the right thing about a product and nobody will listen. You've got to say it in such a way that people will feel it in their gut." (S012)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S012, S013, S014, S029, S043, S045)

### 7. The art director and copywriter as one team
- **What it is:** Words and picture are conceived together by a two-person team, so the ad is one idea, not copy with decoration. DDB put writers and art directors in the same room; before that they sat in separate departments (S019, S023).
- **Parts:** (1) One writer, one art director, one brief. (2) The headline and image must need each other (Lemon: the word only works next to a flawless-looking car). (3) The creative head edits hard and protects the idea.
- **When to use:** Structuring a creative process: designer and copywriter (or the merchant and one designer) brief and concept together, not in sequence.
- **How to score:** Cover the image, read the headline; cover the headline, look at the image. If either alone tells the whole story, the pairing scores 5 or below. If together they make a meaning neither makes alone, 8 or above.
- **Quote:** "Adapt your techniques to an idea, not an idea to your techniques." (S007)
- **Sources:** 9 (S007, S008, S019, S022, S023, S026, S036, S039, S044)

### 8. Simplicity: tear away the unrelated
- **What it is:** Cut everything that does not serve the one product message, including your own cleverness.
- **Parts:** (1) One theme. (2) Remove "weeds that are smothering the product message". (3) Edit to the half line: he would ask for copy "a half-line shorter" (S008). (4) Do not put in everything you want to put in (the "REGARDS" telegram story, S003).
- **When to use:** Busy statics, PDP heroes with five badges, videos with three messages.
- **How to score:** Count messages competing for attention. One = 9 to 10; two = 6; three or more = 3 or below.
- **Quote:** "Our job is to simplify, to tear away the unrelated, to pluck out the weeds that are smothering the product message." (S011)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S008, S011, S013, S014, S020, S024, S039)

### 9. Disciplined imagination, not acrobatics
- **What it is:** Creativity is imagination harnessed to the product theme. Wildness for its own sake is not creative.
- **Parts:** Every word, line, light and shadow must make the chosen product advantage more vivid, believable and persuasive (S027). "The heart of creativity is discipline." (S012)
- **When to use:** When a concept is striking but the product could be swapped for any other.
- **How to score:** Swap test: replace the product with a competitor's. If the ad still works, score 3 or below.
- **Quote:** "Merely to let your imagination run riot, to dream unrelated dreams, to indulge in graphic acrobatics and verbal gymnastics is not being creative." (S027)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S012, S013, S027)

### 10. Dull or irrelevant: the two ways to fail
- **What it is:** A two-sided gate. Dull work is not seen; brilliant work that is irrelevant to the product does not sell.
- **Parts:** (1) Attention gate: is it noticed? (2) Relevance gate: is the brilliance about the product and the buyer? Looking polished is not the same as being looked at (S011).
- **When to use:** Quick triage of any creative batch.
- **How to score:** Two scores, attention and relevance, each 1 to 10. The creative's score is the lower of the two.
- **Quote:** "dullness won't sell your product, but neither will irrelevant brilliance" (S012)
- **Sources:** 6 (S011, S012, S013, S014, S015, S038)

## Truth and belief

### 11. Interesting, listened to, understood, believed (the truth chain)
- **What it is:** Truth only counts when it is believed, and belief needs attention first, which needs freshness.
- **Parts:** Interesting, so they listen; listened to, so they know what you say; known, so they can believe; believed, so it becomes true for them (S005).
- **When to use:** When a team says "but it's true" about a flat ad, or "but it's creative" about an unclear one.
- **How to score:** Score each link 1 to 10; the chain scores its weakest link.
- **Quote:** "The truth isn't the truth until people believe you" (S005)
- **Sources:** 5 (S005, S013, S015, S011, S003)

### 12. The candour play: admit the flaw
- **What it is:** Saying something unflattering about yourself makes the rest believable and makes you stand out from boasting competitors.
- **Parts:** (1) Find the honest weakness (small and ugly car; No. 2 in rentals). (2) Turn it into proof of a strength (strict inspection; trying harder). (3) Keep the tone warm and witty, not apologetic. Lemon called one car a reject over a blemished chrome strip "Inspector Kurt Kroner" caught (S021).
- **When to use:** A challenger brand, a premium price, a product with an obvious objection (taste, size, price, wait time).
- **How to score:** Does the ad name a real weakness and convert it into a believable strength? Both = 8 to 10. Weakness named but not converted = 4. No candour = not applicable, score on other frameworks.
- **Quote:** "Nobody's perfect and no one's going to believe you if you claim to be." (S005)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S005, S007, S010, S020, S021, S022, S027, S048)

### 13. The truth as the most powerful element
- **What it is:** Truth is not only ethical; it is the strongest persuasive material, and false claims collapse.
- **Parts:** (1) Only claims the product can back. (2) No invented advantages or gimmicks. (3) Belief is the product of truth plus freshness (#11).
- **When to use:** Every claim on supplements and beauty: "results", "clinically", "No. 1", reviews.
- **How to score:** Any claim the merchant cannot prove = automatic fail (1/10), regardless of creativity.
- **Quote:** "No matter how skillful you are, you can't invent a product advantage that doesn't exist." (S011)
- **Sources:** 8 (S009, S011, S013, S014, S023, S035, S037, S039)

## Product

### 14. The product is the hero
- **What it is:** The idea must come out of the product. Cleverness that outshines the product is a failure.
- **Parts:** (1) "The magic is in the product" (S009). (2) Kill the cleverness that makes the agency shine (S011). (3) The test of a great ad is the product being remembered, not the ad.
- **When to use:** Reviewing funny or cinematic ads where viewers remember the joke and not the brand.
- **How to score:** Ask testers what was advertised and why to buy it. Correct product and reason = 8 to 10; product only = 5; joke only = 2.
- **Quote:** "Our job is to kill the cleverness that makes us shine instead of the product." (S011)
- **Sources:** 7 (S009, S011, S013, S014, S026, S035, S038)

### 15. Live with the product until one theme emerges
- **What it is:** Immersion before ideas. Know the product through and through, then crystallise one single theme.
- **Parts:** (1) Use it, look at it, understand it, love it. (2) Find the simple story in it (S012). (3) Write one sentence: what must be conveyed. Bernbach himself watched the Wolfsburg inspector reject a car for a scratch invisible to the naked eye (S003), the seed of Lemon.
- **When to use:** Before any brief. For a Salla store: taste the product, read every review, visit the warehouse, watch customers open the box.
- **How to score:** Can the team state one product truth that only this product can claim, with a source (lab sheet, review, process)? Yes = 8 or above.
- **Quote:** "Find the simple story in the product, and present it in an articulate and intelligent, persuasive way." (S012)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S012, S013, S014, S038)

### 16. Bad product, faster failure
- **What it is:** Great advertising accelerates the truth about a product. If the product disappoints, better ads only spread the disappointment.
- **Parts:** DDB refused products that could not live up to their advertising (S024). Gablinger's beer: a strong 1967 campaign drove trial, and most people did not come back (S006).
- **When to use:** Before scaling spend on a product with weak reviews, high returns or poor repeat.
- **How to score:** Repeat rate and review sentiment first. A weak product caps the creative score at 4 no matter how good the ad is.
- **Quote:** "A great ad campaign will make a bad product fail faster. It will get more people to know it's bad." (S011)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S007, S011, S012, S013, S024)

## Team, process and judging

### 17. Research as springboard, not as idea generator
- **What it is:** Use research and marketing facts to aim; do not ask research to produce the idea or to veto it on averages.
- **Parts:** (1) A creative needs "a springboard of information" (S003). (2) Research describes the past and cannot produce an idea (S014). (3) Pre-test scores can mislead: Avis tested badly, half the people disliked it, and he ran it because the other half loved it (S007).
- **When to use:** When a team wants a survey or AI tool to "tell us the angle", or wants to kill a polarising concept on an average score.
- **How to score:** Was research used to find the problem and the product truth (good) or to choose between creative ideas by average liking (risky)? Polarising with a strong positive half beats bland approval.
- **Quote:** "We don't ask research to do what it was never meant to do, and that is to get an idea." (S014)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S007, S010, S012, S013, S014, S040, S043)

### 18. Stand for something (polarisation is acceptable)
- **What it is:** Work that stands for something attracts some people and repels others; work that stands for nothing moves nobody.
- **Parts:** (1) Choose who the ad is for. (2) Accept that some will dislike it. (3) Measure the strength of the "for" half, not the average.
- **When to use:** When feedback says "some people won't like it".
- **How to score:** Does a clear segment love it? Then disapproval elsewhere is not a penalty.
- **Quote:** "If you stand for something, you will always find some people for you and some against you." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S012, S013, S014)

### 19. The distinctive personality
- **What it is:** Every ad, person or product needs its own personality, or it will not be noticed. Bernbach demanded it of the agency itself in 1947.
- **Parts:** (1) A consistent tone across executions (VW's ten teams kept one voice, S008). (2) Different from the category. (3) Recognisable without the logo.
- **When to use:** Brand look and voice decisions; checking that a new batch does not drift into category generic.
- **How to score:** Logo-cover test across five recent creatives: do they look like one brand with a personality? 1 to 10.
- **Quote:** "If we are to advance we must emerge as a distinctive personality." (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S007, S008, S038, S035)

### 20. The principle that costs money
- **What it is:** A principle only counts when you keep it at a price. DDB refused cigarette accounts after the 1964 Surgeon General's report although that work was the most profitable kind (S004).
- **Parts:** (1) Write the red lines. (2) Keep them when a test shows breaking them would sell more. (3) Do not grandstand about it; he said "We were never on a crusade" (S004).
- **When to use:** Fake urgency, fake reviews, borderline medical claims, non-halal or culturally offensive imagery, competitor bashing.
- **How to score:** Pass or fail. Any red-line breach fails the creative, regardless of predicted ROAS.
- **Quote:** "But I don't feel a principle is a principle until it costs you money." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S005, S010, S012, S013, S046)

### 21. Mass media shapes society
- **What it is:** Persuasion skill is morally neutral; the communicator decides whether to vulgarise, brutalise, or lift the audience.
- **Parts:** (1) Respect the audience's intelligence. (2) Good causes still need skilled communication (S003). (3) Choose to lift.
- **When to use:** Tone decisions: body-shaming hooks, fear appeals, mocking customers.
- **How to score:** Does the ad respect the viewer? Insult or fear for its own sake = 2 or below.
- **Quote:** "All of us who professionally use the mass media are the shapers of society." (S003)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S012, S013, S015)
