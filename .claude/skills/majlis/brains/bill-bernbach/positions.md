# Positions: Bill Bernbach

34 topics, framed for a Saudi or Gulf DTC merchant on Salla or Zid (beauty, supplements, a collagen product). Each has his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and year where known, and an applied note. He died in 1982, so anything about social media, UGC or A/B platforms is inferred from his principles and marked as inferred, with no direct source. Lines from quote compilations (S011 to S015) are undated. Dated anchors: the 1947 letter (S001), the 1965 Wall Street Journal and Herald Tribune interviews (S006), the 1971 Los Angeles Times interview (S004), the 1980 4A's speech (S003).

## Ideas and attention

**1. Hooks and the first seconds.** The first job is to be noticed; if the opening is the category default, the rest is wasted. But the hook must come out of the product, not a random shock.
- "If your advertising goes unnoticed, everything else is academic." (S011)
- "nothing fails like something they already know about" (S003, 1980)
- Applied: a "Did you know collagen..." hook used by ten competitors fails his test. A first frame that shows the product truth in a way no one else shows it passes.

**2. Headlines.** The headline and picture must make one idea together; a single surprising word can carry a whole campaign (Lemon). Plain claims ("the greatest inspection system") get ignored.
- "Suppose the Volkswagen ad, positioned to tell its inspection story, had said" a superlative instead of one shocking word; he answers that nobody would have noticed it (paraphrase of S003, 1980).
- "But in one stroke, we drew attention to the fact that this was a great inspection system." (S003, 1980)

**3. Long versus short copy.** No recorded rule on length. He cut hard ("Make it a half-line shorter.", S008) but VW ads carried full, candid body copy under a short headline. His test is whether every word serves the one theme.
- "Sometimes it's better not to put everything into an ad you want to put in." (S003, 1980)
- (inferred, no direct source) For a PDP: short, fresh headline; honest, specific body copy; cut every badge that does not support the one story.

**4. The big idea versus a stream of variants.** One strong idea does more than many weak ads.
- "Properly practiced creativity can make one ad do the work of ten." (S007)
- "Nobody counts the number of ads you run; they just remember the impression you make." (S011)

**5. Humour.** In favour, when it is warm, intelligent and about the product. DDB's Levy's posters were called "the commercial with a sensayuma" by Walter Winchell (S031); Alka-Seltzer's "spicy meatball" spot (1969) built a whole joke around indigestion (S010). Humour that steals attention from the product breaks his rule 19.
- "Our job is to kill the cleverness that makes us shine instead of the product." (S011)
- (inferred, no direct source) For Gulf audiences: light, respectful self-irony about the product is in his spirit; jokes about the customer's body, religion or family are not.

**6. Emotion versus reason.** Emotion decides; reason rationalises. Facts must be wrapped in artistry that makes people feel.
- "And only feeling leads to action." (S003, 1980)
- "Because an appeal makes logical sense is no guarantee that it will work." (S011)

**7. Repetition and frequency.** Freshness beats repetition: familiarity breeds apathy, and any phrase becomes a cliche.
- "In communications familiarity breeds apathy." (S003, 1980)
- "The most freshly sculptured phrase will eventually become a cliche." (S003, 1980)
- Note: VW kept one consistent voice across many executions (S008). Keep the idea and tone; refresh the executions.

**8. Creative fatigue.** Expect every winner to wear out, because its power came from originality.
- "a subtle, ever-changing art, defying formularization, flowering on freshness and withering on imitation" (S010)
- (inferred, no direct source) Plan new executions of the same idea before frequency climbs; do not just re-cut the same hook.

## Truth, claims and people

**9. Claims and honesty.** Truth is the most powerful element; never invent a product advantage. For a collagen supplement this means: only claims the merchant can back, no medical promises, no fake numbers.
- "The most powerful element in advertising is the truth." (S011)
- "No matter how skillful you are, you can't invent a product advantage that doesn't exist." (S011)

**10. Admitting flaws.** Strongly for. Candour about a weakness makes the strength believable (Lemon, Think Small, Avis No. 2).
- "Nobody's perfect and no one's going to believe you if you claim to be." (S005)
- Applied: "it tastes like medicine for the first week" or "results take eight weeks, not eight days" can be stronger than another "visible results fast".

**11. UGC and testimonials.** No recorded position (UGC did not exist). (inferred, no direct source) He would welcome real customers when they carry a real insight, and reject template testimonials that all sound alike, since sameness is invisible and fake praise breaks the truth rule.
- Nearest anchor: "In advertising not to be different is virtually suicidal." (S011)

**12. Real people versus models and celebrities.** DDB favoured real-looking, specific people: Levy's posters used a choirboy, a policeman and a Penobscot railroad engineer biting rye bread (S030). DDB also used stars (Laurence Olivier for Polaroid, S008) when they served the idea.
- "What we wanted to do was enlarge its public acceptance." (Judy Protas, DDB copywriter, on Levy's, S031)
- (inferred) Real people where belief matters; a famous face only if the face is the idea, not decoration.

**13. Fake urgency and manipulation.** No recorded position on countdown timers. (inferred, no direct source) A trick that works by deceiving fails his principle test even when it lifts conversion.
- "But I don't feel a principle is a principle until it costs you money." (S004, 1971)

**14. Ethics and red lines.** DDB refused cigarette accounts after 1964, knowing it cost money, and said so without moralising.
- "We were never on a crusade" (S004, 1971)
- "It was a matter of principle and it cost us money" (S004, 1971)
- Applied: halal checks, no medical claims, no fake reviews are principles; keep them when a test says breaking them would sell.

## Brand, price and design

**15. Brand versus performance.** He rejected the split ("hard sell" versus "soft sell"); every ad must sell and every ad builds the brand's personality.
- "Forget words like 'hard sell' and 'soft sell.' That will only confuse you." (S011)
- "The purpose of advertising is to sell." (S005)

**16. Premium positioning and price perception.** No recorded position on price levels. His Ohrbach's work made bargain shopping chic: "high fashion at low prices" (S007). (inferred, no direct source) Premium is earned by taste, restraint and truth in the execution, not by saying "luxury".
- "Don't confuse good taste with the absence of taste." (S012)
- "Let us prove to the world that good taste, good art, and good writing can be good selling." (S001, 1947)

**17. Discounts and price promotions.** No recorded position. (inferred, no direct source) He would not object to a real offer, but would demand it be presented with an idea and a personality, not as another red "50% OFF" tile identical to every store's.
- Nearest anchor: "lift your claims out of the swamp of sameness" (S011)

**18. Design and layout.** Design is persuasion. Think Small's white space, small car, sans-serif type and black and white page broke every car-ad convention (S020); Polaroid ads cut words and enlarged pictures (S026).
- "Is creativity some obscure, esoteric art form? Not on your life." (S007)
- "Just because your ad looks good is no insurance that it will get looked at." (S011)

**19. Packaging and naming.** No recorded position. (inferred, no direct source) Same tests: distinctive personality, a simple story, truth on the label.
- Nearest anchor: "If we are to advance we must emerge as a distinctive personality." (S001, 1947)

**20. Landing pages and product pages.** No recorded position. (inferred, no direct source) One theme above the fold, a fresh expression of the true benefit, candid detail below, and nothing that smothers the product message.
- "Our job is to simplify, to tear away the unrelated, to pluck out the weeds that are smothering the product message." (S011)

**21. Video ads.** DDB made story-led TV: VW "Funeral" (1969), Alka-Seltzer "Mamma mia", Polaroid "Visit to Grandpa" (S008, S021). The product reveal could come last if the story led to it (S010).
- "Is a picture of a man crying just a picture of a man crying?" (S003, 1980)
- He answered that it can be shot so the viewer cries; that talent "touches and moves people and makes things happen" (S003).

## Research, testing and measurement

**22. Research before creative.** Use research to find the problem and the product truth; never to generate the idea.
- "A creative man can't jump from nothing to a great idea. He needs a springboard of information." (S003, 1980)
- "We don't ask research to do what it was never meant to do, and that is to get an idea." (S014)

**23. Testing and A/B.** Respectful but sceptical of pre-testing. He nominated John Caples, the father of tested advertising, for the Hall of Fame, and still credited Caples's creative ability (S003). He ran Avis despite poor test scores (S007).
- "I have a great respect for tested advertising." (S003, 1980)
- "But in the end it was his creative ability that fashioned his most effective ad." (S003, 1980)
- (inferred, no direct source) Today he would test ideas against ideas, not tweak button colours, and would not let an average score kill a polarising winner.

**24. Data and measurement.** Data describes the past; persuasion makes the future. Results still matter: sales are the measure.
- "We are so busy measuring public opinion that we forget we can mold it." (S007)
- "Properly practiced creativity must result in greater sales more economically achieved." (S011)

**25. Awards and "creativity" versus sales.** Creativity is only valuable if it sells; he worried about creativity becoming a fashion.
- "I fear that we may be entering an age of phonies." (S009)
- "It is our belief that every other activity in our business is a prelude" to the ad, and its measure is persuasion (paraphrase of S026).

## Organisation and competition

**26. Agencies versus in-house.** No recorded position on in-house teams. He built an agency around the writer and art director pair and a creative head who edits. (inferred, no direct source) Structure matters less than having a pair who concept together and one person with taste who can say no.
- "You can't do this job if you're not arrogant" (S008)

**27. Hiring creative people.** Talent and decency both.
- "If you're nice, but untalented, we don't need you. If you're talented, but a bastard, we don't need you." (S026)

**28. Competitors.** Do not copy them; use their sameness as your opening. Avis turned being No. 2 to Hertz into the idea (S007, S048).
- "When you're only No. 2, you try harder" (S010)
- "in communications, imitation is commercial suicide" (S010)

**29. Comparative and challenger positioning.** Strongly for honest challenger framing. "We Try Harder" is called "one of the first real challenger brand campaigns" (David Angelo, S048).
- "We're only Number Two. So we try harder." (Avis line found in Helmut Krone's wastebasket, per Willens, S027)

**30. Product quality before ads.** Fix the product first; ads speed up the verdict.
- "A great ad campaign will make a bad product fail faster. It will get more people to know it's bad." (S011)
- DDB would not take a product that could not live up to its advertising (S024).

**31. Respecting the audience.** Treat the consumer as intelligent; mass persuasion is a moral responsibility.
- "All of us who professionally use the mass media are the shapers of society." (S003, 1980)
- Bernbach's work was grounded in the notion "that the public had to be respected" (Ad Age profile, S008).

**32. Culture and identity in ads.** Levy's used ethnic identity warmly to widen a market (S030, S031). (inferred) For Saudi work: use local life with affection and accuracy; never mock a group.
- "You don't have to be Jewish to love Levy's real Jewish Rye." (S032)

**33. Intuition versus logic in decisions.** Intuition, refined by practice, is the practical tool; over-analysis kills ideas.
- "Logic and over-analysis can immobilize and sterilize an idea." (S008)
- "there is nothing more practical to an advertiser than an intuition so refined by practice" (S026)

**34. Sales numbers and bragging in ads.** No recorded position. (inferred, no direct source) Boasting is what the father does about his daughter, and "people don't believe us" (S003). A fresh, believable truth beats a big number.
- "There are so many of us doing that with our products. But people don't believe us." (S003, 1980)
