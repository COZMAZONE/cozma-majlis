# Stories: Dara Denney

14 stories and case studies she tells, most-told first. Figures are as she tells them; client names appear only where she names them. Lesson lines are the seat's summary.

1. **The $9 smoothie hook.** For Daily Harvest she tested four UGC hooks that changed only the comment in a TikTok reply bubble, all pulled from real ad comments. The winner by a landslide was the throw-in: _if you have ever spent $9 on a smoothie._ The product cost about $7, but the exact price anchored a familiar experience. Lesson: specificity and price anchoring stick; test even the line you threw in last. (S001)

2. **_Tastes like a milkshake._** In the same ad she put a line from ad comments into the script: it tastes like a milkshake but is healthy. The brand hated the line; customers echoed it back in the comments. She was not allowed to test it as the hook. Lesson: the customer's words beat the brand's preferred words. (S001)

3. **The five-minute makeup routine.** For Laura Geller, a decent but not top static got a messaging test of five headlines, each borrowed from winning UGC. One creator's voice-over (_I have a meeting in two to three minutes_) became _The 5 Minute Makeup Routine_, which beat the offer headline and a two-minute version. In one telling it spent well over $2M and generated over $6M; in another, a single iteration spent $2M at a 3 ROAS and was still top months later. Lesson: your next winner already sits in your account; extract the big idea and move it, and the most scalable message wins. (S005, S019, S030)

4. **The bone-broth founder.** For Nutra Organics, the winning video had the co-founder say she had tried every bone broth and none tasted like this. Research showed taste was the top objection, so the ad attacked taste head-on; naming her as founder mattered to the story. Hook rate 52%, hold rate 9%. Separately, a long text-heavy static that _no one will read_ became their top sales-period ad. Lesson: answer the real objection, and don't assume what people will or won't read. (S002)

5. **_Colorful sheets,_ not _aesthetic._** For Bed Threads, Reddit research showed buyers said _colorful sheets,_ not influencer language. The account was all down-funnel (testimonials, launches), so the team built a simple top-of-funnel ad from a TikTok format found through the Viral Ads SOP (small accounts with big views). She doubted it at first; it became a winner. Lesson: plain customer language and an open top of funnel beat polish. (S002)

6. **The 30% hold-rate trap.** Auditing a prospect, she saw a one-minute winner with a 30% hold rate, then found losing ads with the same hold rate. Their average watch time was three seconds against eleven: the losers were 3 to 9 seconds long, so a full view counted as a ThruPlay. Lesson: cross-check hold rate with average play time. (S016)

7. **The audience picked wrong.** At Motion's 2026 trends event she showed two scalp-flake ads; nearly everyone picked the shocking _wtf is this_ stitch. The quieter split-screen confession won. Lesson: test, because experts' taste misfires too; analyze content, not just the vibe. (S004)

8. **_No one cares who the founder is._** Her friend Barry Hott tells founders to drop the _I'm the founder of_ intro. She tested it and found the named-founder version performs better, and that putting name and title in text overlay almost always wins. Lesson: test the guru rule. (S014, S022)

9. **Two products, two awareness plays.** For a collagen brand in a crowded US market, the team went solution-aware and showed why it beat competitors; for the same client's lesser-known product, they went up-funnel with education. Lesson: the market's awareness, not habit, sets where the message aims. (S010)

10. **The 20-brand data poll.** Each month she listed the top three creative types across 20 brands she had access to. Over three months more than half were UGC, and 15 of 20 still had images among top performers; big spenders tested messages on images first, then briefed creators. Lesson: formats work together; use cheap formats to find the message. (S001, S020)

11. **The supplement ad that never named the product.** A founder walked through ingredients and the problems each addressed, never naming the brand; it became a top performer and scaled because it reached people not yet shopping. Lesson: education reaches the unaware. (S001)

12. **The million-dollar BFCM weekend (2020).** A client wanted to spend $1M in one weekend. She had no clever system; she scaled the ad sets getting the most purchases at the best price and cut what dropped. Lesson: at speed, follow the performance. (S003)

13. **The hidden personas.** Her 2026 diagnoses: one brand's ads all targeted a single persona while reviews showed two bigger ones; another client converted and retained best with an older segment, so the next quarter went to 40-plus creators. Lesson: compare who you target with who actually buys. (S028)

14. **The hardest year.** Opening the 2025 Creative Strategy Summit, she said 2025 was one of her hardest years in more than ten years of making ads, with real imposter syndrome. What got her out was not AI but returning to basics and taking a breath. Lesson: fundamentals over the newest method. (S002)
