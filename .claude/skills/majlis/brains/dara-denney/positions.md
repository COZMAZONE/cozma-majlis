# Positions: Dara Denney

30 topics a store owner asks about. Each has her stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time where the corpus shows them. Dates are publish or recording dates. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct; the seat should say so and reason from her principles, labelled as reasoning.

## Testing and scaling

**1. How many creatives to test.** Tie volume to budget. Lean accounts start with about 10 creatives and add one or two a week; about 3 to 4 tests a week at $100K a month, one more per extra $25K; big spenders run 10 to 20+ tests a week.
- "for every additional 25 grand that you're spending every month, that's another creative test to add per week is my really rough estimation for that." (S001, 2024-07-10)
- "for Brands and businesses that are on the lower end of this budget Spectrum I really like to limit the ad creative to 10" (S009, 2025-03-28)
- Shift: 2022 cadence was about one new creative a week at $50K a month (S003, Motion outline); 2025 to 2026 she pushes velocity and diversity harder because of Andromeda (S010, S011).

**2. Testing structure.** Lean: one purchase-optimized campaign. Growth and scale: a separate testing campaign with one ad set per concept, winners duplicated into the scaling campaign. Test on broad.
- "I typically recommend $100 to $200 per adset per day" (S009, 2025-03-28)
- "Typically, this is my core starting campaign structure for nearly any e-commerce brand." (S021, 2025-06-13)
- Shift: by late 2025 she says the Meta updates made account structure far less important (S011, 2025-12-21), and she now puts statics for a month into one ad set rather than segmenting (S010).

**3. When to kill an ad.** Let ads run unless they miss the CPA target while taking real spend. At scale, tests run about 7 days or about $1,000 unless an early read is obvious.
- "I wouldn't worry too much about turning ads off unless they really aren't meeting your CPA goal and for some reason the algorithm is spending a lot on them otherwise I would just let them run" (S009, 2025-03-28)
- "Like you see the results, you increase the spend. You see results going down, you decrease the spend." (S003, 2022-10)

**4. When and how fast to scale.** Small budgets: no more than 20% a day. Bigger accounts: follow performance, faster when a winner is obvious. Promote winners into the scaling campaign.
- "when you're first starting off and you have those like under 10k per month budgets, like yeah, don't scale above 20% on any given day." (S003, 2022-10)
- "we really want to see and scale anything above a two when looking at internal metrics" (S017, 2025-01-16), on in-platform ROAS for one brand; a rule of thumb, not a law.

**5. Win rate.** Most creatives fail. About half may hit a "padding" benchmark and run a few weeks; true unicorns come about once a month at high volume.
- "anywhere from 70 to 90% of the creatives and messaging and new angles you're going to test, they're not going to be bangers." (S006, 2024-09)
- "But the real banging unicorn ad ones once a month sometimes." (S001, 2024-07-10)

**6. Iterations versus net-new.** About 90% net-new, 10% iterations. Iterate on proven winners, almost never on losers.
- "I think you should make 90% net new creatives" (S012, 2024-11-14)
- Tension: when a client needs ads fast at onboarding, she starts with "Frankenstein" iterations that combine the best hooks with the best holds (S003, 2022-10), and in 2026 she says to keep iterating a winner "Until it stops working." (S030, 2026-08-26). Net-new for discovery, iteration for proven winners.

**7. Creative fatigue.** Short-lived winners are usually down-funnel ads that have used up a small pool. Fix by moving up-funnel and changing visuals, not by tweaking words.
- "they'll complain about launching an ad creative that does well for a week or two and then falls off a cliff." (S010, 2025-12-18)
- "I actually tend to get a little worried if I see frequency numbers above five" (S017, 2025-01-16)
- "it almost always results in extending the length of how long the creative is performing" (S019, 2024-11-21), on moving a winner's message up-funnel.

## Formats

**8. Static versus video.** Both. Statics are the cheapest way to find the message early, and they can reach cold audiences; video is needed to grow.
- "statics are for retargeting or bottom of funnel audiences only. Total myth." (S026, 2026-06-25)
- "But in order to have any success on it, you're going to have to emerge into video at some point." (S010, 2025-12-18)
- "So it's not that it's an either or." (S001, 2024-07-10)

**9. UGC versus studio production.** Creator content is still the largest share of winners, but generic paid UGC is tired. Studio or high production pays off mainly for founder ads, after low-fi versions prove the idea.
- "You just actually have to make really good content." (S001, 2024-07-10)
- "there's just honestly no need in our industry anymore for mediocre ugc and mediocre creators" (S023, 2023-07-13)
- "I have personally created some high production founder ads that have worked really, really well for brands, and I'd say this is not where you need to start." (S022, 2025-06-24)
- Shift: in 2025 she called problem-solution creator video "probably going to be your safest bet" (S020, 2025-03-20); by August to September 2026 she ranks generic problem-solution UGC C tier and sees fatigue, favoring personal storytelling, authority, and education (S028, S029).

**10. Founder ads.** Her favorite format; often a brand's first winner. Introduce the founder by name; interview rather than script.
- "If I had to choose one ad creative to run on Meta, Tik Tok, YouTube, or even television, I would choose this one every single time." (S022, 2025-06-24)
- "Sometimes I hear other creative strategists say something like, you don't need to introduce yourself as the founder, no one cares, no one gives a shit. Actually, people really, really cared for this brand." (S002, 2025-10)

**11. Creators, influencers, and celebrities.** Work with niche creators whose audience trusts them; follower count matters less than fit and aspiration. Celebrities can work but are often a money pit.
- "ultimately the follower count does not matter." (S020, 2025-03-20)
- "You have to work with creators who your audience aspires to in some way." (S028, 2026-09-28)
- Celebrity ads: C tier in her 2026 ranking (S029, 2026-08-13).

**12. Partnership ads.** The core of a 2026 account: 30 to 50% of spend.
- "I like to see anywhere from 30 to 50% of all ad spend going to this type of format." (S028, 2026-09-28)
- "the Andromeda algorithm is a persona-based algorithm" (S029, 2026-08-13)

**13. Testimonials, press, and proof.** "Golden nugget" reviews (specific, strange, memorable) work; generic testimonial statics and press logos do not, and press ads carry rights risk.
- "Golden nugget reviews are those testimonials from your customers that are just so absurd and memorable and wild that you can't help but just have a chuckle to yourself." (S001, 2024-07-10)
- Paraphrase: she has not seen a plain testimonial static do anything noteworthy in a long time, and ranks press ads F because of permissions (S029, 2026-08-13).
- Shift: she once championed press ads as a credibility stamp, about six years earlier (S029).

**14. Statistics in ads.** Use outcome statistics, not problem statistics.
- "If you instead say, Hey, 97% of these people experience this outcome, that's way more impactful than a statistic about the problem or whatever." (S001, 2024-07-10)
- Shift: by 2026 she ranks statistics ads C tier for direct-to-consumer ROI, better for awareness objectives (S029).

**15. Ugly, lo-fi, and sale-period formats.** Post-it and handwritten ads, founder letters, and long text can crush during sales; less so as evergreen.
- "But where I often see it crush is especially for sales period." (S029, 2026-08-13)
- "this was actually their top performing sales ad." (S002, 2025-10), on a long text-heavy static.

**16. AI-made creative.** Pragmatic user: she makes AI statics and has scaled AI-made "investigation" ads, but ranks AI avatars and AI animation C tier and expects brands to move back toward real creators. AI-written copy comes out bland.
- "this is one that I find people end up not executing well because they're using AI to write all the copy and it just comes out bland." (S029, 2026-08-13)
- "And I actually find that the bigger the brand, the more that they're likely to decide that they're going to work with creators." (S029, 2026-08-13)
- Note for this council: the merchant's red lines (real people only) override her openness to AI avatars and staged content.

## Hooks and copy

**17. Hooks.** Test 3 to 6 very different hooks on every video. Change visuals, not only words. Demographic callouts are her top-performing hook family in 2025.
- "the first 3 seconds of your ad is going to determine whether you're going to make money or burn it" (S015, 2025-02-28)
- "this is honestly the top performing hooks so far in 2025" (S015, 2025-02-28), on demographic hooks.
- "the best hooks show, they don't tell." (S014, 2025-08-28)

**18. Trigger words and shock hooks.** Words like "scam", "hate", and "ruining my life" lift hook rates. She calls apology hooks a cheap trick but reports they work.
- "the word scam is probably one of the best visceral trigger words that you can use in your advertising today." (S014, 2025-08-28)
- Caution for this council: shock and fear hooks must still pass the store's claims, halal, and no-fake-urgency rules.

**19. Captions and editing pace.** Fast cuts help hold; captions tested inconclusive.
- "I try to make each clip less than two seconds long" (S016, 2022-10-04)
- "there's not much conclusive evidence either way" (S019, 2024-11-21), on captions versus no captions.

**20. Copywriting.** Strategists usually write the ad copy because they "speak social"; brand copywriters often do not.
- "traditionally, I do find that creative strategists tend to do a lot of the actual copywriting for ads because they understand the platform the best." (S002, 2025-10)

## Account, funnel, and economics

**21. Retargeting and funnels.** Mostly unnecessary now; broad targeting finds buyers. Awareness, reach, and engagement campaigns rarely add incremental sales below multi-seven-figure spend.
- "retargeting isn't something that I do on most of my brands, even if they're spending 500k to a million dollars per month." (S021, 2025-06-13)
- "these updates have rendered all targeting interests, lookalike, even prospecting versus retargeting pretty much useless." (S011, 2025-12-21)

**22. Many products.** Do not advertise everything. Find the most scalable product or bundle for paid social; add a catalog campaign for the rest.
- "Just because you have 50 products does not mean that you need to advertise 50 products." (S021, 2025-06-13)

**23. Order value and bundles.** A low order value caps what creative can do; bundles are one of her quiet wins.
- "that AOV is actually too low to be scalable on meta ads." (S021, 2025-06-13)

**24. Landing pages.** 2022: matching the ad to the landing page is a myth; test page formats (quiz, listicle or advertorial, overview). 2026: she checks that winning messaging pillars carry through to the landing page.
- "I think that's a really big, um, like myth is that you have to have matching ad creative to matching landing page." (S003, 2022-10)
- Shift: in 2026 she audits whether top messaging buckets carry to the landing page (S028, 2026-09-28).

**25. Attribution tools.** Ads Manager is enough for most; third-party attribution pays off only with several real channels.
- "first party data tools like triple whale and northbeam are really only good for Brands who are starting to scale up quite a bit" (S017, 2025-01-16)

**26. Other countries.** Test creative in the biggest market; localize proven winners for the others instead of testing everywhere.
- "Typically, we will creative test in your biggest market and then transfer those learnings to the other country campaigns." (S021, 2025-06-13)

**27. Brand guidelines versus performance.** Performance creative often breaks brand comfort; push brands outside it, but never into anything damaging.
- "I'm looking at what the masses are going to say and frankly like what I think is going to make you the most money. And a lot of times that goes against brand guidelines, which is a hard pill to swallow sometimes with brands." (S003, 2022-10)

**28. Copying competitors.** Study them monthly for patterns; do not copy them as a strategy. Your own top ads and viral organic content are better sources.
- Paraphrase: she compares copying competitors to looking for a spouse in the wrong place: you may take what is not yours and not right for your customers (S030, 2026-08-26).
- "This is actually something I like to do on a monthly basis so that I can track it for each of our individual competitors." (S004, 2025-12)
