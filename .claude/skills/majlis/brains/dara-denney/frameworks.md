# Frameworks: Dara Denney

16 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (testing, diversity, hooks, creators, analysis, structure). "Sources" counts the sources in this corpus that use the framework. Quotes come from auto captions or published transcripts, so they keep spoken quirks. Each framework ends with **Applied to a Saudi store**: that line is the seat's reasoning, not her words.

---

## Testing

### 1. Budget-tiered creative testing (lean, growth, scale)
- **What it is:** How you test creative depends on spend. One method does not fit a $5K month and a $3M month.
- **Parts:** *Lean* ($5K to $15K a month): one sales campaign optimizing for purchases (she uses Advantage+ Shopping), about 10 creatives, mostly statics, add one or two a week, only turn off ads that miss CPA while taking real spend. *Growth* ($30K to $50K a month): keep that campaign as the scaling campaign and add a manual testing campaign, one ad set per concept, 3 to 6 creatives inside, $100 to $200 per ad set per day, winners get duplicated into the scaling campaign. *Scale* ($1M+ a month): same testing campaign, but 10 to 20+ tests a week, often around $200 a day for about 7 days, sometimes with cost caps (S009). Cadence rule of thumb: about 3 to 4 tests a week at $100K a month, plus one weekly test per extra $25K (S001).
- **When to use:** Setting up or auditing any Meta account; deciding how many creatives to brief this month.
- **How to score:** Does the plan match the budget tier? A small store running a multi-campaign funnel, or testing video before it knows which message converts, scores low.
- **Quote:** "the way that you test ad creative when you're spending $3 million per month is vastly different than when you're just starting off or you have a much lower budget" (S009)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Most Salla or Zid stores sit in the lean or early growth tier. Ten creatives, statics first to find the message, one campaign, and a weekly drip of new tests. Separate testing only when spend can feed it.
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S009, S021, S030)

### 2. The creative test anatomy: one concept, several big-swing variations
- **What it is:** A test is never a single ad. Each concept ships with 3 to 6 variations, and the variations differ a lot, not by one word.
- **Parts:** For video, 3 to 6 hooks that are "fundamentally different": a reaction, a product-first value prop, a problem-first line (S001, S013). For statics, very different headlines or visuals. Uneven spend inside the test is fine; one variation pulling away is a good sign it can scale (S001). Every test needs a stated learning goal (S003).
- **When to use:** Writing any brief or launching any test.
- **How to score:** Count the variations and how different they are. Four near-identical hooks score as one test. No stated hypothesis means the test should not run.
- **Quote:** "Hey, like should we only change one word so that we know the variable? And absolutely not." (S013)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** For each new concept, shoot or write at least three openings: one in the customer's own dialect words from reviews, one that leads with the product, one that leads with the problem.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S009, S012, S013, S015)

### 3. The 90/10 rule: net-new over iterations
- **What it is:** Spend about 90% of tests on net-new creative and 10% on iterations, because iterations are already baked into every test's variations.
- **Parts:** Iterate hard only on true winners ("until it dies") (S012). Almost never iterate on a loser unless a clear execution mistake explains the loss (S019). Ten iteration types, easiest to hardest: messaging swap, visual edit, text-bubble or headline treatment, format change, new creator, green screen over the winning ad, move the message up-funnel, swap the human desire, high-production founder interview, and the rare loser rescue (S019).
- **When to use:** Building the monthly creative roadmap; when the team keeps "optimizing" the same ad.
- **How to score:** What share of this month's tests are genuinely new concepts? Under half is a warning sign of iteration paralysis (#4).
- **Quote:** "you should spend 90% of your tests on net new creatives and only 10% on iterations" (S019)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Before shooting a fifth version of last month's winner, ask whether a new persona, a new desire, or a new format is on the roadmap at all.
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S012, S019, S030)

### 4. Iteration paralysis and the squint test
- **What it is:** The failure mode where every ad is built from the last ad's data, so the account looks like one ad. The diagnostic is to put the top 10 to 20 ads side by side and squint.
- **Parts:** If they share colors, fonts, footage, format, and creator type, you are blending into the feed and limiting yourself to the same narrow buyer (S001, S012, S019). The cure is a few big swings every month (S001).
- **When to use:** Any creative review; whenever results plateau after a run of "safe" tests.
- **How to score:** Squint at the live ads. If you cannot tell them apart at a glance, score creative diversity low regardless of individual metrics.
- **Quote:** "I can always tell a brand is in iteration paralysis when I look at their ad account or I look at the creative they're currently testing and if I can squint and they all kind of look the same" (S001)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Lay every live ad on one screen. If they all use the same product shot and the same caption style, the next test must change the visual world, not the wording.
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S012, S019, S030)

### 5. Prioritization: easiest to make, most evidence, fastest data
- **What it is:** Rank roadmap ideas by how cheap they are to validate, how much evidence supports them, and how quickly they return data. Validate a big idea cheaply (a static, founder content, an in-house creator) before paying for a shoot.
- **Parts:** Two frameworks feed the list: (a) your next winner already lives in your top 20 ads, so extract each one's "big idea" and move it into a new format, often creator content; (b) a viral-content SOP: search TikTok and Reels for your idea and look for small accounts with big views (S002, S030). Some calls are gut, and she says to honor gut as you get more experienced (S030).
- **When to use:** Choosing between creative ideas; deciding whether a production budget is justified.
- **How to score:** Which option gets a real signal fastest and cheapest, and which has the most proof behind it?
- **Quote:** "your next top performing creative already likely exists in your ad account in one way or another" (S030)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Before a studio shoot, test the line as a static and as a phone-shot founder clip. Spend production money only on ideas that already won once.
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S030)

## Creative diversity (Andromeda era)

### 6. Five levels of creative diversity
- **What it is:** A ladder for diagnosing how diverse an account really is.
- **Parts:** *Level 0:* statics or video only. *Level 1, format expansion:* visual diversity through many formats, though few unicorns come from format alone. *Level 2, awareness expansion:* messaging aimed at different awareness stages, usually more up-funnel. *Level 3, desire and motivation expansion:* angles built on core human desires. *Level 4, persona and use-case expansion:* new audience segments with their own reasons to buy. *Level 5, true creative diversity:* all of the above across products, often with a brand-owned organic content engine (S010).
- **When to use:** Account audits; explaining why an ad "works for a week then falls off a cliff."
- **How to score:** Place the account on the ladder. Each level up adds new buyers the algorithm can find.
- **Quote:** "In order for you to have true creative diversity, you need to have visual diversity" (S010)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Many stores stop at level 1 (a few formats, one message). The next lever is usually personas: the same product bought by mothers, brides, gym-goers, or gift buyers for different reasons.
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S004, S010, S011, S012, S028)

### 7. Andromeda as a persona engine
- **What it is:** Her reading of Meta's 2024 to 2025 ad system. Andromeda retrieves a smaller pool of ads per user, GEM narrows it with deeper prediction, and Lattice picks the final ad. In her view these updates make interest targeting, lookalikes, and prospecting versus retargeting nearly useless, and make account structure matter far less (S011). Andromeda also groups retrieved ads into personas, so every creative should be mapped to an intended audience (S002, S004).
- **Parts:** Know your core audience segments; build a diverse range of formats for each; double down on messaging angles and awareness levels where a format wins; treat it as a creative operations and velocity problem, not a volume problem (S011). Results that fell after the update usually mean competitors fed the system better creative and better economics (S011).
- **When to use:** When someone blames "the algorithm" for falling results; when planning creative volume.
- **How to score:** Can you name each live ad's intended persona? Ads that target nobody in particular score low.
- **Quote:** "But what a lot of people don't talk about with Andromeda is that it's also grouping these different ads into different personas." (S004)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Write the persona on every brief ("Najdi mother of three, buys for the family", "young woman preparing for a wedding"). Then check that the creator, the setting, and the words actually match that person.
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S004, S007, S010, S011, S029)

### 8. Awareness levels as an analysis lens, not a starting point
- **What it is:** Five stages (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware). Up-funnel messaging is the most scalable; down-funnel is the most likely to convert (S004, S024).
- **Parts:** Most aware: offer and brand names. Product aware: "3 reasons why", testimonials. Solution aware: us versus them. Problem aware: questions that mirror the buyer's problem. Unaware: education, curiosity, demographic callouts (S024). Ads that die after a week or two are often solution or product aware and have exhausted that small pool (S010). She warns not to start strategy here; start from desires and problems, and use awareness in retrospect (S024).
- **When to use:** Diagnosing short-lived winners; deciding how to make a message scale.
- **How to score:** Map the live ads to stages. If most sit at offer or product aware, the account is bleeding its top of funnel.
- **Quote:** "it is a bad strategy to begin your creative strategy with awareness levels people are not motivated by their awareness they are motivated by other things like their desires their problems" (S024)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** A store living on discount codes and "اطلبه الحين" ads is all most-aware. One up-funnel test a week (education, a problem question, a demographic callout) is how it grows past the warm pool.
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S004, S005, S010, S017, S024, S028)

### 9. Core human desires as messaging diversity
- **What it is:** Use Steven Reiss's 16 basic desires (from his book *Who Am I?*) as a menu of angles. She finds romance, beauty, power, social connection, tranquility, and family recur in winners (S019, S025, S004).
- **Parts:** Four messaging buckets in analysis: human desire, demographic, strategy angle (celebrity, negative marketing, taboo, personal history), and user journey stage (S004).
- **When to use:** When messaging feels repetitive; when choosing the next iteration of a winner.
- **How to score:** How many distinct desires does the account speak to? One or two means narrow reach.
- **Quote:** "each desire represents a core human need like the need for acceptance or curiosity or power" (S025)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Family, hospitality, social approval, and looking good for occasions (Eid, weddings, Ramadan gatherings) are strong desire angles in the Gulf. Romance angles need care with modesty norms.
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S010, S019, S025)

## Hooks

### 10. The hook is a package, not a line
- **What it is:** The hook is everything in the first three seconds: text overlay, sound, visual, and the overall vibe. Changing the visual hook often moves performance more than changing the words (S014). Meta also reads the first seconds to decide who to deliver to (S020).
- **Parts:** Hook types she has tested and named: investment (time or money spent on failed attempts), "I thought this was a scam", give-me-time, POV you hate X, founder introduction, in-action creator hooks, emotional cliffhanger, "why did no one tell me", "if you" callouts (S014); go against the tradition, conversational ("no because..."), viral product, reaction, controversy, trigger words, demographic, multiple-hook reel, "how do I know if", speed and transformation (S015); trigger-word, "why is it important", a day without pain, how-to (know if, pick, start), "this one thing", apology and "you're welcome", Snapchat-filter visual, whisper voice-over (S013); curiosity by not giving away the plot (S002). Drawn from customer reviews and comments, not invented (S001, S014).
- **When to use:** Every video brief and every iteration of a winner.
- **How to score:** Does the opening change at least two of the four layers across variations? Does it select the right person?
- **Quote:** "Your hook is not just the first line of a script." (S013)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Pull hook lines from Arabic reviews and comments as written by customers. Demographic and "if you" callouts work well in Arabic ("إذا عمرك فوق الأربعين..."). Shock or "scam" hooks need a halal and claims check first.
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S013, S014, S015, S016, S020, S022, S029)

### 11. Hook rate, hold rate, and their limits
- **What it is:** Hook rate (3-second views over impressions) and hold rate (ThruPlays over impressions) as custom columns. Getting a good hook is the easy part; holding attention is harder and more telling (S016).
- **Parts:** ThruPlay fires at 15 seconds, or at completion for shorter videos, so short ads inflate hold rate. Cross-check with average play time (S003, S016, S017). Her reference points: good hold rates around 8 to 12% (S016, 2022); an account average of about 30% hook and 7 to 8% hold, with a winner near double (S017); a winning bone-broth ad at 52% hook and 9% hold (S002). Raise hold with cuts under two seconds, multiple angles, agitating the problem, continuing the hook's story, and hook reels (S016).
- **When to use:** Explaining why a video worked or failed; briefing an editor.
- **How to score:** Low hook and low hold: the creative is not landing. High hook and hold with low CTR: possibly the wrong audience or a small one (S020).
- **Quote:** "knowing how to create a good hook is frankly the easy part when it comes to creative strategy" (S016)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** On Snapchat and TikTok, the same logic applies with each platform's own view metrics; judge them against your own account average, not a US benchmark.
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S003, S016, S017, S020, S004)

## Creators and formats

### 12. Creators over "UGC": the modular brief
- **What it is:** Generic paid UGC is played out; work with real creators who already make content in your niche and whom your buyer aspires to (S001, S020, S023, S028). Brief with as much information as possible, plus a 15-minute call with every new creator (S018).
- **Parts:** Ask for modular raw footage: one testimonial plus around 10 B-roll clips (S018), or 20 to 30 B-roll clips for heavier editing (S016). Modules: hook, problem agitator, solution, features and benefits, social proof, call to action (S020). Spell out shooting specs (vertical, good light, no filters, no logos, extra seconds at start and end) (S018). Negotiate for the raws (S018). Find creators on TikTok, often 10K to 100K followers (S001). Leave room for the creator's own flair (S018, S020).
- **When to use:** Every creator brief.
- **How to score:** Does the brief name the persona, give the modules, ask for raws and B-roll, and allow the creator's own voice? Would the creator pass the "sniff test" in the first three seconds (S020)?
- **Quote:** "you don't need UGC. You need the right creators and the right formats to tell stories that they are already passionate about." (S020)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Brief Saudi micro-creators who already post about the category, in their own dialect. Real people only, no paid actors posing as customers, and modest dress and setting that the store's audience respects.
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S016, S018, S020, S023, S028, S029)

### 13. Partnership ads as the core of the account
- **What it is:** Running ads through creators' handles. She wants 30 to 50% of spend there and calls it the S-tier format of 2026 because the creator's own audience seeds the persona (S028, S029).
- **Parts:** Match creators to personas, but by aspiration, not a one-to-one demographic copy (S028). Ask contracted creators for a few low-fi "story-style" statics too (S029). Track monthly rolling reach; partnership ads are how big spenders find new people (S029).
- **When to use:** When reach is flat; when creator content "doesn't work for us."
- **How to score:** Share of spend on partnership ads, and whether the creators are people the persona actually follows.
- **Quote:** "I like to see anywhere from 30 to 50% of all ad spend going to this type of format." (S028)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** The Gulf equivalent is creator whitelisting on Snapchat and TikTok as well as Meta. Watch disclosure rules for paid influencer posts.
- **Sources:** 3 (S014, S028, S029)

### 14. Founder ads
- **What it is:** Her favorite single format: the founder tells why the product exists. People connect to a human more than a brand, and stories are remembered (S022, S029).
- **Parts:** Formats: problem-solution origin story (often a VSL, 35 seconds to 3 minutes), objection handler (price is the common one), product demo, education, sale announcements. Script: personal intro naming the founder, the personal problem with concrete detail, the turning point, the product as solution with 2 or 3 differentiators, proof, soft CTA (S022). Name and "founder" in text overlay usually beats leaving it out (S014). For high-production versions, interview the founder for 30 to 60 minutes instead of scripting (S019, S022).
- **When to use:** A brand with a real founder story, especially early, when it is often the first winner (S029).
- **How to score:** Is the founder's problem specific and true? Does the ad answer a real objection?
- **Quote:** "If I had to choose one ad creative to run on Meta, Tik Tok, YouTube, or even television, I would choose this one every single time." (S022)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** If the owner is comfortable on camera (or the voice alone, if not), a founder clip explaining sourcing, quality, or price builds the trust that cash-on-delivery buyers need. Respect the owner's privacy limits.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S014, S019, S022, S029)

### 15. The format tier list (2026)
- **What it is:** Her August 2026 ranking of 51 formats by how often they become long-running scaling winners.
- **Parts:** S tier: founder content, partnership ads, VSLs. A tier: amateur investigation, yapper (personal storytelling to camera), David and Goliath, grid statics, authority (doctor or expert), green-screen commentary, catalog/DPA. B tier ("supporting cast"): callouts, before and after, progression, us versus them, objection handling, headline ads, educational infographics, creator low-fi statics, comment reply, conversation, mood board. C tier: AI animation, AI avatars, celebrity, warehouse, street interview, ASMR, duet and stitch, generic problem-solution UGC. D to F: AI billboard, breaking news, get-ready-with-me, listicles, text-only, testimonial statics as usually made, press ads, podcast ads, notes-app and UX ads (S029).
- **When to use:** Choosing which formats to fund; spotting "supporting cast" formats that will never be the main winner.
- **How to score:** Does the plan put most effort into formats with a history of scaling, with B-tier formats used to fill the ecosystem?
- **Quote:** "So, I love thinking of these two ads as supporting characters." (S029)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Several high tiers (founder, authority, creator partnership, grid statics for bundles) translate directly. Street interviews she suggests faking, and AI avatars, are off the table under the store's red lines.
- **Sources:** 3 (S026, S028, S029)

## Analysis

### 16. Four-step creative analysis: metrics, content, comparison, feedback
- **What it is:** Her method for judging what worked and why. Metrics are the least important step; content analysis is the most important (S004).
- **Parts:** *Metrics:* primary KPIs (spend, purchases, cost per purchase, ROAS) say what worked and are the only ones to optimize on; storytelling KPIs (frequency, CPM, CTR, hook, hold, play time, shares, comments) hint at why (S004, S017). The ads that work are the ones Meta gives the most spend and results, not the highest CTR (S004). *Content:* format, creator, messaging, imagery, and the persona they add up to (S004). *Comparison:* against your own winners and against competitors, monthly (S004). *Feedback:* ad comments, customer service, sales and CX teams (S004, S020). Analyze before you optimize (S017). Write a one-slide learning only for winners (S002).
- **When to use:** Every test readout and monthly retro.
- **How to score:** A readout that stops at "B won, 1.2 ROAS" is not an analysis. It must say what in the content probably caused the result and what the next test is.
- **Quote:** "And it's actually the metrics that are the least important part of this process." (S004)
- **Applied to a Saudi store:** Judge on the store's own purchase data and cost per order, read Arabic comments and WhatsApp questions as feedback, and note dialect, setting, and creator for every winner.
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S016, S017, S020)
