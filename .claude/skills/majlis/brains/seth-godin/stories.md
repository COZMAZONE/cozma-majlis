# Stories: Seth Godin

9 stories and examples he uses, as told in the sources. Figures are as he tells them.

1. **The cow by the road.** You drive past a field of brown cows and keep driving, because you have seen cows before. A purple cow would make you stop. Lesson: the ordinary is invisible; remarkable gets noticed. (S015)

2. **Sliced bread failed for 15 years.** Sliced bread was available and almost nobody bought it until Wonder figured out how to spread the idea. Lesson: the winner is not the best invention but the idea that spreads. (S015)

3. **The pain reliever he never noticed.** A brand manager spent about $100 million in a year trying to interrupt him with ads and coupons, and he ignored all of it because he always buys the one in the yellow box. Lesson: people with no problem do not listen; interruption buys little. (S015)

4. **The 14-seat restaurant.** One of the three best restaurants in New York has only 14 seats; with the right fans that is enough to build an experience the chef is proud of. Lesson: the smallest viable audience can be the whole business. (S001)

5. **Junior the appliance repairman.** Runs a successful repair business without a truck or office, by earning a reputation in a very specific lane on one website. Lesson: specificity beats scale for a small operator. (S001)

6. **PSAudio.** Does not reach many people, but its focus is precise and deep enough to build one of the largest operations in its industry. Lesson: SVA can lead to scale. (S001)

7. **The crushed straw hats.** He ordered straw hats for a party; the shipper used a plastic bag instead of a box because it was cheaper, the hats arrived crushed, customer service sent an autoreply, and nobody answered the phone, all because it was cheaper. Lesson: the pursuit of cheap destroys the experience that made you worth buying. (S013)

8. **Henry Ford learned people did not want the cheapest car.** After winning on efficiency, Ford found buyers wanted a car they could be proud of, a bit safer and more stylish. Lesson: even the cost leader is chosen for more than price. (S011)

9. **The Daily Candy reader.** A reader complained because his newsletter had not arrived for three days. Lesson: that complaint is what real permission looks like. He also gave away a third of Permission Marketing for an email address and never used those addresses again, because that was not the deal. (S006)
