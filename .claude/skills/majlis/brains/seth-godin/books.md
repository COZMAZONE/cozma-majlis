# Books: Seth Godin

Built only from how he discusses the books in public (blog, talks) and from short passages others quote. No book text is reproduced.

## Permission Marketing (1999)

- **Core thesis:** As attention gets scarce, the best customers ignore interruption. Marketers should earn the privilege of sending anticipated, personal, and relevant messages to people who want them, and keep the promise they made to get it.
- **Key frameworks:** permission definition; real vs presumed permission; the promise as the deal. See frameworks.md #5.
- **Taught in:** S006 (his 2008 post defining it, written nine years after the book).

## Purple Cow (2003)

- **Core thesis:** In a world of too many choices and too little time, people ignore the ordinary. Build remarkable products (worth making a remark about) for the people who care most, and let them spread it. Safe is risky; very good is boring.
- **Key frameworks:** remarkable; otaku (obsessed early customers); ideas that spread vs the TV-industrial complex. See frameworks.md #3, #6.
- **Taught in:** S015 (TED2003 talk, given the same year and built on the same argument).

## Tribes (2008)

- **Core thesis (from public descriptions only):** The internet lets people with a shared idea form groups, and anyone can lead one. No tribe text or quote is verified in this brain; do not quote it.

## This Is Marketing (2018)

- **Core thesis:** Marketing is serving a specific group of people; start with the smallest viable audience, know who it's for and what it's for, and build trust and status for them. Brand marketing needs patience; direct marketing needs measurement.
- **Key frameworks:** smallest viable audience; direct vs brand; the identity idea in the chapter title (people like us do things like this) (S018, unverified fetch). See frameworks.md #1, #7.
- **Taught in:** S001 to S005 (SVA posts), S016 (brand-ads passage), S017 (podcast launch episode, "Price is a story.").
