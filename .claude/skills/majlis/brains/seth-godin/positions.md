# Positions: Seth Godin

16 marketing topics a Saudi or Gulf e-commerce merchant faces. Each has his stance from the sources, verbatim quotes with id and date, and an "Applied to the store" line. The applied line is this seat's inference from his principles, not something he said about Saudi Arabia; never present it as his quote. "No recorded position" means the brain has nothing direct.

## Budget and measurement

**1. Brand vs performance budget split.** He gives no ratio. He insists they are two different games with different yardsticks: measure direct ads and keep what pays; give brand work patience and consistency, or do not buy it at all.
- "Direct marketing is outbound, measured and designed to pay for itself." (S009, 2015-03-31)
- "If you’re buying brand marketing ads, be patient. Refuse to measure. Engage with the culture. Focus, by all means, but mostly, be consistent and patient." (S016, This Is Marketing 2018, quoted by a third party)
- Warning about the web: direct-marketing tactics used as a shortcut can destroy the brand you want (S009).
- Applied to the store: fund performance ads only at a CPA the margin can carry; put brand money only into something you can keep doing for a year (the same idea, the same audience). Do not judge a brand video by 7-day ROAS.

**2. Judging an ad by ROAS.** Valid for direct ads, wrong for brand ads.
- "If you try to measure brand ads, like quarks and other quantum phenomena, the benefits disappear." (S010, 2022-11-10)
- "The very things you would do to make them measure better cause them to be pretty lousy brand ads." (S010)
- Applied: if the merchant wants every ad to show ROAS by Thursday, that is a direct-marketing business; say so and plan accordingly.

## Who to target

**3. Narrow vs broad targeting.** Narrow, deliberately. Pick the smallest audience that can sustain the store and serve it so well it spreads.
- "Stake out the smallest market you can imagine." (S002, 2017-07-12)
- "The thing is, you don’t get to 3% of the market by trying for 40% and failing." (S004, 2025-06-23)
- Concession: if the goal truly is market share, he admits you "need to aim for the middle" and become the normal choice (S004); he just thinks the SVA route gets there more often.
- Applied: before any broad Advantage+ or Snap campaign, name the one group the hero product is for (for example: busy mothers 30 to 45 in Riyadh who already buy collagen) and make the creative, the page, and the offer unmistakably for them. The algorithm can go broad; the message should not.

**4. Who the customer is.** Defined by worldview and what they want to become, not demographics alone. The TED talk targets the people who care obsessively and let them tell the rest (S015). The chapter title (people like us do things like this) (S018, unverified fetch) names the identity idea; use it as a concept, not a quote.

## Discounts and promotions

**5. Discounting, White Friday, salary week.** Against competing on price. Cheaper is a race you might win and still lose.
- "The problem with the race to the bottom is that you might win. Even worse, you might come in second." (S011, 2014-05-30)
- "Anything cheaper than what’s on offer is a waste of the customer’s money, because it won’t get the job done." (S012, 2023-06-08)
- "Cheap is the last refuge for the marketer who can’t figure out how to be better." (S013, 2016-08-25)
- No recorded position on seasonal sales specifically.
- Applied: for White Friday (الجمعة البيضاء), prefer something remarkable and generous for the chosen audience (a limited bundle, a gift, a better experience) over a deeper percentage than the competitor. On salary week, show up with permission (people who asked to hear from you), not a blast.

**6. Matching a competitor's price.** No. Be known for something other than cheap.
- "Every great brand (even those with low prices) is known for something other than how cheap they are." (S011)
- Applied: if a competitor undercuts, answer with proof, service, and story, not a counter-cut.

## Loyalty and penetration

**7. Loyalty vs new customers.** His growth model runs through devotion: delight a few, and they recruit the others. That makes the existing fans the engine of penetration, not an alternative to it.
- "Two things happen when you delight your minimum viable audience" (S003, 2019-03-20): you discover the group is larger than you expected, and they tell the others (his two-item list, paraphrased).
- Applied: invest in the experience of current buyers (delivery, unboxing, follow-up) as the acquisition channel. This is the main clash with Sharp, who says growth comes from light buyers and reach.

**8. Messaging past buyers (WhatsApp, SMS, email).** Only with real permission and only what you promised.
- "Real permission is different from presumed or legalistic permission." (S006, 2008-01-31)
- "Just because you somehow get my email address doesn’t mean you have permission." (S006)
- Applied: a phone number from checkout is not permission to broadcast weekly. Ask, promise a rhythm, keep it, and stop if they stop caring.

## Brand and positioning

**9. Distinctive brand assets.** He defines brand as expectations, memories, stories, and relationships, and puts logo and design below that.
- "A brand used to be something else. It used to be a logo or a design or a wrapper." (S007, 2009-12-13)
- "Design is essential but design is not brand." (S007)
- Applied: consistent colours and pack shapes are useful, but they mark a brand; they do not make one. Sharp and Ritson weight distinctive assets far more.

**10. Positioning against competitors.** Be remarkable to your chosen people rather than "better" on a feature list. He uses design and story, not comparison tables, as the weapon (S015, S014).
- "The race to the top is focused on design and respect and dignity and guts and innovation and sustainability and yes, generosity when it might be easier to be selfish." (S014, 2012-08-20)

**11. Premium pricing.** For it, when the product is worth it.
- "An alternative is: You’ll pay more than you hoped but get more than you paid for." (S012)
- Episode title: "Price is a story." (S017, 2018-11-01)

**12. Over-promising in ads.** Against. Hyped expectations kill brands.
- "Expectation is in the eye of the beholder, but expectation is often enhanced and hyped by the marketer hoping for a quick win." (S008, 2016-08-27)

## Research and process

**13. Research before tactics.** No recorded position on formal market research in this brain. His substitute is knowing the chosen audience so well that you can say who it is for and what it is for (S001, S005).
- Applied: he would ask the merchant to describe ten real customers by name and what they say about the product before choosing a channel.

**14. Goals.** Pick what the work is for; you cannot optimize for everything at once.
- "Measure the right things and it’s more likely you’ll end up where you hope to go. But it’s certain you can’t have all of these." (S005, 2026-07-08)

## Channels

**15. Interruption ads.** Skeptical: people have learned to ignore them (S015). He accepts that the web turns everyone into a direct marketer and asks you to do it well and for the right reasons (S009).

**16. Influencers, AI, specific platforms.** No recorded position in this brain.
