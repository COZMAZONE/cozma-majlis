# Stories: Rory Sutherland

18 stories and case studies he reuses, most-told first. Figures are as he tells them; they drift between tellings (the Eurostar sum is "six billion pounds" in 2009 and "six million pounds" in the 2011 transcript).

1. **Eurostar, supermodels and wi-fi.** Engineers spent about six billion pounds to cut roughly 40 minutes off London to Paris. For a fraction of that you could add wi-fi, or pay supermodels to serve free Chateau Petrus, and "people will ask for the trains to be slowed down" (S001). Lesson: the engineer's frame (make it shorter) misses the psychological frame (make it better). (S001, S002, S003, S027, S007, S011)

2. **The London Underground dot-matrix boards.** The best improvement in satisfaction per pound was not more trains but a board saying when the next one comes. Lesson: the pain of a wait is uncertainty, not minutes. (S003, S005, S011)

3. **The Uber map.** Uber is not cheaper or quicker than a phoned cab; it removes the "Twilight Zone of uncertainty" by showing the car approach (S005). Lesson: show progress and people relax. (S005, S017, S007)

4. **Red Bull.** Small can, high price, unpleasant taste, and the most successful challenger to Coke; no research group would have asked for it. Lesson: the illogical cues are the proof of potency. (S006, S009, S011, S013)

5. **Frederick the Great's potatoes.** Compulsion failed; declaring the potato a royal vegetable guarded loosely made peasants steal and grow it, because "if something is worth guarding, it's worth stealing" (S001). Lesson: scarcity and status persuade where force fails. (S001)

6. **Diamond Shreddies.** Ogilvy Canada relaunched a square cereal by turning it 45 degrees and calling it new. Lesson: intangible value created "without changing the product in the slightest" (S001). (S001)

7. **The hotel doorman.** A consultant replaces the doorman with an automatic door and loses recognition, security and status. Lesson: never define a role by its narrowest function. (S038)

8. **Virgin's salt and pepper set.** Engraved "Stolen from Virgin Atlantic Airways upper-class", remembered long after the aircraft type is forgotten. Lesson: the potent things are very small. (S002)

9. **18 white pills, 6 blue.** Split identical antibiotics by colour and more people finish the course. Lesson: chunking and milestones change behaviour for free. (S003, S005)

10. **Smiley-face speed signs.** About 10 percent of a speed camera's running cost and twice as effective at preventing accidents. Lesson: persuasion often beats compulsion. (S001, S002)

11. **Make the balance optional.** Online banking would get more use if "Tell me my balance" were a choice, not the default screen, at a cost of about 50 pounds. Lesson: remove the moment of bad news from the interface. (S002)

12. **Royal Mail's 98 percent.** The organisation nearly broke itself going from 98 to 99 percent next-day delivery while the public thought it was 50 to 60 percent. Lesson: if perception is worse than reality, fix the perception first. (S003)

13. **The squash-court shower sign.** The J. Walter Thompson copy test asked for a notice at the pool; the real answer was to move it to the squash-court exit: "Shower before swimming." Lesson: change the question. (S039)

14. **Share a Coke.** Ogilvy Sydney's idea made Coca-Cola more than a billion dollars and the agency about 350,000 dollars of profit (S021, S023). Lesson: fat-tailed ideas are paid like hourly labour; marketing is held to thin-tail maths. (S021, S023)

15. **The AMEX Gold Card.** People were not applying from fear of rejection; an inclusive tone and a form that needed only a signature fixed it. Lesson: the barrier is often emotional and invisible to research. (S023)

16. **Chairs outside the coffee shop.** Leave tables out in the rain to signal "open"; stack two chairs upside down and nobody comes in near closing. Lesson: tiny signals do the work of signs. (S019)

17. **The charity mailing.** Of seven tested variables, the one rational idea cut donations by about 30 to 40 percent, and the "wacko" ones raised them. Lesson: test what does not make sense. (S019)

18. **Timotei's green and white pack.** The only shampoo on the shelf that did not read as feminine, so men bought it. Lesson: packaging removes emotional brakes that research never surfaces. (S025)
