# Positions: Rory Sutherland

34 topics a Saudi or Gulf Salla/Zid merchant brings to the creative council (DTC beauty and supplements, for example a collagen jar). Each has his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and year, and a concrete reading for that merchant. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct; the stance that follows is marked "(inferred, no direct source)" and is built from his general principles. His views are stable from 2008 to 2026; where emphasis shifted, it is noted.

## Attention and the first seconds

**1. Hooks and the first seconds.** No recorded position on social-video hooks as such. His perception theory gives a clear rule: the brain only spends attention on what deviates from expectation, so the opening must break the category pattern, and a tiny odd detail beats a loud generic one.
- "We effectively only notice things that are surprising" (S029, 2024)
- "we often ignore information which is normal and expected and derive the most meaning and significance from things which are unusual, unexpected or superfluous." (S038, 2018)
- For the merchant: open on the one strange, true detail (the texture, a real customer's odd habit, an unexpected setting), not on a product shot every competitor also uses (inferred application).

**2. Headlines.** Values the headline that selects its reader psychologically rather than by targeting; admires short lines that carry a whole attitude.
- "I never read The Economist. Management trainee. Aged 42" is "a magnificent example of microtargeting, even though it appeared on a poster" (S012, 2019)
- "the best targeting is often psychological, not technological." (S012, 2019)
- For the merchant: write the line only the right woman recognises herself in; do not rely on the ad platform to find her.

**3. Long versus short copy.** No recorded position in his own quoted words. LBB reports him saying jingles, long-form copy, direct mail and cartoons still work but are out of fashion (S023, reporter's paraphrase). As a former direct marketer he judges length by response, not taste (inferred, no direct source).
- "I always wanted to start an agency called Unfashionable, where the rule was you can only use unfashionable media because they're underexploited" (S023, 2026)

## Proof and people

**4. UGC and testimonials.** No recorded position on UGC. He treats social proof as one of the real engines of a sale (S019) and distrusts self-reported reasons, so he would value real behaviour shown on camera over people explaining why they bought (inferred, no direct source).
- "Anybody I talk to who's serious in academic psychology is deeply skeptical of self-reported anything." (S005, 2014)
- For the merchant: real customers using the jar in real bathrooms, with real quirks, beat scripted praise.

**5. Real people versus models or celebrities.** Celebrities count as a costly signal that makes advertising credible, not as decoration. No recorded position on models versus real people (inferred: realness reads as trust; a celebrity works only when the cost of the association is visible and fits).
- "using celebrities or creative talent or filmmaking skills or storytelling skills which are in short supply and therefore seen as scarce and valuable" (S006, 2019)

**6. Social proof and popularity.** A sale works through scarcity and other people's behaviour, not only price.
- "there's scarcity value, the fear that other people might buy the shit that you want, social proof" (S019, 2024)

## Brand, performance and spend

**7. Brand versus performance.** Both, as explore and exploit. Bottom-of-funnel work funds the business but does not grow it; brand is the exploring bee.
- "Nothing wrong, by the way, with doing bottom of the funnel stuff. It's a perfectly worthwhile source of funds." (S023, 2026)
- "But don't expect to grow. Don't expect to get lucky." (S023, 2026)
- "Marketing should be treated as a branch of R&D" (S024, 2025)

**8. Efficiency of media.** Against the "efficiency bubble". Efficient, cheap-looking delivery can lose the signal that made advertising work.
- "Efficient advertising is hence rather like practical lingerie: it does a job, but not the job it's supposed to do." (S038, 2018)
- "brands don't grow by being more efficient. They grow by being available, wherever, and however, the buyer prefers." (S024, 2025)

**9. Fame.** Being known compounds.
- "Fame is a luck multiplier." (S022, 2025)

**10. Repetition and frequency.** No recorded position on frequency caps. He is wary of the single repeated USP, which he dates to admen retreating from psychology into "fake rationality".
- "something a brand's advertising would reiterate to the point of tedium." (S027, 2008)
- Inferred: repeat the brand's distinctive assets and idea, vary the execution; consistency is part of the costly signal (inferred, no direct source).

## Price and offers

**11. Discounts and price promotions.** A discount is a psychological event, not just a price change; it signals and frames. He prefers framings that add (a bigger trade-in) over cuts, and removing provocative prices over cutting everything.
- "Very few people in business pay any attention to the psychology of price" (S026, 2023)
- He reports a carmaker selling 30,000 more cars by adding 3,000 euros to the trade-in instead of cutting the price (S011, 2011).
- For the merchant: a gift, a bigger size or a bundle protects the jar's price better than a percentage off (inferred application).

**12. Fake urgency and endless sales.** Real scarcity works; fake scarcity only works on people who will not notice, and a brand's customers do notice.
- "There is a shop on Oxford Street that's been holding a closing down sale uninterruptedly for about the last five years" (S019, 2024)
- Inferred: never run a countdown that resets; it burns the reputation that makes the brand worth trusting (inferred, no direct source).

**13. Premium positioning and price perception.** Price is a feeling and a signal; a premium price can be part of the product's credibility.
- "To economists, price is a number. To everyone else, price is a feeling." (S026, 2023)
- "the disgusting taste is actually essential to the overall experience" (S009, 2018), on Red Bull's small can and high price.

**14. Price structure and choices.** Offer tiers; the middle one is the default for most buyers. People dislike commitment even when it saves money.
- "most people go for the one in the middle" (S011, 2011)
- "Many people on pay-as-you-go mobile phone tariffs would pay less if they took out a subscription, but they dislike the commitment entailed." (S026, 2023)
- For the merchant: one month, three months, six months, with the middle made attractive; offer subscription as an option, never the only path.

**15. Generosity and gifts.** Disproportionate generosity is under-used and hard to prove in a spreadsheet.
- "If you want to prove this in a spreadsheet, you'll struggle." (S024, 2025)

## Creative craft

**16. Design and layout.** Small usability details decide experience; big organisations get the big things right and the signage wrong.
- "But the small stuff, what you might call the user interface, is done spectacularly badly." (S002, 2010)
- Goal dilution: people believe a single-purpose thing is better at its purpose (S003, 2011).
- For the merchant: one product, one job per page section; a cluttered "does everything" layout reads as weaker.

**17. Video ads.** No recorded position on short-form video. He praises cheap, idea-led work over lavish production (S031) and says resonance matters more than polish.
- "It doesn't have to be slick" (S022, 2025)
- "imagination can be a good substitute for a big budget." (S031, 2010)

**18. Humour.** Humour is a scarce, costly signal and therefore persuasive; he uses it constantly himself.
- "You can use humor, which is another scarce commodity." (S006, 2019)

**19. Emotion versus reason.** All decisions are emotional; the difference is which emotion (regret for consumers, blame for bureaucrats).
- "Both kinds of decision are emotional" (S043, 2025)
- "a consumer buying a new car is trying to minimise the risk of personal regret, whereas a bureaucrat choosing a supplier is trying to minimise the risk of personal blame." (S043, 2025)

**20. Packaging and naming.** Packaging changes taste; names change acceptance. A bad name can sink a good product.
- "packaging determines what we expect from the contents and thereby alters our experience of the product even after its wrapping has been discarded." (S026, 2023)
- "they completely screwed the pooch by choosing the word nuclear" (S019, 2024)

**21. Claims and honesty.** He defends framing as legitimate but accepts the ethical risk, and argues brands are trusted because they have reputations to lose. No recorded position on medical or health claims (inferred: a false claim destroys the hostage that makes a brand worth buying).
- "you can patently lead people to do things they subsequently regret." (S005, 2014)
- "Look at where capitalism works best and you'll find a business sensitive to shame." (S028, 2012)

**22. Landing pages and product pages.** Optimise the experience, then conversion, then advertising. Small form and wording changes can double response.
- "there's no point in doing good advertising if you lead people to a shop or app or a choice architecture or an application form or a website which doesn't convert." (S013, 2019)
- He recalls that reordering the questions on an application form could double replies (S005, 2014).

**23. Post-purchase and waiting.** Show progress and timing; uncertainty is the pain.
- "It changes the quality of the waiting time by reducing uncertainty." (S017, 2024)

## Research, testing and data

**24. Research before creative.** Useful for facts, poor for motives. Research would never have produced Red Bull.
- "Logic won't tell you, research won't tell you this" (S011, 2011), on Red Bull charging far more than Coke for less drink.
- "these factors generally don't come out in research, but they're incredibly strong emotional drivers or emotional brakes." (S025, 2026)

**25. Testing and A/B.** Strongly pro-testing, but test the strange, not only the sensible. Testing is creativity's partner.
- "The bizarre half-sister of creativity is testing and rigorous measurement." (S019, 2024)
- "It's cheaper to try than to argue" (S024, 2025)

**26. Creative fatigue.** No recorded position on ad fatigue metrics. From his attention theory: once a creative becomes expected it stops being noticed, so keep the idea and refresh the surprising detail (inferred, no direct source).

**27. Data and measurement.** Measure, but do not let measurability define value; past data limits new ideas.
- "Obsessing about the extent to which something you try in the future must make sense in terms of data you have from the past is a massive constraint on innovation and experimentation." (S019, 2024)
- "There's no data to support doing something new" (S024, 2025)

**28. Averages and personas.** The average customer does not exist.
- "you're actually ending up designing for a customer that doesn't exist at all." (S025, 2026)

## Competition, agencies, awards

**29. Competitors.** Do not copy or benchmark; find what they neglect and double down.
- "If your competitors are boring, be weird." (S022, 2025)
- "find something that everybody else has overlooked and doesn't regard as important, double down on it." (S023, 2026)

**30. Agencies versus in-house.** No recorded position on in-house teams. He says agencies' real value is the questions they ask, and that "process" is theatre for procurement.
- "the most valuable thing an advertising agency does is the conversation it asks and the questions it asks." (S023, 2026)
- "In effect, what you do is you talk shit until you get lucky." (S021, 2026)

**31. Awards and "creativity" versus sales.** No recorded position on awards. He insists business already knows creativity sells and is merely uncomfortable with it (S012); as a direct marketer he would judge by response (inferred, no direct source).
- "deep down in their hearts, people in business know perfectly well that advertising creativity works." (S012, 2019)

**32. Finance and procurement.** They own the metrics and only see a fraction of value.
- "The only thing finance can measure is value as expressed in immediate financial transactional value." (S021, 2026)

**33. Experimentation budget.** Ring-fence a slice for odd ideas (70/20/10).
- "You don't accept a high degree of failure. You aim for it." (S025, 2026)

**34. Cultural fit and local meaning (halal, Gulf norms).** No recorded position on the Gulf market. His view that meaning is contextual implies local symbols, language and norms carry more weight than global templates, and that a symbol read the wrong way can poison the product (inferred, no direct source).
- "Human behaviour is sometimes more influenced by the medium in which a transaction takes place than by the content of the transaction itself." (S027, 2008)
