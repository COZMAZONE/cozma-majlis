# Books: Rory Sutherland

Built only from how he teaches the books in public (talks, columns, interviews and the publisher's extract of the rules). No book text is reproduced beyond short quotes from the Penguin extract (S004).

## The Wiki Man (2011)

- **Year:** 2011, a collection drawn from his Spectator column of the same name, which was running by 2008 (S030 is a 2008 column). His bios joke it sells on Amazon "at prices between £1.96 and £2,345.54" (S013).
- **Core thesis (our words):** Small, cheap ideas and changes of perspective often beat big projects, and technology plus psychology lets imagination substitute for money.
- **Key frameworks it carries:** imagination as a substitute for money and "Return on Idea" (#9, #14 in principles); levels of service, streamlining versus elaboration (#13); reputation as a hostage (#7). See frameworks.md #7, #9, #13.
- **Taught in:** S030, S031, S032 (columns titled "The Wiki Man"), S027, S028.

## Alchemy: The Surprising Power of Ideas That Don't Make Sense (2019; US title: Alchemy: The Dark Art and Curious Science of Creating Magic in Brands, Business, and Life)

- **Year:** 2019 (S013 announces UK and US publication in March 2019; S004 is Penguin's extract of the rules).
- **Core thesis (our words):** Logic finds the answers everyone else finds. Human behaviour runs on psycho-logic (signals, context, regret, trust, satisficing), so the biggest gains come from changing perception and from testing ideas that do not make sense in advance.
- **Key frameworks:** the rules of Alchemy (the opposite of a good idea can be another good idea; don't design for average; it doesn't pay to be logical when everyone else is; attention shapes experience; a flower is a weed with an advertising budget; logic kills magic; a lucky accident is still science; test counterintuitive things; rationality is golf with one club; dare to be trivial; if there were a logical answer we would have found it; dare to look stupid), plus costly signalling, satisficing, the doorman fallacy and the Uber map. See frameworks.md #1 to #8, #10, #13 to #18, #20.
- **Taught in:** S004 (rules), S013 (launch interview), S006 (launch podcast), S014, S019, S029, S008, S025.

## Transport for Humans: Are We Nearly There Yet? (2021, with Pete Dyson)

- **Year:** 2021 (S018 extract, copyright 2021 Pete Dyson and Rory Sutherland).
- **Core thesis (our words):** Transport is designed for freight-like "econs" who only value speed; real passengers value certainty, comfort, status, autonomy and the use of time, so cheap psychological fixes beat expensive speed.
- **Key frameworks:** reduce uncertainty (#10); Eurostar and wi-fi; paceometers and time perception (S046). See frameworks.md #10 and stories.md #1 to #3.
- **Taught in:** S018, S007, S046, S017.

## Major talks that act as his canon

- **Life lessons from an ad man (TED 2009):** perceived value, placebos, Frederick the Great, Shreddies (S001).
- **Sweat the small stuff (TED 2010):** disproportionality, the four-quadrant chart, the Chief Detail Officer (S002).
- **Perspective is everything (TED 2011):** reframing, the three lenses, goal dilution, leaky perception (S003).
