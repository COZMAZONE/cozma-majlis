# Frameworks: Rory Sutherland

22 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (perception, signalling and trust, small changes, creativity, efficiency traps). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that use the framework or a clear synonym. Book tags show where the idea sits in his books as he teaches it in public: *Alchemy* (2019), *The Wiki Man* (2011, collected Spectator columns), *Transport for Humans* (2021, with Pete Dyson). No book text is used beyond the Penguin extract of the rules (S004). Every framework has a 1 to 10 score so the council can grade a creative, a page or an offer.

---

## Perception: what the customer actually experiences

### 1. Psycho-logic versus logic *(Alchemy)*
- **What it is:** Two kinds of reasoning. Logic is the spreadsheet: one right answer, proportional cause and effect, value as a fixed number. Psycho-logic is how people actually decide: by feeling, context, signals, fear of regret and shortcuts that make evolutionary sense even when they look irrational.
- **Parts:** (1) The logical problem (build the bridge). (2) The psycho-logical problem (whether people feel safe crossing it). (3) Test both, because the second is invisible to research and to finance. He rejects the word "bias" where a heuristic is sensible in context (S005).
- **When to use:** Any time a brief is framed only as price, features or speed. Before accepting that a creative "makes no sense".
- **How to score:** 1 to 10 on how much of the proposed work addresses the psycho-logical problem. A 2 is a spec sheet, a discount and a buy button. An 8 names the felt worry or the felt reward (trust, status, regret, effort) and changes something to meet it.
- **Quote:** "Solving problems using only rationality is like playing golf using only one club" (S004)
- **Sources:** 14 (S003, S004, S005, S008, S009, S011, S012, S013, S017, S019, S025, S027, S043, S029)

### 2. All value is perceived value *(Alchemy; TED 2009)*
- **What it is:** There is no objective value separate from perception, so improving how something is perceived is as real a gain as improving the thing. Advertising creates "intangible value" that is a legitimate substitute for material cost.
- **Parts:** Perceived, badge or symbolic value; placebo effects (branded painkillers work better, S003); the Shreddies relaunch that changed nothing but the angle (S001); von Mises' point that sweeping the restaurant floor creates value as surely as cooking (S003).
- **When to use:** When the team assumes the only way to win is a better formula or a lower price.
- **How to score:** Does the work add meaning without changing the product? 1 = no meaning added, just facts. 10 = the same jar feels more valuable after seeing the ad, with nothing false said.
- **Quote:** "all value is actually relative. All value is perceived value." (S001)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S003, S005, S011, S013, S016, S019, S026, S027)

### 3. Reframing and frame of reference *(Alchemy; TED 2011)*
- **What it is:** The same fact, price or experience produces different reactions depending on how it is described and what it is compared with.
- **Parts:** Name it differently (a year off versus unemployment, S003); choose the comparison (better than the Germans, S003); frame the payment (surge pricing versus happy hour, S026; a trade-in bonus versus a price cut, S011); frame the meaning (money to a charity at the toll, S003).
- **When to use:** Price presentation, bundle naming, guarantee wording, any claim the customer already knows.
- **How to score:** 1 = the default industry frame (price, size, discount percentage). 10 = a frame that makes the same offer feel fairer, rarer or more meaningful, and stays honest.
- **Quote:** "Choose your frame of reference and the perceived value, and therefore, the actual value is completely transformed." (S003)
- **Sources:** 11 (S003, S005, S011, S019, S022, S026, S027, S039, S041, S043, S013)

### 4. Attention shapes experience; perception is leaky *(Alchemy, rule 4)*
- **What it is:** What we notice and expect changes what we feel. Expectations set by packaging, words and setting leak into the experience of the product itself.
- **Parts:** Expectation (the prison hotel is wonderful if you expected it, S004); leak (a valeted car "drives better", S003); packaging that shapes taste even after it is thrown away (S026); the brain notices only what deviates from prediction (S026, S029).
- **When to use:** Product pages, unboxing, packaging, the first seconds of a video, any copy that sets expectations.
- **How to score:** 1 = expectations left to chance or set wrong ("low in fat" style cues that make it taste worse). 10 = every touchpoint primes the experience the product then delivers.
- **Quote:** "packaging determines what we expect from the contents and thereby alters our experience of the product even after its wrapping has been discarded." (S026)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S004, S013, S026, S029, S038, S017, S001)

## Signalling and trust

### 5. Costly signalling *(Alchemy, rule 5)*
- **What it is:** Messages are believed partly because they were visibly expensive or effortful to send. Waste, extravagance and craft are the proof. Efficiency can strip out the very cost that made the message credible.
- **Parts:** Costly to generate, costly to deliver, displayed broadly (S004). Wedding invitation versus email (S004, S006). Poetry versus prose (S006). Celebrities, filmmaking and humour as scarce resources (S006).
- **When to use:** Deciding between a cheap, efficient execution and one that looks invested; choosing media; judging whether a brand looks like it will be around next year.
- **How to score:** 1 = looks like it cost nothing and could vanish tomorrow. 10 = visibly invested craft, consistency and confidence that a fly-by-night seller could not fake. Do not confuse cost with loudness.
- **Quote:** "Trying to make something efficient and trying to make something effective are not the same thing." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S006, S011, S012, S038, S041, S028)

### 6. Brand as insurance; satisficing *(Alchemy; Edge 2014)*
- **What it is:** Most purchases are satisficing, not maximizing: people want something good enough that is very unlikely to be terrible. A known brand is a premium paid for reduced risk.
- **Parts:** Satisficing (Herbert Simon, cited S005, S011); loss weighs about twice gain (S011); McDonald's is "really good at not being bad" (S005); reputational hostage (#7).
- **When to use:** Any first-purchase decision, especially ingestibles where the fear is harm or a rip-off.
- **How to score:** 1 = the ad argues "best" while leaving the fear of a bad outcome untouched. 10 = the work removes the downside fear (who we are, how long we have been here, what happens if it does not suit you).
- **Quote:** "I will pay a premium as a form of insurance for the reduced likelihood that this product is appalling." (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S005, S011, S028, S043, S015, S036)

### 7. Reputation as a hostage *(The Wiki Man)*
- **What it is:** We trust sellers who have something to lose. A valuable, fragile reputation makes cheating expensive, so visible reputation is a reason to buy.
- **Parts:** Known name, visible presence, physical assets, public commitments; shame sensitivity (TripAdvisor, S028); banks' branches as an immovable asset that reassures (S015).
- **When to use:** Trust copy, "about us", guarantees, showing the real founder or team, choosing where to be seen.
- **How to score:** 1 = anonymous seller, nothing at stake. 10 = the customer can see exactly who would be embarrassed if this went wrong.
- **Quote:** "we like doing business with people we can hurt a bit." (S005)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S015, S028, S043)

### 8. Price is a feeling (price as a quality signal) *(Alchemy)*
- **What it is:** Price is read as information about quality and kind, not just cost. Cheap can signal poor; small and expensive can signal potent; a discount can signal that the full price was never real.
- **Parts:** Red Bull: small can, high price, taste that proves it works (S009, S011); wine enjoyed more when told it is expensive (S001); remove the items that provoke outrage rather than cut every price (S026); the middle option of three is the default (S011); a sale works through scarcity and social proof as much as price (S019).
- **When to use:** Price points, bundles, any proposal to discount, comparison tables.
- **How to score:** 1 = price set on cost and cut when sales dip. 10 = price, pack size and offer structure tell a consistent story about quality, and promotions are framed so they do not cheapen the base price.
- **Quote:** "To economists, price is a number. To everyone else, price is a feeling." (S026)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S009, S011, S019, S026, S006, S008)

## Small changes, big effects

### 9. Butterfly effects and the Chief Detail Officer *(TED 2010; Alchemy, rule 10)*
- **What it is:** In human systems, effect is not proportional to spend. Small, cheap changes (a line in a script, a form, a sign) often beat large, expensive ones. He wants a "Chief Detail Officer" for the neglected cheap-but-huge quadrant.
- **Parts:** A 2x2 of cost versus effect: strategy (big, big), consultancy (big, nothing), trivia (small, nothing), and the unnamed fourth quadrant (small cost, big effect) (S002). Direct-mail lessons: reorder a form and response doubles (S005).
- **When to use:** Before approving a big redesign or a bigger budget; when conversion is stuck.
- **How to score:** List the 10 smallest changes available. Score 1 if none were tried; 10 if the cheap quadrant was tested first.
- **Quote:** "Really small interventions can have huge effects." (S005)
- **Sources:** 9 (S002, S004, S005, S011, S013, S015, S019, S023, S031)

### 10. Reduce uncertainty (the Uber map) *(Alchemy; Transport for Humans)*
- **What it is:** People hate uncertainty more than waiting or cost. Showing progress, time or status changes the felt experience without changing the service.
- **Parts:** Uber's map (S005, S017); dot-matrix train boards (S003, S005); countdown red lights (S003); "approval within 12 hours" and test results in 24 hours (S005).
- **When to use:** Delivery times, order tracking, what happens after payment, how fast results arrive, any wait.
- **How to score:** 1 = the customer does not know what happens next or when. 10 = every step after the click is visible and timed.
- **Quote:** "It changes the quality of the waiting time by reducing uncertainty." (S017)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S005, S007, S011, S017, S018, S046)

### 11. Choice architecture: the interface determines the behaviour
- **What it is:** How a choice is presented (defaults, order, chunking, ease) shapes the choice more than the incentive does.
- **Parts:** Chunking (18 white pills then 6 blue, S003, S005); make the bad news optional (online banking balance, S002); impulse saving button (S001); pledge from future pay rises (S027); timing a request (leave the rubbish by the door, S005).
- **When to use:** Checkout, subscription design, bundle selection, upsell placement, forms.
- **How to score:** 1 = the right choice takes effort. 10 = the default and the layout make the good choice the easy one, with no trickery.
- **Quote:** "The reason is that the interface fundamentally determines the behavior." (S001)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S010, S027, S043)

### 12. Gratuitous detail (Kano delighters)
- **What it is:** Emotional attachment comes from the small, unnecessary, generous touches, because people judge brands as a whole. These are the first things finance cuts.
- **Parts:** Virgin's "Stolen from Virgin Atlantic" salt set (S002); Lydmar lift buttons (S002); Tesla dog mode (S015); disproportionate generosity, small gifts with no catch (S024).
- **When to use:** Unboxing, inserts, thank-you messages, packaging, the post-purchase flow.
- **How to score:** 1 = nothing anyone would mention to a friend. 10 = one cheap, memorable detail people photograph or retell.
- **Quote:** "One of the most under-explored tools in marketing is being disproportionately generous to your customers" (S024)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S015, S024, S031)

## Creativity: unreasonable problem solving

### 13. The opposite of a good idea can be another good idea *(Alchemy, rule 1)*
- **What it is:** In physics opposites cancel; in psychology both can work. Expensive and cheap, elaborate and streamlined, scarce and popular can each be right for different people or moments.
- **Parts:** Two hotel welcomes (S013); streamlining versus elaboration in service (S030); Coke and Red Bull both win (S013); vending machines next to staffed shops (S015).
- **When to use:** When the team copies the category leader, or when the council is stuck between two "correct" options.
- **How to score:** 1 = the idea is the category convention. 10 = a deliberate, coherent opposite that some customers will love.
- **Quote:** "The opposite of a good idea can be another good idea." (S029)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S013, S014, S015, S029, S030)

### 14. Don't design for average *(Alchemy, rule 2)*
- **What it is:** The average customer does not exist. Ideas start with outliers and spread to the mainstream.
- **Parts:** The Earl of Sandwich (S004); the US Air Force cockpit (S025); open-plan offices that serve no one (S015); test different things on different people, not just averages (S026).
- **When to use:** Persona work, creative briefs, choosing which customer story to tell.
- **How to score:** 1 = written for "women 25 to 45". 10 = built for one vivid, specific person whose need is extreme enough to be clear.
- **Quote:** "You are more likely to come up with a good idea focusing on one outlier than on ten average users." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S015, S025, S026, S039)

### 15. Test counterintuitive things, because nobody else will *(Alchemy, rule 8)*
- **What it is:** The valuable discoveries are the ones that would not survive a logic review. Reserve budget for tests that seem silly; aim for some failures.
- **Parts:** 70/20/10 budgeting (S021, S025); random bees, explore versus exploit (S015, S023, S016); the charity mailing where the rational variant lost (S019); eight tests beat four (S025).
- **When to use:** Test plans, creative rotation, deciding what a new ad set should try.
- **How to score:** 1 = every variant is a safe tweak of the control. 10 = at least one variant in every round is something no competitor would dare.
- **Quote:** "Don't just test the things that make sense. Test the things that don't make any sense." (S019)
- **Sources:** 9 (S004, S015, S016, S019, S021, S023, S024, S025, S005)

### 16. It doesn't pay to be logical when everyone else is *(Alchemy, rule 3)*
- **What it is:** Logic is predictable; it leads everyone to the same place and to a price war. Find what competitors neglect and double down.
- **Parts:** Benchmarking makes you similar (S022); unfashionable media (S023); flats near railway stations (S004); Party Cannon's balloon logo among Gothic ones (S029).
- **When to use:** Competitive reviews, when the creative looks like the category.
- **How to score:** Put the ad next to three competitors with logos removed. 1 = indistinguishable. 10 = instantly yours.
- **Quote:** "It's better to be different than it is to be better" (S021)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S021, S022, S023, S029, S039)

### 17. Change the question
- **What it is:** When a problem resists solution, it is usually defined wrongly. Move the sign, change the moment, redefine the job.
- **Parts:** "Shower before swimming" at the squash-court exit (S039); a transport problem that is a timing problem (S027); the Jesuit who asked to pray while smoking (S026).
- **When to use:** Briefs that keep failing, "how do we make people read long copy", "how do we cut CAC".
- **How to score:** 1 = answers the brief as written. 10 = finds a reframed question with a cheaper, better answer.
- **Quote:** "The 'correct' response to this question is not to answer it but to change it." (S039)
- **Sources:** 5 (S026, S027, S039, S019, S004)

### 18. Dare to be trivial, dare to look stupid *(Alchemy, rules 10 and 12)*
- **What it is:** Breakthrough questions sound dumb, and important clues look trivial. Creativity is asking the question no one was stupid enough to ask, then post-rationalizing what works.
- **Parts:** Sherlock Holmes and Darwin's finches (S004); "Have you thought about making it pink?" (S019); two dishwashers (S019).
- **When to use:** Ideation sessions; when status is suppressing odd ideas.
- **How to score:** 1 = only ideas that can be defended in advance. 10 = includes ideas that are obvious only in retrospect.
- **Quote:** "there are far more good ideas out there we can post-rationalize than there are good ideas we can pre-rationalize." (S019)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S019, S021, S029, S039)

## Efficiency traps

### 19. Efficiency versus effectiveness (the efficiency bubble)
- **What it is:** In marketing, efficiency and effectiveness are weakly related. Cutting cost and chasing measurable conversion can destroy the value you cannot see on a spreadsheet.
- **Parts:** Will Lion's "efficiency bubble" (S012); targeting finds customers, creative creates them (S015); thin-tail math for a fat-tail discipline (S024); finance measures only transactional value (S021, S023).
- **When to use:** Media mix, any "make it cheaper" brief, any argument from cost per result alone.
- **How to score:** 1 = judged only by a short-term efficiency metric. 10 = judged also by what it builds (memory, trust, word of mouth) and with a plan to read the long tail.
- **Quote:** "If you optimise targeting, that's helping you find your customers. But good creative can actually create them." (S015)
- **Sources:** 10 (S004, S006, S012, S015, S021, S023, S024, S038, S013, S022)

### 20. The doorman fallacy *(Alchemy)*
- **What it is:** Defining a role or feature by its narrowest function, then automating or cutting it, destroys the other jobs it was quietly doing.
- **Parts:** The doorman also gives recognition, security and status (S038); shopping trips do many jobs (S038); self-checkout and shoplifting (S025); tech that starts as an option and becomes an obligation (S024).
- **When to use:** Cutting a free sample, an insert, a WhatsApp human reply, a premium box, a video's slow opening.
- **How to score:** List every job the element does. 1 = cut on its stated function alone. 10 = all hidden jobs were named and replaced or kept.
- **Quote:** "An automatic door is no substitute for a hotel doorman because the role of a doorman encompasses functions beyond opening the door, such as recognition, security and signalling the status of the hotel." (S038)
- **Sources:** 4 (S024, S025, S038, S015)

### 21. Defensive decision-making
- **What it is:** Many business choices optimise for the decision-maker's ability to defend them, not for the outcome. This produces measurable mediocrity.
- **Parts:** "No one ever got fired for buying IBM" (S005, S043); regret (consumer) versus blame (bureaucrat) (S043); "process" as a bellwether (S036); easier to be fired for being irrational than unimaginative (S021).
- **When to use:** When a creative is rejected as "not proven", or when a safe option wins only because it is safe.
- **How to score:** 1 = chosen because it is easy to justify. 10 = chosen because it is likely to work, with the risk sized and tested small.
- **Quote:** "it is often better to be measurably mediocre than immeasurably brilliant." (S013)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S005, S013, S021, S036, S043)

### 22. Order of optimisation: experience, then conversion, then advertising
- **What it is:** Fix the product experience first (repeat purchase), then the conversion path (site, form, checkout), and only then scale advertising. Advertising into a bad experience accelerates the bad word of mouth.
- **Parts:** Experience, conversion, advertising (S013); perception first if perception is worse than reality (Royal Mail, S003); clean the restaurant floor before improving the food (S003).
- **When to use:** Before raising ad spend, before a new campaign, when ROAS falls while traffic rises.
- **How to score:** 1 = scaling ads into a broken page or unhappy customers. 10 = experience and conversion audited first, ads last.
- **Quote:** "The first thing you optimise is the experience and, therefore, the propensity to repeat purchase." (S013)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S013, S012)
