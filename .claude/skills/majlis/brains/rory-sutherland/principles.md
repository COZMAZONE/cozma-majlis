# Principles: Rory Sutherland

26 decision rules, clustered across sources. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Quotes are verbatim from the cached texts (podcast quotes keep caption quirks).

## Perception is the product

**1. Most problems past basic wealth are problems of perception, so solve the perception before you spend on reality.**
- "most problems are actually problems of perception." (S001)
- "if your perception is much worse than your reality, what on earth are you doing trying to change the reality?" (S003)
- Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S013, S016, S027, S026). Confidence: high.

**2. Perceived value is real value. Do not apologise for adding meaning.**
- "I would argue that there is no objective perception of value whatsoever." (S016)
- "often it simply makes better economic sense to spend money altering perception than altering reality." (S027)
- Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S011, S016, S027, S013). Confidence: high.

**3. Meaning beats amount. People react to what a thing means, which depends on context and description.**
- "What we do is not a product of what is, it is a product of the meaning of what is." (S013)
- "people don't choose between options, they choose between descriptions of options" (S022)
- Sources: 5 (S003, S013, S022, S041, S026). Confidence: high.

**4. Reframing is the cheapest lever there is.**
- "the power of reframing things cannot be overstated." (S003)
- "Two things which are objectively identical can be perceived very differently depending on how they are framed." (S041)
- Sources: 7 (S003, S005, S011, S026, S039, S041, S043). Confidence: high.

**5. Perception leaks: packaging, setting and expectation change the experience of the product itself.**
- "perception is, in any case, leaky." (S003)
- "Analgesics that are branded are more effective at reducing pain than analgesics that are not branded." (S003)
- Sources: 5 (S003, S004, S019, S026, S029). Confidence: high.

## Trust and signals

**6. Signals must be costly to be believed. Visible effort, craft and spend are part of the message.**
- "It seems that a large part of advertising creates trust and conviction in its audience precisely because it is perceived to be costly." (S038)
- "a large reason why marketing works is precisely because it's inefficient" (S006)
- Sources: 5 (S004, S006, S038, S041, S011). Confidence: high.

**7. People satisfice. Reduce the fear of a bad outcome before promising the best one.**
- "one of the reasons people pay a premium for brands isn't because they're objectively better, it's because they're less likely to be terrible." (S005)
- "fear of loss, fear of disappointment actually act twice as powerfully on the human decision making process as hope of improvement" (S011)
- Sources: 5 (S005, S011, S041, S043, S019). Confidence: high.

**8. Trust the seller with something to lose, and show customers what you would lose.**
- "Reputation acts as a kind of cashless deposit in human dealings." (S028)
- "Efficiency without trust is actually meaningless" (S015)
- Sources: 4 (S005, S015, S028, S024). Confidence: high.

**9. Price is a signal and a feeling, not just a number. Set and present it for what it says.**
- "Things are only expensive or cheap by contrast with some preconceived expectation." (S026)
- "the disgusting taste is actually essential to the overall experience" (S009)
- Sources: 6 (S001, S009, S011, S019, S026, S008). Confidence: high.

**10. Uncertainty hurts more than waiting. Show people what happens next.**
- "There's something about the human brain, for whatever reason, which hates uncertainty." (S005)
- "what makes them uneasy is the uncertainty of the arrival." (S017)
- Sources: 6 (S003, S005, S007, S017, S018, S011). Confidence: high.

## Small things, disproportionate effects

**11. Effect is not proportional to cost. Look for the cheap change with the big result before the big budget.**
- "the most potent things are actually very, very small." (S002)
- "Really small interventions can have huge effects." (S005)
- Sources: 7 (S002, S004, S005, S013, S019, S023, S031). Confidence: high.

**12. Sweat the detail: the user interface is where big organisations fail.**
- "But the small stuff, what you might call the user interface, is done spectacularly badly." (S002)
- "The gratuitous attention to detail is often what actually proves decisive and emotional, because people think of brands as a whole." (S015)
- Sources: 4 (S002, S015, S024, S038). Confidence: high.

**13. The interface decides the behaviour. Design defaults and order before you pay incentives.**
- "The reason is that the interface fundamentally determines the behavior." (S001)
- "Human behaviour is sometimes more influenced by the medium in which a transaction takes place than by the content of the transaction itself." (S027)
- Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S027). Confidence: high.

**14. Imagination substitutes for money. A small budget is a reason to think, not to copy.**
- "imagination can be a good substitute for a big budget." (S031)
- "Return on Idea has improved faster than Return on Investment." (S031)
- Sources: 3 (S001, S031, S002). Confidence: high.

## Creativity and testing

**15. If there were a logical answer, someone would have found it. Persistent problems need illogical tries.**
- "Because if there were a logical solution to your problem, someone would already have found it." (S025)
- "Some of the most valuable discoveries don't make sense at first, because if they did, someone would have discovered them already." (S004)
- Sources: 4 (S004, S019, S025, S021). Confidence: high.

**16. Test what does not make sense, and plan to fail some of the time.**
- "Test the things that don't make any sense." (S019)
- "You don't accept a high degree of failure. You aim for it." (S025)
- Sources: 6 (S004, S015, S019, S021, S024, S025). Confidence: high.

**17. Being different beats being better. Do not benchmark yourself into sameness.**
- "It's better to be different than it is to be better" (S021)
- "If you have the same metrics as everybody else, you end up with homogeneity." (S021)
- Sources: 5 (S004, S021, S022, S023, S029). Confidence: high.

**18. Change the question before answering it.**
- "Generally when a lot of people find some problem difficult, it is often because the question has been defined in the wrong way to begin with." (S039)
- "Some of the most brilliant ideas in the history of the world are an answer to a question that no one's actually thought to ask" (S021)
- Sources: 4 (S019, S021, S026, S039). Confidence: high.

**19. Dream first, then post-rationalise. Most good ideas can only be justified after they work.**
- "Dream first and then post-rationalise." (S021)
- "there are far more good ideas out there we can post-rationalize than there are good ideas we can pre-rationalize." (S019)
- Sources: 3 (S019, S021, S029). Confidence: high.

**20. Do not trust self-report. What people say in research is the press office, not the decision.**
- "Anybody I talk to who's serious in academic psychology is deeply skeptical of self-reported anything." (S005)
- "these factors generally don't come out in research, but they're incredibly strong emotional drivers or emotional brakes." (S025)
- Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S011, S025). Confidence: high.

**21. Design for the outlier, not the average.**
- "You are more likely to come up with a good idea focusing on one outlier than on ten average users." (S004)
- "Quite often the right solution isn't an average or isn't an optimal, it's a mixture of two quite different things." (S015)
- Sources: 4 (S004, S015, S025, S026). Confidence: high.

## Efficiency, measurement and nerve

**22. Effectiveness is not efficiency. Cutting cost is easy to see; lost value is not.**
- "Cost savings are easy to measure. Lost sales are not." (S024)
- "Opportunity costs are invisible; short-term savings earn you a bonus." (S012)
- Sources: 7 (S004, S012, S015, S021, S023, S024, S038). Confidence: high.

**23. Creative makes customers; targeting only finds them.**
- "But good creative can actually create them." (S015)
- "the best targeting is often psychological, not technological." (S012)
- Sources: 3 (S012, S015, S024). Confidence: high.

**24. Do not let what is measurable decide what is important.**
- "The very fact that it's measurable has made people obsess about what you can measure, on the assumption that what you can measure must be important." (S015)
- "You're being judged by thin-tail math" (S024)
- Sources: 5 (S015, S021, S023, S024, S043). Confidence: high.

**25. Watch for defensive decisions: choices made to avoid blame, not to win.**
- "it is often better to be measurably mediocre than immeasurably brilliant." (S013)
- "a consumer buying a new car is trying to minimise the risk of personal regret, whereas a bureaucrat choosing a supplier is trying to minimise the risk of personal blame." (S043)
- Sources: 5 (S004, S005, S013, S036, S043). Confidence: high.

**26. Explore and exploit at once: keep a protected slice for discovery.**
- "There's no point in exploiting if you don't explore, and there's no point in exploring if you don't exploit." (S015)
- "Take a proportion of the budget you spend with every agency, give it to the planners and the creatives to spend as they choose." (S023)
- Sources: 5 (S015, S016, S021, S023, S025). Confidence: high.
