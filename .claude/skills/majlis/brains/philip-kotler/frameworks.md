# Frameworks: Philip Kotler

9 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (plan, choose, deliver, journey). The core textbook frameworks are stated in this brain's own words; quotes are only lines that appear in the fetched sources. Book tags name where each idea is taught, per his publication list (S002).

---

## Plan

### 1. The strategic marketing process: R, STP, MM, I, C *(Marketing Management, 1967 onward)*
- **What it is:** Research the market; segment it; choose target segments; position the offer for them; design the marketing mix; implement; control by measuring and correcting. Marketing begins before the product is made.
- **Parts:** Research (needs, competitors, trends). STP (segmentation, targeting, positioning). Marketing mix (4Ps). Implementation. Control (metrics, feedback).
- **When to use:** Any launch, relaunch, new market, or plan that starts with "let's run ads".
- **How to score:** Does the option follow the order, with each step explicit? An option that jumps to promotion without a chosen target and position scores low.
- **Quote:** "Marketing is the homework that we do before we have a product." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S009

### 2. Marketing management as choosing and growing customers *(Marketing Management)*
- **What it is:** The job is choosing target markets and getting, keeping, and growing customers by creating, delivering, and communicating superior value.
- **When to use:** Framing any marketing decision as a customer-value decision rather than a promotion decision.
- **How to score:** Which customers does the option get, keep, or grow, and through what superior value?
- **Quote:** "Marketing management is the art and science of choosing target markets and getting, keeping and growing customers through creating, delivering, and communicating superior customer value." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

## Choose

### 3. Segmentation and the five tests (MSADA) *(Marketing Management; Principles of Marketing)*
- **What it is:** Divide the market into groups with different needs or responses (by demographics, geography, psychographics, behaviour), then keep only segments that are measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable, and actionable.
- **When to use:** Choosing audiences, building a second product line, regional or dialect splits (Riyadh vs Jeddah vs Eastern Province), Gulf expansion.
- **How to score:** Run the five tests on each proposed segment. A segment you cannot measure, reach, or serve differently is not a segment.
- **Quote:** "If markets are to be segmented and cultivated, they must meet certain requirements. Segments must be Measurable, Substantial, Accessible, Differentiable, and Actionable." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

### 4. Targeting: choose whom to serve, and whom not to *(Kotler on Marketing)*
- **What it is:** Pick one or more segments to serve well rather than serving everyone averagely. Not all customers are equally valuable.
- **When to use:** Narrow vs broad audience decisions; deciding which customers to prioritize in service and retention.
- **How to score:** Is the target named, and is it attractive (size, growth, fit with the store's strengths)? Does the plan avoid aiming for the average?
- **Quote:** "No company in its right mind tries to sell to everyone. Gillette does not try to sell razor blades to preteens, and Kimberly-Clark doesn't try to sell its Huggies to childless families." (S003)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

### 5. Positioning and differentiation *(Marketing Management; Marketing Insights from A to Z)*
- **What it is:** Design the offer and image so the target sees a clear, valued difference from competitors. Anything can be differentiated; competitive advantage is what rivals cannot or will not match. Smaller firms should focus on a few areas of real expertise.
- **When to use:** Competitive positioning, premium vs value decisions, homepage and PDP messaging.
- **How to score:** Is the difference real, valued by the target, and hard to copy? Is the brand narrow enough to be best at something?
- **Quote:** "The key to branding, especially for smaller firms, is to focus on a limited number of issue areas and develop superb expertise in those areas." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

## Deliver

### 6. The marketing mix (4Ps) and the 1P trap *(Marketing Management)*
- **What it is:** Product, price, place (distribution), promotion, designed together to deliver the position. Most companies over-use promotion and neglect the other three.
- **Parts:** Product (quality, range, packaging, service). Price (list, terms, value). Place (channels, availability, delivery). Promotion (advertising, sales promotion, PR, digital).
- **When to use:** Any time the proposed fix is "more ads" or "a discount".
- **How to score:** Does the option change the right P? Is the mix consistent (a premium product sold with constant discounts is inconsistent)?
- **Quote:** "Too much of today’s marketing is 1P marketing. Companies mainly concentrate on promotion and sales and disregard product, price and place (distribution)." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

### 7. Brand vs commodity and value pricing *(Marketing Insights from A to Z)*
- **What it is:** Without a brand, price is everything and only the lowest-cost producer wins. Price on value, not cost; sell the price rather than sell through price; earn share instead of buying it.
- **When to use:** Discounts, White Friday, price matching, premium launches.
- **How to score:** Does the option build brand value that supports price, or teach buyers to compare on price?
- **Quote:** "The art of marketing is the art of brand building. If you are not a brand, you are a commodity." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

## Journey

### 8. The 5A customer path *(Marketing 4.0, 2017)*
- **What it is:** In a connected world customers move from Aware (I know about it) to Appeal (I like it) to Ask (I am convinced, after checking with friends, reviews, media) to Act (I buy) to Advocate (I recommend). The path is social and not a straight funnel.
- **Parts:** Two ratios: purchase action ratio (PAR: awareness converted to purchase) and brand advocacy ratio (BAR: awareness converted to advocacy).
- **When to use:** Diagnosing where a store loses people (awareness, appeal, reviews and trust, checkout, recommendation); deciding between reach, creative, reviews, and CX fixes.
- **How to score:** Which A is the bottleneck, and does the option fix that A?
- **Quote:** "In line with the 5 A’s, two metrics are valuable to measure: the purchase action ratio (PAR) and brand advocacy ratio (BAR)." (S004)
- **Sources:** S004, S007

### 9. Blending traditional and digital; technology for humanity *(Marketing 4.0, 5.0)*
- **What it is:** Digital does not replace traditional marketing; the two converge. Marketing 5.0 uses technology (AI, sensors, IoT) to serve customers' human needs across generations and channels.
- **When to use:** Channel strategy, AI personalization, omnichannel decisions.
- **How to score:** Does the technology serve a customer need on the path, or is it a gadget?
- **Quote:** "We’re not dropping traditional marketing. We’re blending traditional and digital." (S004)
- **Sources:** S004, S008
