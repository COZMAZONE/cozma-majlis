# Positions: Philip Kotler

15 marketing topics a Saudi or Gulf e-commerce merchant faces. Each has his stance from the sources, verbatim quotes with id, and an "Applied to the store" line. The applied line is this seat's inference from his principles, not something he said about Saudi Arabia; never present it as his quote. Most S001 lines are undated aphorisms from his website. "No recorded position" means the brain has nothing direct.

## Budget and measurement

**1. Brand vs performance budget split.** No recorded ratio. He holds that brand building is the art of marketing and that under-spending on advertising is costly; Marketing 4.0 asks for metrics at each stage of the customer path.
- "If you spend too little on advertising, you are spending too much." (S001)
- "Kotler said two key principles he wanted to get across to readers in Marketing 4.0 were recognizing the alternative paths to purchase that customers take and having clear metrics at each stage." (S004, the AMA writer's summary of his interview, c. 2017)
- Applied to the store: split the budget by the 5A bottleneck. If few people know the brand, fund awareness; if many know but few buy, fix appeal, reviews, and checkout first. Track PAR and BAR monthly.

**2. Metrics.** Purchase action ratio and brand advocacy ratio along the 5A path; share of mind and heart over short-term profit (S004, S001).
- "I don’t care what happened to your profits. Have you improved your share of the customer’s mind and heart this year?" (S001)

## Who to target

**3. Narrow vs broad targeting.** Target. Choose segments that pass the five tests and serve them better than anyone.
- "No company in its right mind tries to sell to everyone." (S001; longer version in S003 citing Kotler on Marketing)
- "If you aim for the average, you will lose." (S001)
- Applied: segment the Saudi market for the hero product (for example by life stage and need: new mothers, women 35+ with joint or skin concerns, gym-goers), test each with MSADA, and pick one or two to own. Run broad delivery if the platform needs it, but write for the chosen segment.

**4. Most valuable customers.** Not all customers are equal; manage relationships for lifetime value.
- "All customers are important but some are more important than others." (S001)
- "Marketing thinking is shifting from trying to maximize the company’s profit from each transaction to maximize the long-run profit from each relationship." (S001)

## Discounts and promotions

**5. Discounting, White Friday, salary week.** No recorded position on seasonal sales. His principles point against competing on price and against "1P marketing" where sales promotion substitutes for product, price, and place.
- "Don’t buy market share. Figure out how to earn it." (S001)
- "You don’t sell through price. You sell the price." (S001)
- Applied: a White Friday plan should be a whole-mix plan (a bundle or limited product, clear value, guaranteed delivery before the date, a reason to buy) rather than a bigger percentage. Explain the value of the full price instead of cutting it.

**6. Price setting.** Value-based, not cost-plus.
- "Cost is of no importance in setting the price. It only helps you to know whether you should be making the product." (S001)
- "Good quality is cheap; cheap goods are costly." (S001)

## Loyalty and penetration

**7. Loyalty vs penetration.** He favours getting, keeping, and growing customers, with delight and advocacy; he does not frame it as a contest with penetration. The 5A path treats advocacy as the end goal that drives new awareness.
- "It is no longer enough to satisfy your customers. You must delight them." (S001)
- "The role of marketers is to guide customers throughout their journey from awareness to ultimately advocacy." (S004)
- Applied: build the post-purchase experience (unboxing, follow-up, easy reviews, referral) so buyers become advocates, and measure BAR. This sits between Godin (fans first) and Sharp (reach first).

**8. Negative reviews and word of mouth.** Can be useful.
- "Sometimes a brand needs negative advocacy to trigger positive advocacy from others," (S004)
- Applied: do not hide every critical review; let real customers answer them.

## Brand and positioning

**9. Brand vs commodity.** Brand, always.
- "The art of marketing is the art of brand building. If you are not a brand, you are a commodity. Then price is everything and the low-cost producer is the only winner." (S001)

**10. Positioning against competitors.** Differentiate on what rivals cannot or will not match; for small firms, focus on a few areas of real expertise.
- "There is no such thing as a commodity. It is simply a product waiting to be differentiated." (S001)
- "The key to branding, especially for smaller firms, is to focus on a limited number of issue areas and develop superb expertise in those areas." (S001)

**11. Distinctive brand assets.** No recorded position in this brain on assets as such; he treats brand as the whole value proposition. Integrated marketing communications should be designed "from the point of view of the receiver" (S001).

## Research and process

**12. Research before tactics.** Yes: research and STP come before the mix.
- "Marketing is the homework that we do before we have a product." (S001)
- "You should never go to battle before you’ve won the war on paper." (S001)
- Applied: before the launch, write the one-page plan: segment, target, position, the 4Ps, the objective, and the metric.

**13. Product and innovation.** Marketing and innovation are the two essential functions (from Drucker, S005); keep making your own products obsolete before competitors do (S001).

## Channels

**14. Digital vs traditional.** Blend them.
- "We’re not dropping traditional marketing. We’re blending traditional and digital." (S004)
- "If you focus too heavily on digital, you might end up being a specialist and never being the CMO." (S004)

**15. AI and new technology.** Use technology for human needs (Marketing 5.0, S008). He would ask what AI means for work and society (S005). No recorded position on specific tools.
