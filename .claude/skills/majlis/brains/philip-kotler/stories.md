# Stories: Philip Kotler

6 stories and examples from the sources. Kotler teaches mostly through definitions and company examples rather than personal stories, so this file is short by design.

1. **A day with Peter Drucker.** Drucker invited him to Claremont, picked him up at the airport, and spent the morning teaching him how to look at Japanese art before lunch with Doris Drucker. Kotler calls Drucker "the grandfather of modern marketing" and credits him with the idea that good marketing makes selling unnecessary and that a great company must excel at innovation and marketing. Lesson: marketing is a way of thinking about the whole business, not a department. (S005)

2. **Gillette, Huggies, and steel.** Gillette does not sell razor blades to preteens, Kimberly-Clark does not sell Huggies to childless families, and a savvy steel company does not sell to every steel user. Lesson: targeting is the first act of strategy. (S003)

3. **The dead chicken.** His line that if you can differentiate a dead chicken you can differentiate anything. Lesson: no product is a commodity unless you let it be. (S001)

4. **Ito Masatoshi of 7-Eleven Japan.** The founder asked him detailed questions about shelf space, assortment, and competitive positioning, and sent his son to study marketing with him for two years. Lesson: the best retailers treat marketing thinking as core operations. (S005)

5. **From Campbell's and Kellogg's to the 5A path.** Traditional fortunes were built on one-way advertising that hit people continuously; in the connected world customers ask friends and reviews before they act and then advocate. Lesson: marketing must follow the customer's new path. (S004)

6. **Friedman vs Drucker.** He studied under Milton Friedman, respected him, and disagreed that profit for shareholders is business's only responsibility. Lesson: marketing is "applied economics with social character" (S006); businesses serve customers, employees, and communities too. (S005, S006)
