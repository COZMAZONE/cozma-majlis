# Positions: Mark Ritson

16 marketing topics a Saudi or Gulf e-commerce merchant faces. Each has his stance from the sources, verbatim quotes with id and date, and an "Applied to the store" line. The applied line is this seat's inference from his principles, not something he said about Saudi Arabia; never present it as his quote. "No recorded position" means the brain has nothing direct.

## Budget and measurement

**1. Brand vs performance budget split.** Both, always, at campaign level; the right ratio depends on category and brand age; most brands underspend on the long. 60/40 is a mental model, not a law.
- "Long is about building brand equity, building our salience, building our association. Short is about harnessing and activating it." (S012, c. 2023-10)
- "It was never about performance versus brand or long versus short," (S013, c. 2025)
- His summary: Binet and Field's later work ranges from about 80/20 for financial services to about 50/50 in B2B, and young brands need more performance (S013, paraphrase).
- Applied to the store: a young Saudi DTC brand can start performance-heavy, but should ring-fence a long line (the same brand idea, broad reach, every month) and grow it as the brand grows. Keep brand ads and conversion ads separate.

**2. Judging everything by short-term ROAS.** Wrong yardstick for the long; and the long also drives short sales.
- "it isn’t long and short, it’s long-short together versus short." (S013, his pub revelation as summarized)

**3. Setting the budget.** Zero-based, tied to returns, not a percentage of revenue.
- "Never let those two figures become separated." (S012) about investment and promised return.

## Who to target

**4. Narrow vs broad targeting.** Target, based on data, and add broad reach where you can. He called the Ehrenberg-Bass "sophisticated mass marketing" orthodoxy something to mock (S002) and then said he is not against it (S004).
- "Not against sophisticated mass marketing, sometimes it works, but sometimes we should also do targeting." (S004, his words begin "I’m not against")
- Applied: name the core target from data (for example, women 28 to 45 who already buy beauty supplements online), build the plan for them, and still run a broad, always-on brand line.

**5. Research before tactics.** Non-negotiable.
- "Twenty minutes of focus group data or a basic survey of his market would have confirmed the fallacy of his approach" (S006, 2014-03-26)
- Applied: before a new campaign or LP, call 20 recent buyers and 10 non-buyers, run a short survey to the WhatsApp list, read reviews. Then decide.

## Discounts and promotions

**6. Discounting.** Absolutely against.
- "On discounting, absolutism is the correct strategic posture." (S011, 2026-08-03)
- "Value is not low price." (S011)
- Applied: replace "20% off" with added value (gift, bundle, faster delivery, a better product), and keep the shelf price.

**7. White Friday / Black Friday.** A trap: it pulls sales forward and leaves a hole after. He concedes brand damage is smaller when everyone discounts.
- "You sped them up and reduced their marginal value, nothing more." (S010, 2025-11-26)
- "When everyone is drinking martinis, no one has a drinking problem." (S010)
- Applied: if the merchant must play White Friday, limit it in time and range, do the margin math first, measure how many buyers were existing customers, and plan for the post-sale dip.

**8. Salary week and recurring codes.** No recorded position on salary week specifically. His promotion-addiction logic applies: recurring deals teach customers to wait for them (S011 reason two, S009).
- Applied: a code every salary week becomes the real price within a few months.

**9. When a promotion is acceptable.** Value-added promotions such as competitions can build equity (S009). P&G's one sanctioned use of promotion is driving trial (S011). He allows "maybe one or two occasions" (S011).

## Loyalty and penetration

**10. Loyalty vs penetration.** No single recorded stance; he credits Sharp's work on salience and availability (S003) and argues targeting loyal users can be a valid reason to target (S004). He warns that discounts anger the loyal full-price base (S011, reason six).
- "They love your brand. They are not idiots." (S011) about loyal buyers who paid full price last month and now see a 30% badge.

## Brand and positioning

**11. Distinctive brand assets.** Strongly for, credited to Ehrenberg-Bass.
- "The focus on distinctive brand assets over ephemeral attempts to deliver mission and purpose." (S003, 2017-01-18)
- "If you want to drive up recall, you’ve got to codify it," (S012)

**12. Positioning against competitors.** Relative differentiation on two or three attributes, said for years.
- "You aren’t going to be the only bank that is seen as ‘friendly’." (S009) More people seeing you as friendly than your size predicts is the win.
- Applied: pick the two or three attributes (from research) where the brand can be seen as more X than rivals, and say only those, everywhere, for years.

**13. Overused brand words.** Against integrity, quality, excellence, trust, innovation in positioning (S005).

**14. Brand purpose.** Mostly nonsense for most brands.
- "Brand purpose is mostly nonsense talk." (S004, 2017-10-06)

## Channels and creative

**15. Channel choice.** No best channel; decide strategy first, use diverse media for synergy (S012).

**16. Creative.** Find a big idea, use emotion, pre-test, and do not pull ads that still work (S012). Do not mix brand and performance in one "double duty" ad (S013).
