# Stories: Mark Ritson

9 stories and cases he uses, as told in the sources. Figures are as he tells them.

1. **The marketing plan with no strategy.** A general manager asked him to review a marketing manager's plan; it was full of digital tactics, and when asked about targeting, positioning, and objectives the manager was confused that such questions were still asked. Lesson: tactics without strategy; finance will call marketing "the colouring-in department". (S001)

2. **Bingo and beer.** A UK party chairman tweeted that a budget cut on bingo and beer helped "hardworking people do more of the things they enjoy" and was mocked for days. Twenty minutes of research would have shown the assumption was wrong. Lesson: market orientation; assumptions about customers are conjecture. (S006)

3. **"I only watch sport live."** Digital marketers dismissed TV-viewing data because it did not match their own habits. Lesson: a sample of one, yourself, is not the market. (S006)

4. **Three concentric circles.** A brand manager showed him a positioning of brand essence, personality, and identity circles filled with integrity, quality, excellence, trust, and innovation. Two reasons it would fail: too many words, and words everyone uses. Lesson: three words, from research, that differentiate. (S005)

5. **Tyrrells.** After KP Snacks bought the crisp brand in 2018 it cut promotions; average retail price rose 29% and volume fell 27% at first, then the brand recovered by rebuilding a premium position. Lesson: escaping promotion addiction hurts first and pays later, if you rebuild differentiation. (S009)

6. **Dell Australia.** Dell could keep cutting laptop prices and grow units, but not profitably; it chose to stop. "You spend 70% of your time convincing your own bosses and 30% convincing the customer." (Dell's Arjun Deuskar quoting an old adage, S009). Lesson: win management's patience before cutting promotions.

7. **P&G holds price while rivals discount.** P&G's CFO said promotion's only sanctioned job is trial, and P&G accepts slower share recovery rather than buy share with deals; it upgraded a detergent at the same price and returned it to growth. Lesson: attack the numerator of value (what customers get), not the denominator (price). (S011)

8. **Black Friday's name.** Philadelphia police coined "Black Friday" in the 1950s for a day of traffic, crime, and misery; retailers later reframed it as going "into the black". Lesson: for most brands, it darkens profits. (S010)

9. **Tourism Australia ran the same campaign again.** He called the CMO a genius because her follow-up to the big animated kangaroo campaign was the same campaign. Lesson: pick a position and hold it for years. (S008)
