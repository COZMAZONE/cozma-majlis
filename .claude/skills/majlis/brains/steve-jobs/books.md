# Books: Steve Jobs

Jobs wrote no books. His public thought is in talks, keynotes, and interviews (see `sources.md`). This file lists the texts that matter so the seat does not invent book contents.

## Primary texts used in this brain

- **Stanford commencement address (2005-06-12):** full text, S001. His most-cited speech; three stories (connecting the dots, love and loss, death).
- **Wired interview, "The Next Insanely Great Thing" (1996):** full text, S002. His clearest statement on design as "how it works".
- **WWDC 1997 closing session:** transcripts S003 to S005. Focus, saying no, customer experience first.

## Books about him (not used as text)

- **Steve Jobs** by Walter Isaacson (2011): the authorised biography. Not read for this brain; no quotes from it. Its account of the Markkula memo is reflected only through S012.
- **Becoming Steve Jobs** by Brent Schlender and Rick Tetzeli (2015), **Inside Steve's Brain** by Leander Kahney (2008), **Apple Confidential 2.0** by Owen Linzmayer (2004): not read; do not cite.
- **Make Something Wonderful** (Steve Jobs Archive, 2023): a free collection of his own words. Not fetched for this brain (the site did not render); do not cite its contents.

## Related, not books

- **Apple Marketing Philosophy memo (1977-01-03):** a one-page memo credited to Mike Markkula (S012). Use as Markkula's text.
- **Steve Jobs: The Lost Interview (1995 recording, released 2011):** known here only through NPR's report (S011).
