# Stories: Steve Jobs

10 stories he told or that the sources document, most useful to a merchant first.

1. **Calligraphy and the Mac.** After dropping out of Reed he sat in on a calligraphy class, learning serif and sans-serif type and letter spacing; ten years later it went into the Mac, "the first computer with beautiful typography." (S001). Lesson: taste comes from unrelated experiences; typography is not a detail.

2. **The LaserWriter: "do you want this?"** The first small laser printer was full of great technology, but he knew it would sell the moment he held up the first printout: the customer did not need to know what was in the box (S005). Lesson: sell the result the customer can see.

3. **Putting a bullet in OpenDoc (WWDC 1997).** Back at Apple, he killed projects including OpenDoc, apologised to the developers who had worked on them, and said focus means saying no; a developer insulted him on stage and he answered by conceding the technology was good but did not fit a vision customers would buy (S003, S004, S005). Lesson: good things that don't fit must go, and you take the criticism.

4. **The Miele washer.** In 1996 his family spent about two weeks of dinner conversations choosing a washer and dryer, trading speed against softer clothes, less water, and longer life, and chose an expensive German machine because "These guys really thought the process through." (S002). Lesson: design is a set of value trade-offs, and buyers will pay for a product that was thought through.

5. **Fired, then NeXT and Pixar.** At 30 he was fired from the company he started; he says it freed him into "one of the most creative periods of my life", leading to NeXT, Pixar, and his return to Apple (S001). Lesson: setbacks reset you as a beginner.

6. **NeXT's great technology that did not sell.** Wired opens by noting NeXT failed to sell its "elegant and infamously buggy black box" though the insight behind it was right (S002); Gruber reads the 1997 answer as Jobs admitting NeXT was his biggest scar (S004). Lesson: technology first, customer second, fails.

7. **Built for ourselves (the Mac, 1985).** The Mac team did no market research; they built the computer they wanted and judged it themselves (S010). Lesson: a team with taste that uses the product can be the first judge, then the market decides.

8. **Saying no to 1,000 things (2004).** Explaining Apple's innovation, he described hallway conversations, late-night calls, and ad hoc meetings of six people, and a discipline of saying no to almost everything (S007). Lesson: process makes you efficient; focus makes you innovative.

9. **The craftsmanship gap (Lost Interview, 1995).** He blamed fallen product companies on sales and marketing people who could not tell a good product from a bad one and lacked the craftsmanship to turn a good idea into a good product (S011). Lesson: keep the people who care about the product in charge of it.

10. **Markkula's three points (1977).** Early Apple's investor and partner Mike Markkula wrote a one-page marketing philosophy: empathy, focus, impute; people judge a book by its cover (S012). Lesson: presentation is part of the product. (Markkula's memo, not Jobs's words.)
