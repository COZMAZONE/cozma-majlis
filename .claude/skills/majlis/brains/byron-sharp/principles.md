# Principles: Byron Sharp

12 decision rules. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Quotes are verbatim from the fetched text; (Institute) or (colleague) marks words that are not Sharp's own.

## Growth

**1. Grow penetration. Loyalty follows size; it is not the lever.**
- "People believe brands need to increase loyalty to grow. That's not true," (S002)
- "You need more customers, not more loyalty from existing customers." (S002)
- Sources: S002, S003, S004, S007, S010. Confidence: high.

**2. Acquisition does the growing; retention does not stop decline.**
- (Institute) "Brands that grow do so due to greater acquisition of category buyers, rather than from reducing the defection of existing customers." (S003)
- Sources: S003, S002, S007. Confidence: high.

**3. Reach everyone who buys the category, including light and future buyers.**
- "So reach is not optional. You have to find a way to reach everyone, cost effectively." (S001)
- "People on the streets would be amazed at marketers writing plans to say they don't want to talk to 80% of their customers." (S005)
- Sources: S001, S005, S011, S010. Confidence: high.

## Memory and availability

**4. Be easy to think of and easy to buy, in the same moments.**
- "Growth and maintenance largely depends on mental and physical availability," (S001)
- Sources: S001, S010. Confidence: medium.

**5. Advertising works on memory, slowly; today's sales were built in the past.**
- "Most of your [current] sales will come from the work that people have done in the past." (S001)
- "You spend money today to lift the value of your brand and company," (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: low (one source, many quotes).

**6. Be always on. Spread exposures; do not burst and vanish.**
- "If you watch TV tonight, I bet you will see the same ad multiple times. But two weeks later you can watch TV and you won’t see that ad at all" (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: low.

**7. Look like yourself. Keep distinctive assets and do not change them for novelty.**
- "You don't have to have lots of different creative." (S001)
- (colleague) "And don't waste money on packaging changes, character changes... all these activities make it difficult for consumers to identify your brand." (S004)
- Sources: S001, S004, S006, S008. Confidence: high.

**8. Distinctiveness beats perceived differentiation.**
- "Branding lasts, differentiation doesn’t." (S006)
- Sources: S006, S007, S010. Confidence: high (as his position; Ritson disputes it).

**9. Get noticed, then stop paying for more attention.**
- "Our job is to get some attention. I don’t want to do advertising and not be seen. But after that, paying for a lot more [attention]? No." (S001)
- "You need to fit into their busy lives, that is all," (S001)
- Sources: S001. Confidence: low.

## Promotions, metrics, and method

**10. Promotions and performance ads catch the in-market few; they do not build the brand.**
- "Paid search, in store display, price promotions, special offers" are all for catching people when they fall, and "most people are not in market" (S001, two fragments of one sentence).
- Sources: S001, S002, S007, S012. Confidence: high.

**11. Distrust magic ratios and fashionable metrics.**
- "Les Binet is a lovely guy, and I would say a good friend of mine, but [the 60:40 rule] really is terrible, very misleading," (S001)
- He called "things like attention is the new metric" "nonsense" (S001, reported).
- Sources: S001. Confidence: low.

**12. Use evidence and experiments, not stories.**
- "So I would advise people to learn how to do controlled experiments, that that’s an important skill to have, rather than knowing about how ink bleeds off the page." (S005)
- Sources: S005, S001, S009. Confidence: high.
