# Books: Byron Sharp

Built only from public talks, Institute articles, and short passages others quote. No book text is reproduced.

## How Brands Grow: What Marketers Don't Know (Oxford University Press, 2010)

- **Core thesis:** Brand growth follows empirical laws found across categories and countries. Brands grow by increasing penetration; loyalty varies little and follows size (Double Jeopardy). Most buyers are light buyers. Marketers should build mental and physical availability, reach all category buyers, and use distinctive assets consistently rather than chase differentiation, targeting, loyalty programs, or price promotions.
- **Key frameworks:** Double Jeopardy; light buyers and the Pareto myth; duplication of purchase; mental and physical availability; distinctiveness over differentiation; the seven rules for brand growth (rule one: "continuously reach all buyers of the category", as quoted in S011). See frameworks.md.
- **Taught in:** S001 (his 2022 talk), S002, S005, S006 (the differentiation line), S007 and S010 (summaries), S011 (rule one).

## How Brands Grow Part 2 (2015, with Jenni Romaniuk)

- **Core thesis (from public descriptions):** Extends the laws to new markets, services, and emerging economies, with more on distinctive brand assets and building mental availability. Romaniuk leads much of the distinctive-asset work.
- **Taught in:** S004 (a co-author colleague on duplication of purchase and Double Jeopardy), S008.
