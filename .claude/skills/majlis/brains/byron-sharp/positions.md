# Positions: Byron Sharp

16 marketing topics a Saudi or Gulf e-commerce merchant faces. Each has his stance from the sources, verbatim quotes with id and date, and an "Applied to the store" line. The applied line is this seat's inference from his principles, not something he said about Saudi Arabia; never present it as his quote. "No recorded position" means the brain has nothing direct.

## Budget and measurement

**1. Brand vs performance budget split.** Both are needed, but without memory-building advertising no amount of performance spend delivers growth. He rejects 60:40 as a magic number built on award-entry data.
- "Les Binet is a lovely guy, and I would say a good friend of mine, but [the 60:40 rule] really is terrible, very misleading," (S001, 2022-08-23)
- "60:40 is supposed to be the magic formula for you." (S001)
- "When people buy a car or financial services they typically look at two brands." (S001) Activation wins your share of those in market; advertising decides whether you are one of the two.
- Applied to the store: do not pick a ratio from a slide. Ask what share of Saudi category buyers the plan reaches each month, and keep a steady brand-building line running all year next to the performance campaigns. If the account is almost all retargeting and lookalikes of buyers, it is harvesting, not growing.

**2. Attention and engagement metrics.** Against paying for more than fleeting attention.
- "Am I just going to look at the bus shelter ad for 10 seconds? Would I need to? No, so don’t be suckered in." (S001)
- Applied: a 2-second, well-branded Snap view is worth more than an engagement metric. Judge by reach and branding, not by watch time alone.

**3. Flighting around seasons.** Always on, skewed for seasonality, never dark.
- He advised dividing the annual budget into about 50 weekly portions, or about a twelfth a month allowing for seasonality (S001, paraphrase of reported advice).
- Applied: Ramadan, White Friday, and National Day can carry more weight, but the brand should not vanish between them.

## Who to target

**4. Narrow vs broad targeting.** Broad. Reach all category buyers; tailor only to extend reach.
- "There are two golden rules. First, you have to reach everyone in the category, you can’t just target the susceptible." (S001)
- "So tailor wisely, not too much because you want scale" (S001)
- Applied: for a collagen or beauty product, the category buyer is most Saudi women who buy supplements or skincare, not a narrow interest set. Run broad audiences with Arabic creative that fits each region's dialect if that adds reach (his "Spanish ads" logic), not to exclude anyone.

**5. "Our best customers" and VIP focus.** Heavy buyers matter less than marketers think; growth comes from light buyers.
- "Most of your sales growth will come from your lightest customers" (S001)
- Applied: a VIP-only campaign or a points program will not grow the brand. Keep current buyers happy with good service; spend the growth money on people who have not bought in a year.

## Discounts and promotions

**6. Discounting, White Friday, salary week.** Promotions sell mostly to existing buyers and have little or no lasting effect on sales or loyalty (S007, S012 paraphrase). Price promotions are a way to catch buyers when they fall, not a way to grow.
- Paid search, in-store display, price promotions, and special offers: "all of these things are to catch people when they fall." (S001)
- Applied: if the merchant runs a White Friday discount, judge it only on that week's profit. Do not count the buyers as "new customers acquired" without checking how many bought before. On salary week, the evidence says be present and easy to buy, not deeper discounts.

**7. Raising prices.** (colleague) There is nothing wrong with raising prices, but watch the gap to alternatives because penetration can drop.
- "There is nothing wrong with raising prices." (S004, 2023-11-09)

## Loyalty and penetration

**8. Loyalty vs penetration.** Penetration, decisively.
- "Brands grow by increasing the size of the customer base. You need more customers, not more loyalty from existing customers." (S002, 2011-01-22)
- "They're like medieval doctors." (S002) about marketers who keep writing loyalty-led growth plans.
- Applied: WhatsApp blasts to 10,000 past buyers can earn sales, but they do not grow the brand. Plan the growth number from new buyers.

**9. Loyalty programs.** Weak. His 1997 study with Anne Sharp found loyalty programs produce only a small amount of excess loyalty, later replicated (S009).

**10. Retention vs acquisition budgets.** Acquisition first; most acquisition activity also refreshes current buyers.
- (Institute) "So, aim for acquisition, but understand that most tactics aimed at acquisition can also work to retain customers." (S003)

## Brand and positioning

**11. Distinctive brand assets.** Central. Keep them consistent, use them in every ad, and do not refresh them out of boredom.
- "Branding is absolutely crucial when you're talking to people that may not be in the market at the moment, that might not buy for ages or have just bought," (S001)
- Applied: one colour, one pack shape, one recognizable person or sound, in every Snap, TikTok, and PDP, for years.

**12. Differentiation and positioning against competitors.** Skeptical. Brands share customers with competitors in line with size; seek distinctiveness, not a claimed unique difference.
- "Rather than striving for meaningful, perceived differentiation, marketers should seek meaningless distinctiveness." (S006)
- He argued against Ritson that sophisticated mass marketing understands differences but still tries to reach the whole market (S005).
- Applied: do not build the plan on "we are the only halal collagen with X". Build it on being the brand people recognize and find first.

**13. Brand purpose.** Against using purpose as an apology for marketing.
- "If marketers don’t stand up for marketing, who will?" (S005, 2017-10-06)

**14. Many creative variants.** Against: lots of different creative can weaken memory; make creative that works at scale and looks like you (S001).

## Research and process

**15. Research before tactics.** For empirical evidence and controlled experiments; against marketing built on stories, awards data, or fashion.
- "So I would advise people to learn how to do controlled experiments" (S005)
- Applied: before changing the budget split, run a geo or holdout test (for example, broad reach on in one region, off in another) and read sales, not platform ROAS.

## Channels

**16. Cross-channel synergy.** Mostly unproven: you need reach, and one medium is fine if it delivers it.
- "You need reach, but if you can do that with just one medium, fine." (S001)
