# Frameworks: Byron Sharp

10 frameworks (empirical laws and the rules that follow from them), grouped by the job they do. "Sources" lists the sources in this brain that teach the framework. Book tags show where the idea sits in How Brands Grow (HBG, 2010) or How Brands Grow Part 2 (HBG2, 2015), as known from public material; no book text is reproduced.

---

## How buying works (the laws)

### 1. Double Jeopardy *(HBG; Ehrenberg's law)*
- **What it is:** Brands differ hugely in how many buyers they have and only a little in how loyal those buyers are. Small brands are punished twice: fewer buyers, and those buyers buy a bit less often.
- **Parts:** Penetration (share of category buyers who bought you in a period) drives size. Loyalty metrics move with size, not independently. Holds across categories, countries, and time (S003).
- **When to use:** Any plan whose growth thesis is "make existing customers buy more" or "build loyalty first".
- **How to score:** Does the option grow the number of buyers? An option that only deepens loyalty among current buyers scores low for growth, even if it is cheap.
- **Quote:** "Brands grow by increasing the size of the customer base. You need more customers, not more loyalty from existing customers." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S004, S007, S010

### 2. Acquisition beats retention for growth *(HBG; Riebe, Wright, Stern & Sharp 2014)*
- **What it is:** Growing brands grow by acquiring more category buyers, not by cutting defection; declining brands decline because they fail to acquire enough. Much defection is outside your control (the baby stops needing formula).
- **Parts:** Two levers: loyalty (reduce defection, raise frequency) and penetration (more buyers). The Institute's reading of the evidence: acquisition is roughly twice as important as reduced defection. Most tactics aimed at acquisition also reach and refresh current buyers.
- **When to use:** Budget fights between "retention/CRM" and "new customer" campaigns; win-back vs prospecting.
- **How to score:** Share of the option's reach that lands on non-buyers and lapsed buyers. Keep service good enough not to upset current buyers, but do not over-invest in them.
- **Quote (Institute):** "This means retention does not help brands grow, nor does it stop brands declining. Acquisition does both." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S002, S007

### 3. Light buyers and the Pareto myth *(HBG)*
- **What it is:** Most of a brand's buyers are light or very light buyers; their sheer number makes them central to sales and growth. The heaviest 20% deliver closer to half of sales than 80% (S007).
- **Parts:** Many buyers have not bought you in the past year; they still matter. Growth adds mostly light buyers.
- **When to use:** Lookalike vs broad audiences, VIP-only campaigns, "focus on our best customers" plans.
- **How to score:** Does the option reach the long tail of light and lapsed buyers, or only the heavy few?
- **Quote:** "Most of your sales growth will come from your lightest customers" (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S007, S008

### 4. Duplication of purchase *(HBG; HBG2)*
- **What it is (as taught by a colleague):** Your brand shares customers with every competitor in proportion to their size. You compete with the whole category, most with the biggest brands, not with a "niche" set.
- **When to use:** Competitor positioning ("our real rival is X"), choosing who to conquest.
- **How to score:** Does the plan assume a special, separate segment that the evidence would not support?
- **Quote (colleague, S004):** "So you compete with all the brands in the category, knowing that more of your sales will come from the biggest brands and less from the smaller brands." (S004)
- **Sources:** S004, S007

## How to grow (the rules)

### 5. Mental and physical availability *(HBG)*
- **What it is:** Growth and maintenance depend on two things: being easy to notice and recall in buying situations (mental), and being easy to find and buy (physical). They must overlap.
- **Parts:** Mental: memory structures linked to category cues, built by consistent, well-branded advertising over years. Physical: presence where the category is bought, stock, delivery, findability, price within the buyer's range. Activation and performance ads are part of physical availability.
- **When to use:** Every channel, assortment, marketplace, and creative decision.
- **How to score:** Rate the option on both. High mental, low physical: "your return on your mental availability is terrible". High physical, low mental: shoppers do not see you.
- **Quote:** "If people are going into the store or onto the screen but they don't see you, then your return on physical availability is miserable. But if people know you but you're too difficult to buy, then your return on your mental availability is terrible." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S010

### 6. Reach is not optional / sophisticated mass marketing *(HBG; talks)*
- **What it is:** Reach all buyers of the category, cost-effectively. Tailor only where it extends reach (Spanish-language ads for Hispanic buyers), never to shrink it. Targeting a slice by choice is the error.
- **Parts:** Golden rule one: reach everyone in the category, including people not yet in it. Targeting used "sparingly when it helps expand your reach" (S001).
- **When to use:** Narrow interest targeting, retargeting-heavy plans, "our customer is only X" briefs.
- **How to score:** What share of Saudi category buyers will this plausibly reach in a year? An option that deliberately excludes most category buyers scores low.
- **Quote:** "Sophisticated mass marketing is understanding the differences but trying to reach all of the market." (S005)
- **Sources:** S001, S005, S011, S010

### 7. Continuous, spread-out exposure *(talks)*
- **What it is:** Golden rule two: ad exposures have diminishing returns, so spread them across weeks, time slots, and places. Never burst and go silent.
- **Parts:** Divide the annual budget into about 50 weekly slices (or a twelfth a month, skewed for season). Frequency capping matters; repeated back-to-back ads waste money.
- **When to use:** Flighting around Ramadan, White Friday, National Day; "go big for one week" plans.
- **How to score:** Does the plan keep the brand present most weeks of the year? A one-week blast with eleven silent months scores low.
- **Quote:** "Don’t do this. If your research agency says, ‘You have to spend above a certain number of TARPS or share of voice at a certain time, just sack them and get someone else, because they're idiots." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

### 8. Distinctive brand assets and consistency *(HBG; HBG2 with Romaniuk)*
- **What it is:** Branding that makes the brand instantly identifiable (colours, logo, shapes, characters, sounds, tone). Distinctiveness matters more than perceived differentiation. Do not change assets; consistency builds memory.
- **Parts:** Creative that works at scale; few executions, strongly branded; avoid packaging and character changes that make the brand harder to identify (S004).
- **When to use:** Rebrands, new packaging, every ad, "fresh look for the season" ideas.
- **How to score:** Would a light buyer know it is this brand in two seconds without reading the name? Does the option keep or break existing assets?
- **Quote:** "Rather than striving for meaningful, perceived differentiation, marketers should seek meaningless distinctiveness." (S006)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S006, S008, S010

### 9. In-market vs out-of-market: activation catches buyers when they fall *(talks; Dawes 95:5 is a colleague's rule)*
- **What it is:** Most people are not in the market at any moment. Paid search, displays, price promotions, and special offers only reach those who are. Advertising builds the memory that decides whether you are in the shortlist when they enter the market.
- **Parts:** Activation gets your fair share of in-market buyers; brand advertising decides whether you are among the two brands they consider, often years earlier.
- **When to use:** Brand vs performance split, ROAS-only reporting, cutting brand spend because "it doesn't convert".
- **How to score:** Does the plan only harvest current demand, or also build memory among the many out of market?
- **Quote:** "Physical ability is about catching people when they fall." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

### 10. Promotions and loyalty programs do not build brands *(HBG; Sharp & Sharp 1997)*
- **What it is:** Price promotions spike sales but the extra buyers are mostly existing buyers, and long-term sales and loyalty return to normal afterwards. Loyalty programs produce only a small amount of excess loyalty.
- **Parts:** Evidence summarized in S007 and S012 (not fetched); loyalty-program paper in S009.
- **When to use:** White Friday deep discounts, salary-week codes, points programs.
- **How to score:** Judge a promotion on its immediate profit, not as brand building or customer acquisition.
- **Quote:** "Marketers write plans about how to grow brands for loyalty rather than penetration, and it's a faulty plan," (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S007, S009, S012 (paraphrase only)
