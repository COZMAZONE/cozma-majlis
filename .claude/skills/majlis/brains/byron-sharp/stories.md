# Stories: Byron Sharp

8 examples he and his Institute use, as told in the sources. Figures are as reported.

1. **The car buyer's two brands.** People buying a car or financial services typically look at two brands; activation gets you about half of those where you are in the two, but advertising, often years earlier, decides whether you are in the two at all. Lesson: brand advertising sets the shortlist; performance only converts it. (S001)

2. **The same ad three times tonight, then never.** Watch TV tonight and you see the same ad repeatedly; two weeks later it is gone. "That is the dumbest thing ever." Lesson: spread exposures; do not burst and vanish. (S001)

3. **The bus shelter.** A bus shelter ad needs about two seconds to get its message across; nobody looks for ten, and you should not pay for ten. Lesson: fleeting attention is normal; brand it so two seconds is enough. (S001)

4. **Medieval doctors.** Marketers who write loyalty-led growth plans, see them fail, and write another one next year are like doctors bleeding patients and not noticing they are dying. Lesson: check the evidence on how brands actually grow. (S002)

5. **7 of 140 brands.** The Institute found only seven of 140 leading FMCG brands grew market share by more than 1% a year over six years, and three-quarters stayed within three points. Lesson: share is sticky; promotions do not move it much. (S002)

6. **Sam's dog food and Sally's car insurance.** (Institute) Sam will not buy more dog food unless he gets another dog; it is easier to win Sally, who is renewing her car insurance. Lesson: you cannot make buyers need more; win more buyers. (S003)

7. **The banana, the pink cement mixer, the bulldog.** Examples of highly effective distinctive assets discussed on the Uncensored CMO podcast; it is hard to improve on the banana. Lesson: instantly recognizable cues do the branding. (S008)

8. **Wine before chemistry.** Winemakers could not be creative until they understood the chemistry; before that, a good year was luck and they repeated everything. Lesson: science enables creativity rather than killing it. (S005)
