# Positions: Eugene Schwartz

34 topics, framed for a Saudi/Gulf Salla or Zid DTC store (beauty and supplements, e.g. a collagen powder). Each gives his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and year, and how it applies. Book quotes are 1966 (S001 to S016); talk quotes are from his 1980s to 1994 seminars as transcribed by others. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct; the inferred stance is marked.

## Hooks and headlines

**1. Hooks and the first seconds.** The hook's job is to stop the right viewer and earn the next second, not to sell. Its content comes from desire, awareness, and sophistication.
- "Your headline has only one job" (S003, 1966): to stop the prospect and compel the next sentence.
- "If your first thought holds him, he will read the second." (S002, 1966)
- Apply: for cold Snap/TikTok, open on her situation or feeling, not the tub and the price. For retargeting, open on the product and offer.

**2. Headlines (product page, landing page, banner).** Five or ten words carry most of the value; they must be built from the market, never copied.
- "These five or ten words will make up about 90% of the value of your ad." (S006, 1966)
- Apply: the PDP hero line should name the one performance she wants (what it does), not "premium marine collagen".

**3. Matching the hook to awareness.** The single biggest error he would flag in a DTC account: one creative for every audience.
- "The more aware your market, the easier the selling job, the less you need to say." (S003, 1966)
- Apply: past buyers and cart abandoners (most aware): product, offer, delivery. Followers who know the brand (product aware): new proof, new use, new mechanism. Cold women who want firmer skin but never tried collagen (solution or problem aware): name the desire or problem, prove it can be solved, then the product. Unaware: identification, no price.

**4. Long vs short copy.** Length is set by the work to be done (desire, identification, belief), and every sentence must pull. For direct mail and big space he wrote long; short media need compression plus campaign repetition.
- "To attempt to do the same selling job in ten words, instead of a hundred, or a thousand, is to shoot craps with your client's money." (S003, 1966)
- "and the length of your ad will depend on three factors" (S007, 1966)
- Apply: short hook in the ad, long, structured product page and landing page for the skeptical cold buyer.

**5. Clever, cute, and humorous hooks.** Against humour as a substitute for a market. Not against wit that carries the desire.
- "It cannot simply be a startler, or an attention-getter, or humorous, or cute." (S003, 1966)
- Apply: a funny skit is fine only if the first line still says "this is about you and the thing you want".

## Desire and positioning

**6. Mass desire for the category.** Ride the desire already moving (looking young, firm skin, strong hair, energy), never try to teach a new want.
- "When it tries to create this desire, it is no longer advertising but education." (S002, 1966)
- Apply: do not spend ad money explaining what collagen is to women who do not care; find the desire they already voice in their own words.

**7. Premium positioning and price perception.** Price objections are solved by redefinition: change the standard of comparison, and justify any price with a mechanism.
- "By switching the comparison, and relating it to some other, more expensive standard." (S011, 1966)
- Vivid scenes of fulfillment make the price feel smaller; the sharper the pictures, "the less important will seem your price" (S008, 1966)
- Apply: compare the monthly cost to a salon treatment or a coffee habit, not to the cheaper collagen on Noon. Premium comes from a believable mechanism and real proof, not from adjectives.

**8. Discounts and price promotions.** A price-led headline only works on the most aware; a price cut without a reason is not believed.
- "Price means nothing to a person who does not know your product, or want your product." (S003, 1966)
- "Price cuts must be justified." (S012, 1966)
- Apply: offers belong in retargeting and to past buyers; every offer needs a true reason (National Day, bundle economics). No fake "was" prices, no fake countdowns.

**9. Competitors.** Study every competing ad first (sophistication); attack only with a documented weakness your product truly fixes.
- "A few hours research should give you samples of every competing ad in the field" (S004, 1966)
- "Never attack a weakness unless you can provide the solution to that weakness at the same time!" (S013, 1966)
- Apply: run a Meta Ad Library sweep of Saudi collagen brands before every brief; if all say "glowing skin", that claim is spent.

**10. Brand vs performance / direct response.** He was a pure response man who judged by measured profit, but he believed products have a personality expressed by one dominant trait.
- "I sell, or do not sell, on the basis of one tool alone" (S001, 1966)
- "One of the most potent discoveries of motivation research is that a product, or a store, or a whole group of products has a distinct and complete personality to the consumer." (S006, 1966)
- Apply: keep one brand trait constant across ads (for example, honest and expert), and judge each ad by response.

**11. Packaging and naming.** Physical facts (look, structure, background) are sources of identification and proof, never the headline. Naming a problem or mechanism is a core device.
- "No physical part of your product can ever become a headline." (S002, 1966)
- Apply: show the pack as proof and as an identity cue; name the mechanism in a short memorable phrase only if it is true.

## Proof, people, and honesty

**12. Claims and honesty.** His book records claim escalation in reducing ads at the limits of legality and he admits it was a tactic, not a recommendation. His core rule is believability and product truth.
- "not as recommendations, but as possible strategies to be chosen or rejected" (S004, 1966)
- "if your copy is better than your product, then send it to your client instead of your prospect, and tell him to make it a reality" (S013, 1966)
- Apply: for a supplement, no disease, cure, or medical claims, no weight-loss or "joint repair" promises beyond what is approved; a believed small promise outsells a doubted big one.

**13. UGC and testimonials.** Testimonials are part of intensification ("bring in an audience"): fresh perspectives on the same benefit, best from people the reader identifies with.
- "These new participants may be celebrities, who relate their experiences through the testimonial." (S008, 1966)
- Apply: real customers saying the benefit in their own Gulf dialect; each clip a different angle on the one desire. Never fabricated reviews.

**14. Real people vs models and celebrities.** The image must be one the market believes of itself; elite or unreachable images collapse campaigns.
- "Many campaigns have collapsed because they have asked their market to identify themselves with an unbelievable image." (S009, 1966)
- Apply: real Saudi women close to the buyer, with one aspirational cue; a bridge from her side, not a foreign model.

**15. Guarantees.** Turn the guarantee into the climax of the close, restating the performance.
- "Put Your Guarantee to Work" is his heading; "you can turn that guarantee into the climax of your ad" (S008, 1966)
- Apply: if the store has a real satisfaction or refund policy, make it the last beat of the page, phrased around the result.

**16. Proof and mechanism for supplements.** Readers ask "how does it work?"; answer with a named, described mechanism at the moment of doubt.
- "You have to demonstrate your product, in words, logically, so that he can understand exactly HOW it gives him the end result you promise." (S012, 1966)
- Apply: hydrolysed peptides, dose, how it is taken, in plain words, all within approved language.

**17. Emotion vs reason.** Both, woven together: desire and identification move her, belief structures make the move safe.
- "The first rule of all copy, of course, is that it produce an emotional impact." (S015, 1966)
- "Belief ultimately depends upon structure." (S010, 1966)

## Creative execution

**18. Video ads.** No recorded position on online video (he died before it; S031). On TV he noted short media need compression and campaign repetition.
- For limited media he names two tools: "The first is compression" and "And the second is the campaign" (S008, 1966).
- (inferred, no direct source) Apply: first 2 seconds = awareness-matched hook; middle = one benefit from several real angles; end = mechanism, proof, offer.

**19. Design and layout.** He rated layout below copy, except when matching a medium's native format.
- "I have not discussed layout in this book, because I do not believe layout is nearly as effective as copy in determining the results of your ad." (S014, 1966)
- "A single change in format can add 50% to your readership, and your results." (S014, 1966)
- Apply: native-looking creatives for each platform; but no disguise as news or reviews.

**20. Landing pages and product pages.** The body must carry desire, identification, and belief, with momentum so she keeps scrolling.
- "The objective here is to keep the reader going" (S016, 1966)
- Apply: hero hook by awareness, then benefit scenes, real faces, mechanism, proof, guarantee, offer. Every section must make the next one wanted.

**21. Research before creative.** Non-negotiable; research is the hard work and the writing is assembly.
- "I want to know the book as well as or even BETTER than the author!" (S020, 1994)
- "If you don't spend at least two hours a week finding out where your market is today, you are finished!" (S017, talk)
- Apply: read the merchant's product PDFs, reviews, and WhatsApp replies before writing a word.

**22. Customer language.** The market's words are the copy; mine low culture and everyday talk.
- "There are the words that they use." (S017, talk)
- Apply: harvest phrases from TikTok comments and customer chats in Saudi dialect.

**23. Repetition and frequency.** Short media win by repeating key words across a campaign with fresh embellishments.
- "the repetition of these key words over and over again" (S008, 1966)
- Apply: one core desire line across many fresh executions, not one execution repeated until it dies.

**24. Creative fatigue.** Styles and stages wear out; when a direct claim stops pulling, the product needs rebirth with a new approach.
- "When that same style grows trite, people cannot see the message for the advertisement." (S003, 1966)
- Apply: when a winning hook decays, change the angle or the stage, not just the colour.

**25. Testing and A/B.** He came from mail order, the test lab of copy, and valued big swings over small lifts; motivation research tests hunches cheaply.
- "mail order is the greatest copy writing school in the world" (S001, 1966)
- "I would rather flop badly and succeed greatly than I would coming in with that little five percent boost." (S017, talk)
- Apply: test different desires and awareness stages, not button colours.

**26. Data and measurement.** Judge by measured dollars of profit; research data is direction, not the idea.
- "But a motivation research finding is not a headline, nor even the central theme of an ad, nor will it ever be." (S006, 1966)
- Apply: dashboards tell you which hook lost; only market listening tells you why.

**27. Awards and "creativity" vs sales.** Beautiful copy that people praise is a warning sign.
- "The surest way to know that something is failing as copy is to have someone come in and say" it was great copy (S017, talk); he wanted readers to react to the danger or desire instead.
- "If you want to write poetry, if you want to write prose, if you want to write novels, and if you want to write literature, go outside of advertising." (S021, talk)

**28. Agencies vs in-house.** No recorded position on agencies vs in-house. He owned his own mail-order firms and later traded copy for mailing lists rather than fees (S026).
- (inferred, no direct source) Whoever writes must know the product and market best; an agency that has not read the product documents fails his test.

**29. Formulas, templates, and AI tools.** Formulas are guideposts; copying headlines produces echo ads.
- "They are the kind of ads that clients write, rather than copy writers." (S006, 1966)
- Kurtz on AI: tools are fine only with a process for getting inside the prospect's mind (S029, 2026). Apply: no template ads with swapped product names.

**30. Writing style and clarity.** Simple, transparent, image-rich, second person.
- "is simple, transparent writing. It is not literary writing." (S017, talk)

**31. Fear and the "black side".** Painting the problem is legitimate when the product truly relieves it.
- "You irritate the wound, and then you apply the salve that heals it." (S008, 1966)
- Apply: show the real frustration (dull skin, brittle hair) without shame tactics or medical scare.

**32. Native and editorial formats.** Borrowing a medium's believability works, but today it must never deceive.
- "If he no longer believes in the publication, he won't believe in the advertising it carries." (S014, 1966)
- (inferred) Apply: creator-style, platform-native videos, clearly the brand's ad; never fake news, fake screenshots, or fake reviews.

**33. Sales numbers and social proof in ads.** No recorded position on showing order counts. His proof devices are experts, comparisons, tests, and testimonials.
- (inferred, no direct source) Use proof that the reader can identify with (real customers, how to test it) over raw volume claims; the store's rule bans sales figures anyway.

**34. Work rhythm for creative teams.** Short timed sprints, three hours a day of real writing.
- "I work three hours a day, five days a week." (S018, talk)
- Apply: batch hooks in 33-minute blocks; research the rest of the day.
