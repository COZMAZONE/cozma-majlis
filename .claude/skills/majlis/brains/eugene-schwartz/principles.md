# Principles: Eugene Schwartz

26 decision rules, clustered. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Book quotes are from *Breakthrough Advertising* (S001 to S016); talk quotes are transcriptions by the cited authors.

## Desire

**1. Copy channels desire that already exists; it cannot create it, and it dies fighting it.**
- "Copy cannot create desire for a product." (S002)
- "The greatest mistake marketers make is trying to create demand." (S028)
- Sources: 6 (S002, S007, S019, S028, S037, S008). Confidence: high.

**2. Choose one dominant desire; that choice decides the ad before a word is written.**
- "Your choice among these alternate desires is the most important step you will take in writing your ad." (S002)
- "If it is wrong, nothing else that you do in the ad will matter." (S002)
- Sources: 3 (S002, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

**3. Sell what the product does, not what it is.**
- "The physical product does not sell." (S002)
- "Focus on what your product does, not is" (S019)
- Sources: 4 (S002, S009, S019, S038). Confidence: high.

**4. Never sell; invite a trial of the satisfaction.**
- "Never, ever, ever, ever, sell anything." (S018)
- "In mail order you ask a person to TRY something." (S018)
- Sources: 3 (S013, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

## Awareness and sophistication

**5. Meet the prospect at her exact stage of awareness; a hook that works at one stage fails at another.**
- "a headline which will work to a market in one stage of awareness will not work to a market in another stage of awareness" (S003)
- "Each of these stages is separated from the others by a psychological wall." (S003)
- Sources: 4 (S003, S012, S034, S041). Confidence: high.

**6. To an unaware market, say nothing about price, product, or claim: call the market together by naming who they are.**
- "Price means nothing to a person who does not know your product, or want your product." (S003)
- "You are defining them for themselves." (S003)
- Sources: 3 (S003, S004, S009). Confidence: high.

**7. Count the claims the market has already heard; when claims are exhausted, sell the mechanism, and when mechanisms are exhausted, sell identification.**
- "Here the emphasis shifts from what the product does to HOW it works." (S004)
- "The emphasis shifts from the promise and the mechanism which accomplishes it, to identification with the prospect himself." (S004)
- Sources: 3 (S004, S012, S034). Confidence: high.

**8. If you are first, be plain.**
- "Be simple. Be direct." (S004)
- "And if you are the first in your field, there is no better way." (S005)
- Sources: 2 (S004, S005). Confidence: medium.

**9. Markets move; a winning hook expires when its market changes stage.**
- "You never step in the same river twice. No market ever stands still." (S003)
- Sources: 3 (S003, S004, S006). Confidence: high.

## Headlines and hooks

**10. The headline's only job is to make the right person read the next sentence.**
- "Actually, your headline does not need to sell at all." (S003)
- "From that moment on, your body copy does the selling." (S007)
- Sources: 3 (S003, S007, S016). Confidence: high.

**11. A good hook selects: it pulls in prospects and pushes away everyone else.**
- "Your prospect must identify with your headline before he can buy from it." (S003)
- It must "reject as many people as it attracts" (S003)
- Sources: 2 (S003, S017). Confidence: medium.

**12. Attention without a market is waste: no empty, cute, or merely startling headlines.**
- "It cannot simply be a startler, or an attention-getter, or humorous, or cute." (S003)
- "Most copy writers use a fifth stage problem to write an empty headline" (S003)
- Sources: 1 (S003). Confidence: low (one chapter, stated twice).

**13. The first five or ten words carry most of the value; build them, do not borrow them.**
- "These five or ten words will make up about 90% of the value of your ad." (S006)
- "Copying can be done by any cub." (S006)
- Sources: 2 (S006, S005). Confidence: medium.

## Belief and proof

**14. One fully believed promise beats a stack of half-believed ones; piling claims breeds resistance.**
- "One fully-believed promise has ten times the sales power of ten partially-believed promises." (S010)
- "Most copy writers try to strengthen ads by piling promise upon promise." (S010)
- Sources: 2 (S010, S004). Confidence: medium.

**15. Build on the beliefs the reader already holds; never ask her to break one.**
- "If you violate your prospect's established beliefs in the slightest degree" (S010, S037)
- "Advertising, like science, must accept reality as it exists, not as it might wish it to exist." (S007)
- Sources: 3 (S007, S010, S037). Confidence: high.

**16. Show how it works, and justify every price cut with a reason.**
- "Price cuts must be justified." (S012)
- "A price cut" is "only as good as your words, and your strategy, makes it." (S012)
- Sources: 2 (S012, S011). Confidence: medium.

**17. The product is the source of the ad's power; if the copy is better than the product, fix the product.**
- "Most great copy claims come from the assembly line." (S013)
- "the power of the ad is always in the product itself; it's not in the copywriter" (S038)
- Sources: 3 (S013, S038, S029). Confidence: high.

**18. Identify with images the market can believe of itself.**
- "Many campaigns have collapsed because they have asked their market to identify themselves with an unbelievable image." (S009)
- "Every product you work on should offer your prospect two separate and distinct reasons for buying it." (S009)
- Sources: 2 (S009, S007). Confidence: medium.

## Words

**19. Copy is a clear window: transparent, simple, never literary.**
- "Copy should never call attention to itself." (S018, S021)
- "Oh, god, you're seeing the words, not seeing through the words." (S021)
- Sources: 4 (S017, S018, S021, S025). Confidence: high.

**20. Write to one person who shares a private want with many, and say "you".**
- "You are writing to a number of people who share a private want." (S017)
- "You're writing to an individual, single person always, who shares a problem or a desire with a huge mass of other people." (S018)
- Sources: 3 (S002, S017, S018). Confidence: high.

**21. Write in pictures; choose the right word before worrying about grammar.**
- "The first thing you have to do with this book is forget everything you were ever taught about grammar!" (S025)
- "The shorter your images, the easier they are to understand." (S039)
- Sources: 4 (S022, S025, S031, S039). Confidence: high.

## Craft and habits

**22. Research is the work; the writing is assembly.**
- "I want to know the book as well as or even BETTER than the author!" (S020)
- "The person who is the best prepared and the most knowledgeable makes the most money." (S018)
- Sources: 6 (S018, S020, S029, S033, S035, S036). Confidence: high.

**23. Stay in touch with ordinary people every week, through their media and their talk.**
- "You cannot lose touch with the people of this country, no matter how successful or how potent you are." (S017, S019)
- "Low culture makes big money." (S017)
- Sources: 4 (S006, S017, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

**24. Swing for big wins and accept flops; small safe lifts are not the goal.**
- "I would rather flop badly and succeed greatly than I would coming in with that little five percent boost." (S017)
- "Always shoot for the moon" (S016)
- Sources: 2 (S016, S017). Confidence: medium.

**25. Work in short timed blocks with no goal but staying with the copy.**
- "I don't think anyone can work for a very long period of time without interruption." (S017)
- "I work three hours a day, five days a week." (S018)
- Sources: 5 (S017, S018, S024, S035, S036). Confidence: high.

**26. Guideposts, not formulas: every product, market, and moment needs its own solution.**
- "What this first part of the book has tried to give you is not molds, but compasses." (S006)
- "I don't think I have to tell you that this mechanical approach is not my idea, and that it won't work." (S015)
- Sources: 2 (S006, S015). Confidence: medium.
