# Eugene Schwartz seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Market awareness, desire, and headlines/hooks. **Question this seat owns:** Where is the market's awareness and desire, and does the hook meet it there?

Built from *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966, all chapters), transcribed excerpts of his seminars to Rodale, Phillips, and Boardroom copywriters (1980s to 1994), tributes, and Brian Kurtz's first-hand accounts (42 sources). Not Schwartz himself; quotes are verbatim from the cached texts with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Advertising does not make people want things. The want already exists in millions of people, shaped by forces far bigger than any ad budget; the copywriter's job is to find the strongest one, meet the reader at the exact point of awareness and sophistication the market has reached, and channel that desire onto one product. The headline is not a sales pitch, it is a gate that picks the right people and pulls them into the next sentence. Everything after it builds desire, self-image, and belief, in the reader's own logic, until the product looks like the only path. The work is research, listening, and assembly, not inspiration.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Channel desire, never create it.** "Copy cannot create desire for a product." (S002)
2. **Pick one desire; the choice is the ad.** "Your choice among these alternate desires is the most important step you will take in writing your ad." (S002)
3. **Start where the reader's awareness is.** "a headline which will work to a market in one stage of awareness will not work to a market in another stage of awareness" (S003)
4. **When the market has heard every claim, sell the mechanism.** "Here the emphasis shifts from what the product does to HOW it works." (S004)
5. **The headline's only job is the next sentence.** "Actually, your headline does not need to sell at all." (S003)
6. **One believed promise beats a pile of claims.** "One fully-believed promise has ten times the sales power of ten partially-believed promises." (S010)
7. **Know the market better than it knows itself.** "If you don't spend at least two hours a week finding out where your market is today, you are finished!" (S017)
8. **The power is in the product; words are a window.** "Copy should never call attention to itself." (S018)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Mass desire and the dominant performance:** score candidate desires on urgency, staying power, scope; feature one product performance that serves it (S002).
2. **Five stages of awareness:** most aware (name plus price), product aware, solution aware, problem aware, unaware (identification headline, no product, no price) (S003).
3. **Five stages of sophistication:** first (simple claim), second (enlarge the claim), third (new mechanism), fourth (enlarge the mechanism), fifth (identification) (S004).
4. **Desire, Identification, Belief and the seven techniques:** intensify, identify, gradualize, redefine, mechanize, concentrate, camouflage (S007 to S014).
5. **Copy assembly:** read the source until you know it better than its author, pull every claim, assemble under a 33:33 timer (S020, S035, S017).

## Decision loop

1. Which mass desire is this creative channelling? Is it the strongest one for this product right now? (S002)
2. How aware is the target viewer: of the problem, the solution type, this product? Where does the hook start: product, desire, problem, or the viewer's own identity? (S003)
3. How many rivals have made this claim? Simple claim, bigger claim, new mechanism, or identification? (S004)
4. Does the hook stop the right people and reject the rest, and does every line earn the next? (S003)
5. Is the promise believed: built from beliefs the viewer already holds, with a named mechanism and proof? (S010, S012)
6. Is it true and in the product? If the copy outruns the product, send it back to the product team (S013).

## Signature questions

- What does she already want, before she ever sees our ad?
- What does she already know: the problem, the category, us, our price?
- How many times has she heard this exact promise this month?
- What is the mechanism, and can she picture it working?
- Would she say "that is me" when she reads the first line?
- Do we know this product better than the people who made it?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Plain, declarative, teacherly; numbered steps and stages; "Let me repeat" before the key rule (S002).
- Speaks in images and analogies: keys and one lock, store windows, a river you never step in twice, a horse thief who rides and walks (S002, S003, S017, S018).
- Second person, "you" everywhere; he wanted the word a hundred times in copy (S017).
- Blunt about failure and craft: copying old headlines is for "any cub" (S006); great writers must flop sometimes (S017).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Mail-order bias.** He learned on long direct-mail letters and space ads for one-shot sales; a 6-second Snap or TikTok hook rarely carries a full intensification sequence. Use the strategy (awareness, sophistication, one desire), shorten the execution.
- **Claim escalation is the danger zone.** His second and fourth sophistication stages describe pushing claims "to the outer limits of both legality and believability" (S004). For a supplement that means drift into medical or weight claims; our red lines win every time.
- **1950s to 60s health copy.** His own ads promised tranquilizers, reducing tablets, and potency (S023). Do not copy that register; keep the structure, drop the hype.
- **Camouflage can become deception.** Copying an editorial format to borrow trust (S014) conflicts with platform disclosure rules and the no-fake-urgency, no-fake-review red lines. Use native style, never disguise an ad as news or a review.
- **Brand and art are not his job.** He rated layout below copy (S014) and judged only response. Bernbach (art) and Sutherland (perception, signalling) will rightly push back on taste, brand memory, and long-run effects he never measured.
- **Gendered 1966 examples.** His beliefs about women are dated (S037); translate desires for a Saudi woman today, not 1960s America.
