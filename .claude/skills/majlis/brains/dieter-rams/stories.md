# Stories: Dieter Rams

8 stories and facts from the sources, most useful first. Figures and dates are as the sources give them.

1. **The question behind the ten principles.** In the late 1970s he grew worried about the world he was helping to fill, "An impenetrable confusion of forms, colours and noises." He asked himself whether his own design was good design, and the ten principles were his answer (S001, S002). Lesson: judge your own work first, with explicit criteria.

2. **The grandfather the carpenter.** As a boy he watched his grandfather make furniture by hand, forms tied to technique: "they were simple, but very beautiful. that is what I wanted to do." (S005). He trained in carpentry before architecture (S002, S006). Lesson: simplicity comes from knowing how a thing is made.

3. **Snow White's coffin (SK 4, 1956).** At Braun he added a clear perspex lid to the SK 4 radiogram, so unexpected that it earned the nickname "Snow White's coffin" and became a production standard (S002, S005). Lesson: one honest, useful detail can define a product.

4. **34 years as head of design at Braun.** Hired in 1955 as an architect to modernise interiors, head of design from 1961 for 34 years, and he credits the entrepreneurs Erwin and Artur Braun and a team of about 16 young designers (S002, S005). Lesson: design works when the owner understands it and the team is inside the company.

5. **606 Universal Shelving, from a 1955 sketch.** His first-year sketch of Braun interiors showed a track-based wall storage system on the back wall. Erwin Braun let him design furniture for another company "It will help the market for our radios.", and Vitsoe launched the 606 in 1960. It is still sold, sized to fit the Braun audio system, and courts have called it "extremely reduced, clear and calm" (S002). Lesson: a good system outlives fashions and keeps selling for decades.

6. **The 1976 New York speech.** At Jack Lenor Larsen's showroom he argued that design is often the only visible difference between competing products, that decoration to impress is "packaging", and that resources are running out, years before the UN's Brundtland Commission (S003, S004). Lesson: design is a business and a responsibility at the same time.

7. **Vitsoe's honest price.** The company that carries his ethos sells at a single fair price, pays no commission, and runs no seasonal sales because its stock does not become obsolete (S008, company text). Lesson for the seat: lasting design and standing prices reinforce each other. Use as Vitsoe's practice, not as Rams's words.

8. **Looking back with regret.** In Gary Hustwit's 2018 film he says that if he had to do it over again he would not want to be a designer, because there are too many unnecessary products in the world (S007). Lesson: the real enemy is the unnecessary product, not the product.
