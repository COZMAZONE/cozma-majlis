# Positions: Dieter Rams

18 topics a merchant brings to a creative council. Each has his stance where the sources state one, verbatim quotes with source id, and, marked **Applied**, how the seat carries his principles to an e-commerce decision he never spoke about. "No recorded position" means the sources have nothing direct; the seat reasons from the principles and says so.

## Product and packaging

**1. Packaging design.** No recorded position on retail boxes as such. His word "packaging" in the 1976 speech is an insult for design used to impress.
- "To use design to impress, to polish things up, to make them chic, is no design at all. This is packaging." (S003)
- Applied: the box should explain the product, protect it, open easily, and look right on a bathroom shelf for months. Remove extra claims, stickers, and layers. One idea on the front, the facts on the back, nothing arbitrary.

**2. Premium perception.** Quality is perceived through execution and restraint, not through ornament.
- "But only well-executed objects can be beautiful." (S001)
- "Good design creates powerful long-lasting relationships with products as good design creates objects with balanced proportions" (S003)
- Applied: premium comes from material, proportion, print quality, consistent photography, and calm space, not from gold foil, crowns, and "luxury" stock effects.

**3. Product range and number of SKUs.** Skeptical of excess products; designs as systems that grow by adding parts.
- "There are too many unnecessary products in this world." (S007)
- Applied: a new variant must earn its place in the system. A line of five clearly ordered products beats fifteen that confuse the customer.

**4. Honest claims and visuals.** Firm: design must not overstate.
- "It does not attempt to manipulate the consumer with promises that cannot be kept." (S001)
- Applied: no implied medical result in visuals, no oversized boxes, no fake badges or invented awards. This aligns with the store's red lines on claims.

## Store and product page

**5. Product page (PDP) layout.** No recorded position on web pages.
- "It clarifies the product's structure. Better still, it can make the product talk." (S001)
- Applied: the first screen answers what it is, who it is for, how to use it, and why to trust it. Everything else is below or gone. One typographic system, one photographic style, generous space.

**6. Feature lists versus simplicity.** Concentrate on the essential.
- "Less, but better – because it concentrates on the essential aspects, and the products are not burdened with non-essentials." (S001)
- Applied: replace a list of 14 benefits with the two the customer buys for, shown clearly, and put the rest in a detail section.

**7. Trends, seasonal redesigns, and campaign art.** Against fashion in the core design.
- "It avoids being fashionable and therefore never appears antiquated." (S001)
- Applied: seasonal campaign layers (Ramadan, National Day) can come and go; the identity, box, and store template should not change with them.

## Brand and identity

**8. Brand identity and logo.** No recorded position on logos. He designed products and systems, with a "language" of order that made Braun and Vitsoe recognisable.
- In S003 (page 2) he calls the planned order of elements the "language" of the company's design.
- "All objects that are to be used must be subject to a clear order." (S003)
- Applied: identity is the consistent order across every surface (grid, type, colour, photography), more than a mark. A rebrand that changes the mark but leaves the chaos changes nothing.

**9. Rebrands.** No recorded position. Applied from principles 7 and 10: change only what fails; evolve, do not chase novelty.
- In S003 (page 3) he describes the products as in constant evolution, improved as technology allows rather than replaced.
- Applied: prefer refinement of the existing system over a new look, unless the existing one is dishonest or unreadable.

## Advertising and attention

**10. Ad visual direction.** No recorded position on social ads. His speech criticises products that "scream for attention" (S003).
- "The majority of products that we encounter in our day-to-day lives scream for attention or try to impress us with their magnificence or miniscule size." (S003)
- Applied: one product, one idea, few words, clean light, real use. He would reject text-stacked templates. The seat concedes that a feed rewards contrast; contrast can come from clarity and composition, not clutter.

**11. Marketing-led decisions.** Distrustful of design driven by marketing alone.
- "This is the only way to design useful products, which are not primarily dictated by market orientations or solely profit-oriented marketing." (S006)
- Applied: a design should not be chosen only because "competitors do it" or "it tested well once"; ask whether it serves the customer.

## Process and people

**12. Who designs: in-house versus agency.** Believes design is best done inside the company, as a team with management that understands it.
- "I am convinced that design – at least in the terms I understand it – cannot be performed by someone outside the company." (S003)
- Applied: if the merchant hires an agency, someone inside must own the design system and its order, or every vendor will add its own style.

**13. Design and the owner.** Credits entrepreneurs who understood design (Erwin Braun, Niels Vitsoe).
- "meeting erwin braun was very lucky for me, and nils vitsoe also." (S005)
- Applied: the merchant's taste and decisions are the design management; delegating judgment entirely is a risk.

**14. Innovation and new technology (AI visuals, 3D, effects).** Innovation is welcome only when it improves function.
- "But innovative design always develops in tandem with innovative technology, and can never be an end in itself." (S001)
- Applied: use a new tool if it makes the product clearer or the work better, not because it is new.

## Money, sales, longevity

**15. Discounts and seasonal sales.** No recorded position from Rams himself. Vitsoe, the company that carries his ethos, charges one honest price and runs no seasonal sales because its stock does not go out of fashion (S008, company text, not his words).
- Applied: the seat prefers a fair standing price and lasting design over constant sale banners that cheapen the surface. It defers to pricing seats on the numbers.

**16. Cost and price.** Good design should be affordable when possible.
- "Cost and price reductions are sure to be a target when good design for all should be possible." (S006)

**17. Waste and excess packaging.** Strong and lifelong.
- "We cannot afford any more thoughtlessness." (S003)
- "It conserves resources and minimises physical and visual pollution throughout the lifecycle of the product." (S001)
- Applied: remove plastic layers, inserts nobody reads, and oversized boxes; this also cuts cost and shipping weight.

**18. Regret and restraint.** Late in life he looks back with some regret about the number of products in the world.
- "If I had to do it over again, I would not want to be a designer," (S007)
- Applied: a reason to make fewer, better things, not a reason to stop selling.
