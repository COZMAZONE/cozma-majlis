# Frameworks: Dieter Rams

10 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (judge a design, cut it down, keep it honest, make it last). "Sources" lists the texts that state or apply the framework. The first framework is his own; the others are working methods drawn from his speech and interviews and named here for use on the council. Applications to a Saudi e-commerce store (packaging, product page, ad visuals, identity) are the seat's reasoning, not his words.

---

## Judge

### 1. The ten principles for good design (scorecard)
- **What it is:** His answer to the question "is my design good design?", written in the late 1970s after he saw "An impenetrable confusion of forms, colours and noises." (S001). Good design is: (1) innovative, (2) makes a product useful, (3) aesthetic, (4) makes a product understandable, (5) unobtrusive, (6) honest, (7) long-lasting, (8) thorough down to the last detail, (9) environmentally friendly, (10) as little design as possible.
- **When to use:** Any visual or product decision: a box, a product page, a label, an ad frame, a logo refresh, a landing page.
- **How to score:** Score each option 1 to 10 on the six principles most relevant to a merchant: useful, understandable, honest, long-lasting, thorough, as little design as possible. An option that fails "honest" fails overall, whatever its other scores.
- **Quote:** "Good design emphasises the usefulness of a product whilst disregarding anything that could possibly detract from it." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S006

### 2. Usefulness, including the psychological
- **What it is:** Use is the centre, but use is not only mechanical. A product must satisfy "certain criteria, not only functional, but also psychological and aesthetic" (S001). Form comes after function, balanced with how it feels to use.
- **When to use:** When someone argues "it is just decoration" or "it is just function". A beauty jar must be easy to open with wet hands and also feel good on a shelf.
- **How to score:** List the jobs the item does for the customer (protect, explain, open, dose, reassure, gift). Each element should serve at least one job.
- **Quote:** "yes, form has to come after function. I can't conceive of it in any other way." (S005)
- **Second quote:** "there are certainly psychological functions as well, it is a matter of balancing the aesthetic content with regard to use." (S005)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S005, S006

## Cut

### 3. Less, but better (the subtraction pass)
- **What it is:** His summary of principle 10 and of his whole design: concentrate on the essential and remove the non-essential, then make what remains better. In 2014, asked for the main characteristic of his design, he answered with this phrase (S006).
- **Parts:** (1) List every element on the surface (words, badges, colours, icons, stickers, photos). (2) For each, ask what the customer loses if it goes. (3) Remove what costs nothing to lose. (4) Spend the saved attention and money on the quality of what stays (material, print, photography, type).
- **When to use:** Crowded product pages, packaging with five claims, ads with more than one idea, feature lists, menus.
- **How to score:** Count elements before and after. An option that adds elements without removing any scores low unless each addition carries a real use.
- **Quote:** "When we concentrate on the essential elements in design, when we omit all superfluous elements, we find forms become: quiet, comfortable, understandable and, most importantly, long lasting." (S003)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S006

### 4. Design versus packaging (the impress test)
- **What it is:** A line between design that serves and styling that impresses. He calls styling-to-impress "packaging", meaning a wrapper with no use.
- **When to use:** Gold foil, fake-luxury textures, stock "premium" effects, glitter, heavy filters, loud badges, animated gimmicks on a product page.
- **How to score:** For each decorative element ask: does it help the customer use, understand, or trust the product? If it only says "look how fancy", it fails.
- **Quote:** "To use design to impress, to polish things up, to make them chic, is no design at all. This is packaging." (S003)
- **Second quote:** "A designer who wants to achieve good design must not regard himself as an artist who, according to taste and aesthetics, is merely dressing-up products with a last-minute garment." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S009

### 5. Unobtrusive (the tool, not the artwork)
- **What it is:** Products that do a job are tools; their design should be "neutral and restrained, to leave room for the user's self-expression" (S001). In his Vitsoe work, the furniture "does not shout" (S003).
- **When to use:** Choosing between a loud and a quiet direction for packaging, the store theme, and the brand system.
- **How to score:** Does the design leave room for the product and the customer, or does it compete with them for attention?
- **Quote:** "Products fulfilling a purpose are like tools. They are neither decorative objects nor works of art." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

## Explain

### 6. Make the product talk (understandable, self-explanatory)
- **What it is:** Principle 4: design clarifies the product's structure, and at best explains itself without a manual.
- **When to use:** Product page hierarchy, how-to-use visuals, dosage and size labels, packaging fronts, unboxing inserts.
- **How to score:** Show the surface to someone for five seconds. Can they say what it is, who it is for, and how to use it? Each "no" is a design failure, not a customer failure.
- **Quote:** "It clarifies the product's structure. Better still, it can make the product talk." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

### 7. Order and system
- **What it is:** Every element follows a planned order (arrangement, shape, size, colour), and that order is the brand's language across products. For design to be understood by everyone, he says, it should be as simple as possible (S003).
- **When to use:** A product range that looks like it came from different companies; a store where every banner has a different style; a new SKU joining a line.
- **How to score:** Put all surfaces side by side (box, PDP, ad, insert, social). Do they read as one family with one grid, one type system, one palette?
- **Quote:** "The order of the elements – their arrangement, their shape, their size and their colour – is based on a thoroughly-planned system." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S002

## Keep it honest

### 8. Honest design
- **What it is:** Principle 6: design does not make a product look more innovative, powerful, or valuable than it really is.
- **When to use:** Before-and-after visuals, implied medical results, fake "award" badges, size-inflating photography, oversized boxes for small products, claims on packaging.
- **How to score:** Any element that overstates is a fail. In this store's terms it also crosses the merchant's red lines (claims, fake numbers).
- **Quote:** "It does not make a product more innovative, powerful or valuable than it really is." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S003

### 9. Thorough down to the last detail
- **What it is:** Principle 8: nothing arbitrary. Care in the details is respect for the user.
- **When to use:** Final checks on print files, type spacing, image consistency, the inside of the box, the thank-you card, the order confirmation.
- **How to score:** List the details a customer touches after paying. Count those designed on purpose versus those left to the supplier's default.
- **Quote:** "Nothing must be arbitrary or left to chance. Care and accuracy in the design process show respect towards the user." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001

## Make it last

### 10. Long-lasting and low-waste
- **What it is:** Principles 7 and 9 together: avoid fashion so the design never looks dated, and minimise physical and visual pollution over the product's life. His 1976 speech calls for an end to "thoughtless design" (S003).
- **When to use:** Rebrands, seasonal redesigns, trend-driven packaging, excess packaging layers, inserts nobody reads.
- **How to score:** Ask whether the option will still look right in five years and whether any layer could be removed without loss. Seasonal campaign art is fine as a temporary layer; the core identity and box should not chase trends.
- **Quote:** "It avoids being fashionable and therefore never appears antiquated." (S001)
- **Second quote:** "The times of thoughtless design, which can only flourish in times of thoughtless production for thoughtless consumption, are over." (S003)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S004, S008
